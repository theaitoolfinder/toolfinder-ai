Write your first prompt

Type anything in the message bar and press Enter (or click the send button). Let's start with something practical. Try this exact prompt:

📋 Copy this prompt
Write me a short professional bio for LinkedIn. I am a [your job title] with [X years] experience in [your industry]. Keep it under 150 words and make it sound confident but not arrogant.
🔒 chat.openai.com

GPT
I'm a marketing manager with five years of experience helping SaaS companies grow through data-driven campaigns and clear messaging. I've led go-to-market launches, built content programs from scratch, and worked closely with product and sales teams to close the gap between strategy and execution.

I care about making marketing that actually moves numbers — not just looks good in a deck. Currently focused on product-led growth and community-driven acquisition.

Notice how specific instructions ("under 150 words", "confident but not arrogant") shape the output quality.

That's your first prompt done. The key thing you just did: you gave it a goal, a context, and a constraint. That 3-part structure is the secret to 80% of great prompts.

Step 4 of 8

The 4-part prompt formula

Most people write one-line prompts and get generic results. The difference between a beginner and an intermediate user is knowing this formula:

① Role
Tell ChatGPT who to be. "Act as a senior copywriter" or "You are a friendly fitness coach."
② Task
State exactly what you want. Use action verbs: write, analyse, summarise, compare, list.
③ Context
Give it background info: your audience, the situation, what it's for.
④ Format
Tell it the output format: bullet list, email, 3 paragraphs, table, code block.
You don't need all four every time — but the more you include, the better the result.

Here's that formula in a real prompt:

📋 4-Part formula example
Act as a senior email copywriter. [ROLE] Write a cold email to a potential client. [TASK] My SaaS tool helps HR managers save 5 hours a week on onboarding admin. The recipient is an HR director at a 200-person tech company. [CONTEXT] Format: Subject line + 3 short paragraphs + single CTA. Maximum 120 words total. [FORMAT]

Step 5 of 8

Bad prompts vs. great prompts

The fastest way to improve your results is to see the difference side by side. Here are the most common beginner mistakes — and how to fix them.

❌ Vague prompt
"Write me a blog post about AI."
✅ Specific prompt
"Write a 600-word blog post intro for a freelance designer. Topic: how AI image tools save time on client revisions. Tone: conversational, practical."
❌ No context
"Improve my email."
✅ With context
"Improve this email to sound more professional. The recipient is a potential B2B client I've never met. Here's the email: [paste email]"
❌ No format
"Give me ideas for social media."
✅ With format
"Give me 10 LinkedIn post ideas for a personal finance coach. Format: numbered list. Each idea = 1 sentence hook + 1 sentence description."
⚠️
The most common mistake: Treating ChatGPT like a search engine. It's not — it responds to instructions, not keywords. Write in full sentences like you're briefing a smart colleague.

Step 6 of 8

Iterate — don't start over

When the first response isn't quite right, most beginners start a new chat and rewrite everything. That's the wrong move. The right move is to iterate in the same thread — ChatGPT remembers everything you've said in the current conversation.

🔒 chat.openai.com
GPT
[First draft of the blog post intro...]

AI tools are changing how designers work. Here are three ways...
U
Good start. Make it punchier — cut the first sentence and start with a question instead. Also make it shorter, around 100 words.
GPT
What if your biggest client bottleneck — revision hell — was completely avoidable?

Most designers spend more time on amends than on the actual design. AI image tools are quietly changing that. Here's how three of them are saving freelancers 4+ hours per client project...
Instead of starting over, give a follow-up instruction. ChatGPT uses the full conversation as context.

Use follow-up commands like: "Make it shorter", "Use a more casual tone", "Give me 3 alternatives", or "Now turn this into bullet points".

Step 7 of 8

Set up Custom Instructions

Custom Instructions let you tell ChatGPT things about yourself once — and it applies them to every future conversation automatically. This is the single biggest time-saver for regular users.

To set it up: click your profile icon (bottom-left) → Customize ChatGPT.

🔒 chat.openai.com
Customize ChatGPT
What would you like ChatGPT to know about you?
I'm a freelance marketing consultant. My clients are B2B SaaS companies. I write mostly for LinkedIn and email. My audience is startup founders and heads of growth.
How would you like ChatGPT to respond?
Always be direct. No fluff. Use short sentences. Avoid corporate jargon. When writing, use an active voice. If you're unsure, ask a clarifying question before responding.
Save
Fill in both fields — your background in the first box, your preferred response style in the second.
💡
What to put in custom instructions: your job, your industry, who you write for, your preferred tone ("direct, no fluff"), things to always avoid ("no bullet points in casual writing"), and any standing context ("I use British English").

Step 8 of 8

Build your first prompt library

The best ChatGPT users don't write prompts from scratch every time. They have a personal prompt library — a document of their best prompts that they copy, paste, and tweak.

Start a Notion page (or even a Google Doc) with this structure:

📚 My Prompt Library — Template
✍️ Writing
LinkedIn post · Cold email · Blog intro · Product description
📊 Research
Competitor analysis · Summarise article · Generate questions
⚡ Quick Tasks
Fix grammar · Translate · Reformat · Generate variations

Every time you write a prompt that gets a great result, save it. After a month you'll have a collection that makes you 10x faster.
