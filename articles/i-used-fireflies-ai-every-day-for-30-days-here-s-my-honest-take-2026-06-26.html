I Used Fireflies.ai Every Day for 30 Days — Here's My Honest Take

June 26, 2026 8 min read By Papabenj · My AI Tools Finder

Six months ago, pulling key insights from a two-hour client meeting meant listening back through the entire recording, typing notes, and spending another 90 minutes organizing action items. Now it takes me about 8 minutes — and that's including time to review and edit. That's not hyperbole. That's what happened when I committed to using Fireflies.ai for every single meeting, interview, and planning session I had for 30 straight days.

I recorded 47 meetings total. Client calls, team standups, podcast interviews, strategy sessions with my editor, even a few uncomfortable conversations I knew I'd need to reference later. I wanted to see if this AI meeting assistant could actually change how I work — or if it was just another tool that sounds great in demos but falls apart in real use. Here's everything I learned.

What Fireflies.ai Actually Does (and Who Built It)

Fireflies is an AI meeting recorder and transcription platform that joins your video calls, records everything, transcribes it in real-time, and then automatically generates summaries, action items, and searchable notes. It integrates directly with Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, and most other meeting platforms. You can also upload audio files if you record interviews or calls separately.

The company was founded in 2016 by Krish Ramineni and Sam Udotong, initially as a voice assistant before pivoting to meeting intelligence. They're based in San Francisco and have raised over $19 million in funding. The platform has grown significantly — they claim over 300,000 organizations use it now, though I can't independently verify that number.

Current pricing breaks down like this: Free plan gets you 800 minutes of transcription storage and basic AI features. Pro is $10/month per user and includes unlimited transcription storage, advanced search, and conversation intelligence. Business is $19/month per user with video recording, team workspaces, and analytics. Enterprise has custom pricing with advanced security and admin controls. I tested on the Pro plan, which is where most solopreneurs and small teams will land.

The One Feature That Justifies the Entire Subscription

The killer feature isn't the transcription — Otter.ai and others do that competently. It's the AskFred AI search that lets you query across all your meetings like they're a knowledge base. I can type "what pricing did we discuss with the SaaS client in May?" and it pulls exact quotes from three different calls, time-stamped, with context.

Here's a concrete example: I had a client ask me to recall a specific feature request they mentioned "a few weeks ago." I had no memory of it. Instead of scrolling through five meetings worth of notes, I typed "feature request navigation redesign" into AskFred. It found the exact moment in a March 12th call, 23 minutes in, where they mentioned wanting to simplify the mobile navigation. Pulled the quote, showed me the surrounding context, and I had my answer in 15 seconds.

Before Fireflies, I would have either pretended to remember, spent 20 minutes hunting through Google Docs, or just asked them to repeat it and hoped they didn't notice I hadn't been paying attention. The AI search turned my meeting history into an instantly searchable second brain. That alone is worth $10/month to me.

Every Feature That Actually Matters (and What They Don't Tell You)

Automatic transcription accuracy: I'd estimate 92-95% accurate in quiet environments with clear speakers. It struggles with heavy accents, multiple people talking over each other, and technical jargon it hasn't seen before. I had to manually correct "Klaviyo" to stop appearing as "clove EO" in every marketing meeting. Once you correct something once, it learns, but the first few mentions are often wrong.

AI-generated summaries: These are genuinely useful but need review. Fireflies pulls out key topics, action items, questions asked, and important moments. The action items are about 80% accurate — it catches most tasks but occasionally marks a hypothetical discussion point as an actual to-do. I learned to scan the action items list and delete the 2-3 things that aren't real tasks. Takes 30 seconds.

Speaker identification: Works well in one-on-one calls and small meetings where people introduce themselves. Falls apart in larger group calls where people don't say their names. It'll label people as "Speaker 1, Speaker 2" and you have to manually rename them. I had a six-person team call where it correctly identified two people and gave up on the other four.

Conversation intelligence: This analyzes things like talk time ratios, longest monologues, questions asked versus answered, and sentiment. Honestly, I found this more interesting than useful. Seeing that I talked for 68% of a client call made me more self-aware about listening, but I'm not sure it changed my behavior consistently. Sales teams probably get more value here than I did.

Video recording: Only on Business plan and up. I stayed on Pro and used Zoom's built-in recording when I needed video. The transcription works identically whether you have video or just audio, so I didn't feel like I was missing much.

Soundbites and highlights: You can clip specific moments and share them as short audio snippets. I used this exactly twice in 30 days — once to grab a client testimonial quote and once to share a particularly good explanation my developer gave about API limitations. It works fine, but it's not a feature I reached for often.

Integration ecosystem: Connects with Slack, Notion, Asana, HubSpot, Salesforce, and about 40 other tools. I set up the Notion integration to automatically push meeting summaries to my client project pages. Worked flawlessly after initial setup. The Make.com integration also exists if you want to build custom automations — I routed action items to my task manager that way.

What Happened When I Actually Used It for Real Work

Test 1: Client discovery call (48 minutes): This was a new potential client, first conversation, lots of questions about their business model and pain points. Fireflies joined the Zoom, recorded everything, and I barely took notes — just jotted down two things I wanted to circle back to. The transcript was ready 3 minutes after the call ended. The AI summary accurately pulled out six key challenges they mentioned, four questions I asked that I needed to research, and three action items (send proposal, schedule technical demo, share case study). I spent 8 minutes reviewing and editing the summary, then copied it into my CRM. Previous process would have been 30-40 minutes of note review and write-up.

Test 2: Podcast interview recording (1 hour 22 minutes): I interviewed a marketing director about their AI content workflow. Instead of recording in Zoom, I uploaded the audio file afterward. Transcription took about 6 minutes to process. Accuracy was excellent for my questions (it knows my voice well by now) but struggled with the guest's Australian accent — probably 85% accurate there. I used the transcript to pull exact quotes for the article I was writing, which was infinitely faster than scrubbing through audio. The timestamp links let me jump to specific moments to re-listen for tone and context. Saved me at least 90 minutes of manual transcription work I would have outsourced to Rev or done myself.

Test 3: Internal team planning session (37 minutes): Four people discussing content calendar and campaign priorities. This is where Fireflies showed limitations. It correctly identified me and one other person who has a Fireflies account, but labeled the other two participants as "Speaker 3" and "Speaker 4." The action items were muddled — it listed eight tasks but three were actually questions we discussed, not things we agreed to do. I had to spend 5 minutes cleaning up the output. Still faster than taking notes myself, but not the magical hands-free experience the marketing promises.

Who Gets the Most Value From This Tool

If you're a consultant or agency owner doing 10+ client calls per week: Fireflies is basically mandatory. The ability to be fully present in conversations instead of frantically typing notes changes client relationships. You catch more nuance, you respond better in the moment, and you have perfect recall later when they say "but I told you we wanted it blue" and you can prove they said green.

If you're a content creator who does interviews or podcasts: The transcription alone pays for itself. I produce two podcast episodes monthly and write articles based on expert interviews. Having searchable, time-stamped transcripts of every conversation means I can find and quote sources in seconds. I also found myself repurposing meeting insights into content — a client's question became a LinkedIn post, a team discussion sparked an article idea.

If you're managing a remote team with regular standups or planning meetings: The automatic summaries and action item extraction keep everyone aligned without requiring someone to be the designated note-taker. I shared Fireflies summaries in Slack after team calls and got feedback that people appreciated having a quick reference instead of vague memory of what we decided.

If you're in sales and need to track customer conversations: The conversation intelligence features are built for you. Talk time ratios, question tracking, keyword monitoring — these are sales coaching tools. I'm not in sales so I didn't use them heavily, but I can see the value if you're managing a sales team or trying to improve your own call performance.

The Honest Problems Nobody Mentions in Reviews

The bot joining your calls is awkward with external people. I had three clients ask "what's that?" when "Fireflies Notetaker" popped into the Zoom. I started proactively mentioning "I use an AI assistant to take notes so I can focus on our conversation — let me know if you'd prefer I turn it off." Nobody ever asked me to turn it off, but the explanation was necessary to avoid weird pauses.

The mobile app is nearly useless. I tried reviewing a meeting summary on my phone and the interface is cramped and hard to navigate. This is a desktop tool that happens to have a mobile app, not something designed for mobile-first use. If you need to reference meetings on the go, you're better off opening the web app in your mobile browser.

There's no way to selectively transcribe parts of a meeting. It's all or nothing. I had a 90-minute call where only the first 30 minutes were relevant to work and the rest was casual conversation. Fireflies transcribed all 90 minutes, and I couldn't tell it to stop or ignore sections. You can delete meetings afterward, but you can't pause during.

The AI sometimes hallucinates action items that sound plausible but never happened. In one summary, it listed "Schedule follow-up call for next Thursday" as an action item. We had discussed maybe scheduling something, but never committed to a specific day. If I hadn't reviewed carefully, I might have sent a calendar invite based on something we didn't actually agree to. Trust but verify is essential.

Breaking Down the Pricing: What Each Tier Actually Gets You

The Free plan is genuinely functional for light users. 800 minutes of storage equals roughly 13 hours of meetings. If you only record your most important calls and delete old ones, you could stay free indefinitely. You get basic AI summaries and transcription, but not the advanced search or conversation analytics. Good for testing the platform or very occasional use.

Pro at $10/month is the sweet spot for individual users. Unlimited transcription storage means you never delete anything and build that searchable meeting knowledge base. You get the AskFred AI search, advanced filters, and unlimited Soundbites. This is what I used and what I'd recommend for most solopreneurs and freelancers who are meeting-heavy.

Business at $19/month adds video recording, team channels where multiple people can collaborate on meeting notes, and conversation analytics. If you're managing a team or need to share meeting insights across an organization, the extra $9 is worth it. For solo users, probably not necessary unless you really want the video recording feature.

Enterprise is custom pricing and adds security features like SSO, advanced admin controls, and dedicated support. This is for companies buying for 50+ users who need IT oversight and compliance features. Not relevant for most readers of this review.

Is it worth it? If you have more than three important meetings per week that you need to reference later, yes. The time savings on note review alone pays back the $10/month in the first week. If you're only in a couple meetings per month, the free plan might be enough, or you might not need this at all.

How It Compares to Otter.ai, Grain, and Built-In Tools

Otter.ai is the closest competitor. Slightly better transcription accuracy in my testing (maybe 2-3% better), cleaner mobile app, and a nicer interface for real-time note-taking during meetings. But the AI search and summary quality feel less sophisticated. Otter is better if you want to actively take notes during calls. Fireflies is better if you want passive recording and post-call analysis. Pricing is similar — Otter Pro is also $10/month.

Grain focuses more on video recording and clip creation, especially for sales and customer research teams. Better video tools than Fireflies, but weaker on transcription search and cross-meeting intelligence. Choose Grain if you need to create highlight reels or video montages from calls. Choose Fireflies if you care more about text search and written summaries.

Zoom's built-in AI Companion is included with paid Zoom accounts now and offers meeting summaries and transcription. It's adequate for basic needs but doesn't have cross-meeting search, the summary quality is noticeably worse, and it only works for Zoom (Fireflies works across platforms). If you exclusively use Zoom and only need summaries for immediate post-meeting review, the built-in tool might be enough. If you want a searchable archive or use multiple meeting platforms, Fireflies is significantly better.

I also tested running meetings through Fireflies and simultaneously using Claude or ChatGPT to summarize the transcripts afterward. That DIY approach gives you more control over summary format, but you lose the automatic action item detection, speaker identification, and searchable archive. It's more work for marginally better summaries. Not worth the effort unless you have very specific summary needs the AI doesn't meet.

My Honest Verdict After 30 Days of Daily Use

For consultants and client-facing professionals: 9/10. The time savings and client relationship benefits are substantial. The ability to be present instead of note-taking is a real advantage, and having searchable meeting history has saved me multiple times when clients reference past conversations.

For content creators and interviewers: 8/10. The transcription quality and search features are excellent for repurposing conversations into content. The timestamp links make it easy to find and quote sources. Loses a point for occasional transcription errors with accents and technical terms.

For team managers and coordinators: 7/10. Good for keeping distributed teams aligned and ensuring nothing falls through the cracks. The speaker identification issues in larger meetings are frustrating, and you'll spend time cleaning up action items, but it's still net positive for team communication.

For casual users with occasional meetings: 5/10. The free plan is fine, but you probably don't need this. Zoom's built-in tools or just taking notes manually is sufficient if you're only in a few important calls per month.

Buy it today if you're doing 5+ important calls per week and frequently need to reference past conversations. Wait or look elsewhere if you're mostly in internal meetings where everyone's taking their own notes anyway, or if you have serious privacy concerns about AI bots joining calls. The Pro plan at $10/month is the right tier for most individual users — start there, and only upgrade to Business if you're managing a team that needs shared access.

After 30 days, I'm keeping my subscription. Fireflies didn't revolutionize my entire workflow, but it reliably saves me 60-90 minutes per week on meeting follow-up and note review. That's worth $10/month, and the searchable meeting archive has already paid dividends when I needed to recall specific client conversations. Just go in knowing you'll need to review and edit the AI outputs — it's an assistant, not a replacement for your own judgment.
