The Real Reason Agencies and Ops Teams Waste hours of repetitive busywork every week (And the Make.com Fix for It)

July 30, 2026 7 min read By Papabenj · My AI Tools Finder

A client email lands at 9:47 AM asking for an updated campaign report—leads from Facebook Ads, conversions from the CRM, budget actuals from the finance sheet, all formatted in the deck template they like. You know exactly where every piece of data lives, and you know you'll spend the next 90 minutes copying, pasting, switching tabs, and reformatting the same information you pulled last week, in the same order, for the same report.

If you run an agency or manage operations for a team, you've lived this scene dozens of times. Client reporting. Lead handoffs between marketing and sales. Onboarding workflows that require the same customer data entered into Slack, Notion, your CRM, and your project tracker. Invoicing that pulls line items from three different sources. Every single one of these tasks follows the exact same logic every time—yet someone on your team still has to do it manually, because the apps don't talk to each other and nobody has time to figure out how to connect them.

The result? Your team burns 6 to 12 hours per week on repetitive data transfer work that creates zero value for clients, generates zero revenue, and leaves everyone drained by tasks a computer should be handling. That's half a day to a full day and a half of billable capacity—gone. And it's not just the hours. It's the context-switching cost, the error rate when someone rushes a copy-paste at 4:50 PM, and the quiet resentment that builds when your strategist realizes she spent more time updating spreadsheets than actually strategizing this week.

This isn't a staffing problem. It's not a discipline problem. It's an automation gap—and it's costing you more than you think.

Why the Obvious Fixes Don't Work

Most agencies try one of three approaches when this problem becomes unbearable. The first is hiring a junior ops person or VA to "own the busywork." This helps for about six weeks, until you realize you've just paid someone $3,000 a month to do work that still shouldn't require a human—and now you're also managing that person, writing SOPs, and dealing with turnover when they leave for something less mind-numbing.

The second approach is adopting an all-in-one platform that promises to replace your entire stack. In theory, everything lives in one place and nothing needs to be copied. In practice, these platforms are either too rigid for how your agency actually works, too expensive to justify for mid-sized teams, or missing the one critical integration your client workflow depends on. You end up back where you started, plus a twelve-month contract you regret.

The third attempt is trying to "just build a process" with Zapier or a similar tool. You or someone technical on the team spends three hours setting up a basic two-app connection, it works for a while, then breaks silently when one app updates its API. Nobody notices until a client asks why their leads stopped syncing two weeks ago. The tool wasn't the problem—it's that nobody on the team had the time or skill to maintain it properly, and the platform didn't give you the flexibility to handle anything beyond the simplest triggers.

What Changes Once This Is Solved

Imagine your project manager finishing client reporting in twelve minutes instead of two hours, because the data pulls itself from Facebook Ads, your CRM, and your time tracker, formats itself into the template, and lands in the shared folder automatically every Monday at 9 AM. She doesn't touch it unless the numbers look wrong. That's 90 minutes back, every single week, to do actual project work.

Imagine new client onboarding happening while your account lead is still on the kickoff call: the signed contract triggers a workflow that creates the Slack channel, adds the client to your project tracker with the right template, sends the welcome email sequence, assigns the internal team, and notifies your finance system to start invoicing—zero manual handoff, zero "did anyone remember to add them to the sheet" confusion.

Imagine your team spending exactly zero hours per week on lead handoff busywork, because every new qualified lead from your website form automatically gets added to the CRM with the right tags, creates a task for the assigned salesperson, gets logged in the weekly lead report, and triggers a Slack notification—all within 60 seconds of the form submission. No spreadsheet. No forgetting. No friction.

That's what it looks like when the repetitive work actually goes away. Your team stays in their zone of genius—strategy, creative, client relationships—and the robot handles the copy-paste layer underneath. The hours come back. The errors disappear. And your people stop feeling like expensive data-entry clerks.

The Fix

Make.com is the automation platform built specifically to solve this problem for agencies and ops teams who need more power and flexibility than Zapier offers, but don't want to hire a developer to maintain it. It's a visual automation builder that connects your apps—CRM, project management, spreadsheets, email, social platforms, finance tools, whatever you use—and moves data between them based on triggers and logic you define once, then let run forever.

What makes Make different from simpler tools is that it doesn't just pass one field from App A to App B. It gives you a full visual editor where you can build multi-step workflows with branching logic, data transformation, error handling, and conditional paths. You can pull data from five sources, reformat it, filter it, combine it, and push it into three destinations—all in one automated sequence. For agencies running complex client workflows that don't fit into a one-size-fits-all platform, this is the difference between "I guess we'll keep doing it manually" and "we automated the entire thing in an afternoon."

The platform uses a scenario-based model: you build a "scenario" (an automation workflow) by dragging modules onto a canvas and connecting them. Each module is an app action—get new rows from a Google Sheet, create a task in Asana, send a Slack message, update a HubSpot contact. You can see the entire workflow visually, test it step-by-step, and modify it without breaking anything. If you've ever tried to debug a broken Zapier chain and had no idea where it failed, you'll appreciate this immediately.

Make.com offers a free tier with 1,000 operations per month—enough to test real workflows and automate lighter processes. Paid plans start at $9/month for 10,000 operations, scaling up to $29/month for 40,000 operations and beyond. For most small to mid-sized agencies, the $9 to $29 range covers the repetitive workflows that eat up the most time, and the ROI is immediate: one saved report cycle pays for a month of the platform.

Exactly How to Use It to Solve This

Let's walk through a real example: automating your weekly client report so it pulls live data from Facebook Ads and your CRM, combines it, and drops a formatted summary into a Google Doc your client can access—no manual work required.

Step 1: Sign up and create your first scenario. Go to Make.com, create a free account, and click "Create a new scenario." You'll land on a blank canvas. This is where you'll visually build your automation.

Step 2: Add your trigger module. Click the plus icon and search for "Schedule." Select the Schedule module and set it to trigger every Monday at 9 AM. This is what kicks off your workflow automatically each week. Once configured, you'll see the Schedule module appear on your canvas as the starting point.

Step 3: Pull data from Facebook Ads. Add a new module, search for "Facebook Ads," and choose "Get Ad Insights." Connect your Facebook Ads account (Make walks you through OAuth authorization), then configure the module to pull the metrics you need—impressions, clicks, spend, conversions—for the past seven days. Map the date range dynamically so it always pulls last week's data. You'll see a preview of the data structure Make retrieves.

Step 4: Pull data from your CRM. Add another module, search for your CRM (HubSpot, Pipedrive, Salesforce—Make integrates with all the major ones), and choose "Search Records" or "List Deals." Configure it to pull new leads or closed deals from the past week, filtered by the client tag or pipeline you're reporting on. Again, you'll see the exact fields Make retrieves, so you know what data you're working with.

Step 5: Combine and format the data. Add a "Text Aggregator" module or use the built-in functions to combine your Facebook Ads metrics and CRM data into a readable summary. For example, you might create a text block that says: "Last week: 12,450 impressions, 340 clicks, $487 spend, 8 new leads, 2 closed deals." Make lets you reference fields from previous modules using a simple dropdown—no code required, but you have full control over formatting.

Step 6: Create or update the Google Doc. Add a "Google Docs" module, choose "Create a Document from Template" or "Update a Document," and connect your Google account. Paste in the formatted text from Step 5, add any static content (headers, context, client name), and tell Make where to save the file or which existing doc to update. You can even include the client's name dynamically if you're running this for multiple clients.

Step 7: Notify your team. Optionally, add a Slack module at the end to post a message in your internal client channel: "Weekly report for [Client Name] is ready: [link to doc]." This closes the loop so your account manager knows the report is live without checking manually.

Step 8: Test and activate. Click "Run once" to test the entire scenario step-by-step. Make will show you exactly what data moved through each module, where it succeeded, and where it failed (if anything breaks). Fix any mapping issues, then flip the scenario to "On." It'll now run every Monday at 9 AM, pulling fresh data and generating the report automatically.

Total setup time for someone with no prior Make experience: 45 to 90 minutes the first time. After that, you can clone the scenario and adapt it for other clients in under ten minutes.

"But What If…" — Answering the Real Objections

Objection 1: "This sounds complicated. I'm not technical." Make's visual builder is designed for ops people and project managers, not developers. If you can map fields in a spreadsheet or follow a recipe, you can build a basic Make scenario. The learning curve is real—it's not as plug-and-play as Zapier's simplest zaps—but it's also not coding. Most users report building their first working automation within an hour of starting. Make also has a template library where you can clone pre-built scenarios and just swap in your own apps and credentials.

Objection 2: "What if it breaks and we don't notice?" Make sends email notifications when a scenario fails, and you can configure Slack or SMS alerts for critical workflows. You can also set up error handling within the scenario itself—if the Facebook module fails, send a fallback message instead of stopping the whole workflow. And because the visual builder shows you exactly where each piece of data comes from and goes to, debugging is far easier than in tools where everything happens invisibly in the background.

Objection 3: "Will this actually work for our specific setup?" Make integrates with over 1,500 apps, including every major CRM, project management tool, marketing platform, spreadsheet app, and communication tool agencies use. If you're using a niche app that doesn't have a native integration, Make supports webhooks and HTTP modules, so you can connect to any API. The flexibility is the point: it's built for teams whose workflows don't fit into cookie-cutter automation templates.

Worth Knowing: Other Options

If your workflows are genuinely simple—one trigger, one action, no branching logic—Zapier remains the easiest entry point, though you'll hit its limits quickly once you need multi-step conditional workflows. For teams with dedicated developers who want full programmatic control, n8n is an open-source alternative to Make that you can self-host, though it requires more technical maintenance. For agencies already deep in the HubSpot or Salesforce ecosystem, the native workflow builders inside those platforms can handle internal automations, but they don't connect well to the external tools most agencies rely on daily. Make sits in the sweet spot: more powerful than Zapier, more accessible than code-based tools, and more flexible than platform-native automation.
