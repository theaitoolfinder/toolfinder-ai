AI Tools Digest — May 22, 2026: What's Trending This Week

May 22, 2026 4 min read By Papabenj · My AI Tools Finder

The AI pricing war went full speed this week. Three major platforms cut prices or added free tiers in the same 48-hour window. Here's the practical impact on what you're paying — and what you should stop paying for.

1. OpenAI's GPT-4o API Is Now 83% Cheaper Than GPT-4 Turbo — Same Model Quality

OpenAI this week officially confirmed GPT-4o as the default API model, with pricing at $5 per million output tokens — versus $30 for GPT-4 Turbo. The performance gap is negligible for most business tasks: GPT-4o matches GPT-4 Turbo on writing, summarization, and reasoning, while being meaningfully faster. If you're building anything with OpenAI's API: switch to gpt-4o now if you haven't already. The cost reduction is immediate and requires no code changes beyond updating the model parameter. For reference, $5 buys roughly 150,000 average-length email summaries.

2. Anthropic Launches Claude Teams: $30/Seat Gets You Shared Projects and 200K Context

Anthropic launched Claude for Teams ($30/user/month, minimum 5 seats), which adds organization-level Projects — a shared workspace where team members can build on each other's Claude conversations and documents. The 200K context window is the real differentiator: you can upload a 400-page contract, a full product spec, and two months of customer feedback and analyze all three simultaneously. No other team AI offering at this price point matches that context length. Best fit: content teams, ops teams running complex processes, or any group that currently passes long documents around via copy-paste to get AI analysis.

3. Canva's Magic Studio AI Features Are Now Permanently Free for All Users

After running Magic Studio as a Pro-only feature for 18 months, Canva announced this week it's making the core set — Magic Write (AI text), Magic Edit (AI image editing), Background Remover, and Magic Resize — permanently free for all accounts. The paid tier adds higher volume limits and video features. This effectively makes Canva the most accessible AI design tool on the market; you'd previously need a $12.99/month subscription to access these. What this means: if you're still paying for a separate AI image editor for basic tasks, it's worth re-evaluating. Canva's image editing isn't Photoshop-level, but it handles 80% of content creation needs.

4. Notion AI Cuts Its Standalone Price From $16 to $8/Month

Notion dropped the price of Notion AI for existing Plus and Business subscribers from $16/month to $8/month. The feature set didn't change: AI writing, auto-fill database properties, meeting note summaries, and Q&A over your entire workspace. The price cut is a direct response to Microsoft Copilot integration pressure — Notion needed to justify its cost when Microsoft is bundling similar features into products people already pay for. Worth knowing: at $8/month, Notion AI becomes one of the better deals in the productivity AI category, especially if you're already using Notion as your knowledge base. The Q&A feature alone — asking natural language questions across all your notes and docs — saves 20–30 minutes per day for most knowledge workers.

5. ElevenLabs Drops Its Starter Tier Price by 40%: Voice Cloning Now at $5/Month

ElevenLabs cut its Starter plan from $11 to $5/month, citing infrastructure efficiency improvements. You get 30,000 characters/month (roughly 30 minutes of audio), access to all standard voices, and voice cloning from a 1-minute audio sample. The change makes professional-quality AI voiceover accessible for solopreneurs who only occasionally need audio — a social media explainer, a podcast intro, a client presentation with narration. Limitation to know: the Starter tier still limits you to 3 custom cloned voices; if you're doing volume audio production, the Creator tier ($22/month) is still the one to evaluate.

Bottom Line

The AI pricing war is doing exactly what competition is supposed to do: making better tools cheaper. GPT-4o API at 83% off, Canva AI features free, Notion AI halved, ElevenLabs cut 40% — this is an unusually good week to audit your current subscriptions. The practical exercise: list every AI tool you're paying for, then check if a free or cheaper alternative now does the same job. You'll likely find $30–$50/month in redundant subscriptions by Friday.
