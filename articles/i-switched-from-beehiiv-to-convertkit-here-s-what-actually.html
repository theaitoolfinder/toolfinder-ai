I Switched from Beehiiv to ConvertKit — Here's What Actually Happened

June 18, 2026 8 min read By Papabenj · My AI Tools Finder

A newsletter consultant charges $4,500 to migrate your email list and set up automation sequences. With the right platform switch, you can do it yourself in three afternoons for $0 in migration fees. That's exactly what I discovered when I moved my 14,000-subscriber newsletter from Beehiiv to ConvertKit in March 2026—and the differences between these two platforms turned out to be far more nuanced than any comparison chart suggested.

I didn't make this switch lightly. Beehiiv had been my home for eighteen months, and I'd built a respectable audience writing about productivity systems for remote workers. But as my newsletter evolved from pure content into a business with digital products and a coaching waitlist, I kept bumping into Beehiiv's limitations in ways that cost me real money. After three months on ConvertKit, I've learned which marketing claims actually matter and which ones are just noise.

Here's what nobody tells you about switching email platforms: the migration itself is the easy part. It's the hundred small workflow changes afterward that either make you more efficient or quietly drain two hours from your week. This comparison covers both the obvious differences and the ones that only reveal themselves after you've sent fifty emails from each platform.

Quick Verdict: Which Platform Wins for Your Situation

  • Best for pure newsletter publishers: Beehiiv wins if your entire business model is ad revenue, referrals, and sponsorships. The built-in ad network and referral program features are genuinely superior.
  • Best for creators selling products or services: ConvertKit dominates when your newsletter feeds into courses, coaching, memberships, or digital products. The automation and segmentation depth matters here.
  • Best if you're budget-conscious under 1,000 subscribers: Beehiiv offers a more generous free tier with better features included, while ConvertKit's free plan caps at 1,000 subscribers with limited automation.

Why I Originally Chose Beehiiv (And What Actually Worked)

I picked Beehiiv in late 2024 because the platform felt purpose-built for what I wanted to do: publish a weekly newsletter that could eventually monetize through sponsorships. The interface looked cleaner than ConvertKit's, the editor was genuinely pleasant to use, and the pricing was straightforward—$49/month for the Scale plan once I passed 2,500 subscribers.

Beehiiv's built-in referral program actually worked. I configured a simple rewards ladder where subscribers who referred three friends got a PDF guide, and ten referrals earned a 30-minute consultation call. Over eighteen months, this drove approximately 1,800 new subscribers without me spending a dollar on ads. The tracking was automatic, the reward fulfillment reminders came built-in, and subscribers received their referral links in the welcome email. Setting up something equivalent in ConvertKit would require integrating with a third-party referral tool like SparkLoop, which adds another $50/month minimum.

The analytics dashboard gave me everything I needed at a glance. I could see open rates by segment, click patterns across different link placements, and subscriber growth trends without building custom reports. For a creator who just wants to write and send, this simplicity had real value. The 3D boost feature, which lets you cross-promote with other newsletters on the platform, brought me about 200 subscribers over the course of a year—modest but genuinely free growth.

Where Beehiiv started showing cracks was in automation sophistication. I wanted to send different onboarding sequences based on which lead magnet someone downloaded. Beehiiv technically supports this through segments and triggered emails, but the conditional logic felt bolted-on rather than native. Creating a sequence where behavior A triggers email series B, but behavior C skips straight to email 5 in series B, required workarounds that broke twice when Beehiiv updated their automation engine.

The final straw came when I launched a small digital product—a $47 Notion template bundle for remote team leaders. I needed to tag purchasers, remove them from sales sequences, add them to a customer-only newsletter, and trigger a five-email onboarding series specific to product users. Beehiiv could technically do this, but it required me to manually create segments, set up multiple automation rules, and constantly check that tags were applying correctly. I spent four hours troubleshooting why 12% of purchasers weren't receiving the product onboarding emails. The issue turned out to be a segment conflict that Beehiiv's interface never warned me about.

Three Months Deep in ConvertKit: What Changed

ConvertKit's interface looked dated compared to Beehiiv's modern design, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. The visual editor feels like it was built in 2019 and hasn't been meaningfully updated since. But within two weeks of migration, I realized I was spending 40% less time managing my email system because the automation architecture actually matches how creator businesses work.

The tagging and segmentation system is where ConvertKit justifies its existence. Instead of creating segments based on static criteria, you build subscriber journeys using tags and automations that stack. When someone downloads my meeting reduction checklist, they get tagged "lead-magnet-meetings," which triggers a seven-email sequence. If they click the product link in email four, they get an additional tag "product-interested-meetings" and enter a sales sequence. If they purchase, a webhook from my payment processor applies a "customer" tag, which automatically removes all sales tags and switches them to the customer broadcast segment. This all happens automatically, and I set it up once in 90 minutes.

I tested this exact workflow in Beehiiv before switching, and it required creating four separate automations, six segments, and manual checks every three days to ensure tags were applying correctly. ConvertKit's visual automation builder shows you the entire subscriber journey on one screen. You can see exactly which path each subscriber is on and why. When I had 23 people not receiving emails last week, I identified the problem in four minutes: a tag condition was set to "has any" instead of "has all." Beehiiv would have required me to export subscriber data and cross-reference it in a spreadsheet.

The Creator Network feature launched in ConvertKit during early 2026, and it's basically Beehiiv's referral program rebuilt with more flexibility. You can set custom rewards, create tiered incentive structures, and even offer affiliate commissions instead of fixed rewards. I migrated my referral program to ConvertKit's system in about an hour, and the tracking has been more reliable. However, Beehiiv's implementation still feels more polished and requires less configuration.

ConvertKit's pricing is higher for equivalent subscriber counts. I'm currently paying $66/month for 14,000 subscribers on the Creator plan, compared to $49/month I was paying Beehiiv for their Scale plan. That $17/month difference ($204/year) is real money. But I'm also saving approximately six hours per month on automation maintenance and troubleshooting, which I value at roughly $300/month of my consulting rate. The economics only make sense if you're actually using the advanced features.

What surprised me most was how much easier it became to coordinate my email platform with other tools. ConvertKit's native integrations with Make.com meant I could build a workflow where new customers in my Gumroad store automatically get added to ConvertKit with the right tags, then get sent a personalized welcome video created through ElevenLabs text-to-speech, and finally receive their product access link. This entire chain happens in under two minutes without me touching anything. Beehiiv's Zapier integration could theoretically do this, but I experienced random failures about twice per month that required manual intervention.

Head-to-Head: Where Each Platform Actually Wins

Email Editor and Design: Beehiiv wins clearly. The editor is more intuitive, the templates look more modern, and the customization options are easier to access. ConvertKit's editor works fine but feels utilitarian. If visual brand presentation in emails is critical to your business, Beehiiv's polish matters. ✓ Beehiiv

Automation Depth and Reliability: ConvertKit dominates. The visual automation builder, conditional logic, and tag-based subscriber journeys are simply more powerful. I can build automation sequences in ConvertKit that would require multiple separate workflows and constant monitoring in Beehiiv. ✓ ConvertKit

Built-in Monetization Features: Beehiiv offers a clear advantage with their ad network, boosts, and premium subscriptions feature. If your business model is "grow audience, sell sponsorships, add premium tier," Beehiiv builds this into the platform. ConvertKit requires you to integrate external tools for most of this. ✓ Beehiiv

Product Sales and Customer Journey Management: ConvertKit wins decisively. The platform was designed for creators who sell courses, memberships, and coaching. Tagging purchasers, managing customer segments, and creating differentiated experiences for buyers versus subscribers is core functionality rather than an afterthought. ✓ ConvertKit

Analytics and Reporting: Beehiiv provides better out-of-the-box analytics with more visual dashboards and easier trend analysis. ConvertKit's reporting is comprehensive but requires more manual report building. For data-driven creators who don't want to export to Google Sheets, Beehiiv saves time. ✓ Beehiiv

Deliverability and Infrastructure: Both platforms have strong deliverability, but ConvertKit's longer track record and larger sender volume gives them a slight edge. My open rates increased by about 3 percentage points after migration, though this could be influenced by dozens of other factors. ✓ Slight edge to ConvertKit

Price-to-Value Ratio Under 5,000 Subscribers: Beehiiv offers better pricing for pure newsletter publishers at smaller sizes. Their free tier is more generous, and the jump to paid plans is more affordable. ConvertKit's pricing makes more sense once you're selling products that benefit from the superior automation. ✓ Beehiiv for newsletter-only, ✓ ConvertKit for product sellers

Real-World Scenarios: Which Platform Fits Your Actual Workflow

Scenario One: The Sponsorship-Focused Newsletter Publisher

You write a three-times-weekly newsletter about sustainable fashion with 8,000 subscribers. Your business model is sponsorships from eco-friendly brands, and you've recently added a premium tier at $8/month with bonus content and early access. You don't sell courses or digital products—your newsletter is the product.

Stick with Beehiiv. The ad network feature lets brands discover and pitch you directly through the platform. The premium subscriptions tool handles paid tiers natively without integrating Stripe webhooks and managing payment tags. The analytics dashboard shows sponsors exactly what they're getting with clear engagement metrics. You're not building complex automation funnels, so ConvertKit's advantages don't matter to your workflow. The $20/month you save on Beehiiv's pricing is actually significant when your revenue is unpredictable sponsorship deals.

Scenario Two: The Course Creator With a Newsletter

You teach project management frameworks and have a 12,000-subscriber newsletter. You offer three digital courses ranging from $149 to $497, plus occasional cohort-based workshops at $1,200. Your newsletter exists primarily to build trust and drive course enrollments. You send weekly content emails, but your real revenue comes from converting subscribers into students.

ConvertKit is the obvious choice here. You need to track who bought which course, ensure customers don't keep receiving sales emails for products they already own, and send targeted pitches based on behavior and purchase history. When someone buys your beginner course, you want them to enter a customer journey that eventually pitches your intermediate course—but only after they've had time to consume the first one. ConvertKit's automation handles this elegantly with visual sequences and tag-based logic. Beehiiv would require you to manually manage segments and constantly check for edge cases where automation breaks.

Scenario Three: The Hybrid Publisher-Entrepreneur

You run a newsletter about productivity for startup founders with 6,000 subscribers. You monetize through a mix of occasional sponsors, a small $97 digital product, and a premium community at $29/month. Your needs fall somewhere between pure publishing and full product business.

This is genuinely the hardest call. If your revenue is 70%+ from sponsorships and premium subscriptions with products as a side income, Beehiiv's lower cost and better monetization features probably win. If your products generate 40%+ of revenue and you want to grow that percentage, ConvertKit's superior automation will pay for itself through better conversion management. I'd actually recommend starting with Beehiiv and planning a migration to ConvertKit if and when product revenue crosses 50% of your total income. The migration is annoying but not technically difficult.

What the Migration Actually Involved (The Part Other Comparisons Skip)

Everyone asks about the migration process, so here's what actually happened when I moved 14,000 subscribers from Beehiiv to ConvertKit. The entire process took me about seven hours spread across three days, and I encountered two problems that weren't mentioned in either platform's documentation.

ConvertKit provides a CSV import tool that worked flawlessly for basic subscriber data. I exported my full list from Beehiiv, which gave me email addresses, signup dates, and basic engagement metrics. ConvertKit imported all 14,000 in about four minutes. The issue was tags and segments—Beehiiv's organizational structure doesn't map cleanly to ConvertKit's tag system. I had to manually recreate my segmentation logic using ConvertKit's tag architecture, which meant reviewing how I'd organized subscribers in Beehiiv and translating it into a tag taxonomy that made sense in the new system.

The automation sequences required complete rebuilding. You can't export automation workflows from Beehiiv in any format that ConvertKit accepts, so I had to screenshot all my Beehiiv automations, then rebuild them from scratch in ConvertKit's visual builder. This was actually beneficial because it forced me to rethink and simplify several overcomplicated sequences I'd built incrementally in Beehiiv. What had been five separate automation workflows in Beehiiv became two well-structured sequences in ConvertKit.

The problem nobody warns you about: historical engagement data doesn't transfer. ConvertKit imported my subscribers, but it had no record of which emails they'd opened or clicked in Beehiiv. This meant my first few emails from ConvertKit went to my entire list regardless of past engagement levels. I solved this by creating a re-engagement campaign specifically acknowledging the platform switch and asking subscribers to click if they wanted to keep receiving emails. About 68% clicked through, which gave me a clean engaged segment to work with going forward.

DNS and domain authentication took about 30 minutes and was straightforward. Both platforms provide clear documentation for adding the necessary DNS records. I experienced zero deliverability issues during or after the migration.

The Honest Verdict: Which Platform Should You Choose

For most newsletter creators who are just starting or who plan to stay focused on content publishing with sponsorship monetization, Beehiiv is the better choice. It's less expensive, easier to learn, and includes the features you'll actually use without overwhelming you with automation complexity you don't need. The interface is genuinely more pleasant to work in, and if you're sending one or two broadcasts per week without complex subscriber journeys, the automation limitations won't affect you.

ConvertKit makes sense when your newsletter is part of a larger creator business that includes products, services, or complex customer journeys. If you're selling anything beyond simple premium subscriptions—courses, coaching, memberships, digital products, workshops—ConvertKit's automation architecture and integration ecosystem will save you hours every week and probably increase your conversion rates through better-managed subscriber experiences.

The break-even point in my experience is around $500/month in product revenue. Below that threshold, Beehiiv's lower cost and simpler feature set make more sense. Above it, ConvertKit's superior automation starts generating enough additional revenue through better conversion management to justify the higher price and learning curve.

If you're already on Beehiiv and it's working, don't switch just because ConvertKit has more features. Migration is disruptive even when it goes smooth
