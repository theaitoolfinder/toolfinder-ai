Fireflies.ai or Otter.ai? A Real User's Take After Testing Both

June 11, 2026 8 min read By Papabenj · My AI Tools Finder

After testing both tools across 40+ hours of meetings, interviews, and podcasts, here's what I found: Fireflies.ai wins for teams who need deep integrations and automated CRM workflows, while Otter.ai is the better choice for individuals and small teams who prioritize real-time collaboration and a cleaner interface. That's the headline, but the truth is more nuanced—and depending on your specific use case, picking the wrong one could mean wasting 15 minutes per meeting wrestling with transcripts instead of getting back to work.

I've been using AI transcription tools since 2023, back when they could barely tell the difference between "their" and "there." But in June 2026, both Fireflies.ai and Otter.ai have matured into genuinely useful products. The question isn't whether they work—it's which one fits your workflow without creating new problems. I spent three weeks running them side-by-side in client calls, podcast recordings, and internal team meetings. Here's what actually matters when you're choosing between them.

Quick Verdict: Which Tool Is Right for You?

  • Best for sales teams and CRM-heavy workflows: Fireflies.ai — it pushes meeting insights directly into Salesforce, HubSpot, and Slack channels without manual export steps
  • Best for collaborative note-taking and education: Otter.ai — multiple people can edit transcripts in real-time during the meeting, making it ideal for workshops and lectures
  • Best if you're budget-conscious: Otter.ai's free tier gives you 300 monthly minutes (versus Fireflies' 800 minutes, but with more restricted features), and its $16.99/month Pro plan undercuts Fireflies' $18/month Business tier while including live transcription

Fireflies.ai: Built for Teams Who Live Inside Their CRM

Fireflies.ai feels like it was designed by someone who got tired of manually updating Salesforce after every sales call. Its core strength isn't just transcription—it's what happens after the transcript is generated. The tool automatically extracts action items, questions asked, prices mentioned, and next steps, then routes them to the right places in your tech stack.

Here's a specific example: Last Tuesday, I recorded a 45-minute discovery call with a potential client. Fireflies automatically identified six action items, pulled out three specific budget numbers the client mentioned, and tagged the moments when they expressed objections. Within two minutes of the call ending, those insights were sitting in our HubSpot deal record with timestamps linking back to the exact audio. I didn't touch an export button.

The AI-powered search is legitimately impressive. You can ask questions like "What concerns did the client raise about implementation?" and Fireflies will pull direct quotes with timestamps. It's not perfect—sometimes it misattributes statements in group calls—but it works well enough that I've stopped taking manual notes during most meetings.

Weaknesses you should know about: The interface feels cluttered compared to Otter.ai. There are multiple tabs, sidebar menus, and feature sections that take time to learn. New users I've onboarded typically spend their first week confused about where to find specific recordings. The mobile app is functional but lags behind the desktop experience—editing transcripts on a phone is painful.

Fireflies also has a quirk where it sometimes joins meetings a minute or two late, especially on Google Meet. I've missed opening remarks more than once because the bot was still connecting. And while the free plan offers 800 minutes of storage, you're limited to searching only the most recent transcripts unless you upgrade.

Pricing breakdown: The Free plan gives you 800 minutes of storage and basic transcription. Pro is $10/month per user (billed annually) and adds unlimited transcription, advanced search, and video recording. Business tier at $18/month unlocks CRM integrations, custom vocabulary, and conversation analytics. Enterprise pricing is custom but starts around $39/month per seat based on what I've seen quoted.

Who should use Fireflies: Sales teams, account managers, consultants, and anyone who needs meeting insights flowing automatically into Salesforce, HubSpot, Notion, or Slack. If you're already using tools like Make.com to automate workflows, Fireflies has webhooks and API access that integrate beautifully.

Otter.ai: Where Real-Time Collaboration Actually Works

Otter.ai takes a different approach. Instead of focusing on post-meeting automation, it's built around the idea that the transcript itself is a collaborative document. Multiple people can highlight text, add comments, insert images, and assign action items while the meeting is still happening. It sounds like a small difference, but it changes how meetings feel.

I tested this in a workshop I ran for eight freelancers learning about AI tools for content creation. I shared the Otter.ai transcript link at the start, and participants could see the live transcription on their screens. When someone asked a question, others added clarifying comments directly in the transcript. By the end of the 90-minute session, we had a fully annotated document that captured not just what was said, but the group's shared understanding of it. That's something Fireflies can't replicate.

The accuracy is comparable to Fireflies—both hover around 90-95% for clear English audio with minimal background noise. Where Otter edges ahead is speaker identification. It's better at distinguishing between voices in group calls, especially when people have similar vocal tones. I've noticed fewer "Speaker 3" and "Speaker 4" labels that need manual correction.

Weaknesses worth considering: Otter's automation features are significantly weaker. You can export to Google Docs, Notion, or Salesforce, but it requires manual steps or Zapier workflows. There's no automatic extraction of budget numbers, competitor mentions, or sentiment analysis like Fireflies offers. If your goal is "record the call and forget about it," Otter makes you do more follow-up work.

The free tier's 300-minute monthly limit feels stingy if you're in meetings all day. I burned through it in less than two weeks during a busy sprint. And while the OtterPilot bot can auto-join scheduled calendar meetings, it's not as reliable across all platforms—I've had it fail to connect to Microsoft Teams calls twice.

Pricing breakdown: Free gives you 300 minutes per month and 30 minutes per conversation. Pro is $16.99/month (billed annually) for 1,200 monthly minutes, unlimited conversation length, and advanced search. Business at $30/month adds user management, priority support, and Salesforce integration. Enterprise is custom pricing for large teams needing SSO and admin controls.

Who should use Otter: Educators, workshop facilitators, podcast creators, and small teams who want a shared record of meetings everyone can annotate. If you're a solopreneur conducting interviews or client calls where collaboration isn't needed, Otter's interface is cleaner and less overwhelming than Fireflies.

Head-to-Head: Where Each Tool Actually Wins

Transcription accuracy: Tie. Both handle clear audio at 90-95% accuracy. Otter is slightly better with accents and multiple speakers. Fireflies struggles more with background noise but has improved significantly since 2024.

Real-time collaboration: Otter.ai wins decisively. Multiple users can edit and comment simultaneously. Fireflies added shared comments in late 2025, but it feels tacked on rather than core to the experience.

Automation and integrations: Fireflies.ai wins by a mile. Native CRM pushes, automatic extraction of insights, conversation intelligence analytics, and webhook support make it the obvious choice for sales and customer success teams. Otter requires third-party tools like Make.com or Zapier to achieve similar automation.

Learning curve: Otter.ai is simpler. You can onboard a new user in five minutes. Fireflies takes at least a week to understand where everything lives and how to set up filters and workflows properly.

Price-to-value ratio: Depends entirely on your use case. For individuals, Otter's $16.99/month Pro plan is the better deal. For teams already paying for Salesforce or HubSpot, Fireflies' $18/month Business tier pays for itself by eliminating manual CRM updates—I estimate it saves our team 20+ minutes per sales call.

Real-World Scenarios: Which Tool Should You Choose?

Scenario 1: You're a sales team running 30+ prospect calls per week. Go with Fireflies.ai. The automatic extraction of objections, pricing discussions, and competitor mentions alone justifies the cost. One of my clients in B2B SaaS sales calculated that Fireflies saved their five-person team approximately 3.5 hours per week in post-call admin work. That's real money.

Scenario 2: You're a consultant or coach recording 1-on-1 client sessions. Otter.ai is the smarter choice. You don't need deep CRM integration, and the cleaner interface makes it faster to review specific moments from past calls. The ability to share annotated transcripts directly with clients is valuable here—I send Otter links after coaching calls so clients have a record they can reference.

Scenario 3: You're recording podcast interviews or long-form content. Slight edge to Otter.ai. The collaborative editing features make it easier to work with a producer or editor who needs to pull quotes and create show notes. Fireflies works fine here too, but you're paying for automation features you won't use. Tools like OpusClip handle the clip creation side better anyway.

The Tool I'm Actually Paying For (And Why)

I'm currently subscribed to Fireflies.ai Business tier, but that's because 60% of my meetings are client calls that feed into our project management system and CRM. The time saved on manual data entry pays for itself within the first week of each month. If I were primarily an educator, content creator, or running internal team meetings, I'd switch to Otter.ai Pro in a heartbeat.

For most solopreneurs and freelancers reading this, start with Otter.ai. It's $2 cheaper per month at the Pro level, the interface won't overwhelm you, and the free tier gives you enough runway to test if AI transcription actually fits your workflow. You can always switch to Fireflies later if you realize you need deeper automation.

If you're on a sales team, in customer success, or managing accounts where meeting insights need to flow automatically into your CRM, Fireflies.ai is worth the extra complexity and cost. The ROI shows up in hours saved per week, and that compounds quickly.

One final note: Neither tool is a replacement for actually paying attention during meetings. I've noticed people lean too heavily on "the bot is recording" as an excuse to zone out. The transcripts are reference material, not substitutes for active listening. Use them to capture details you'd otherwise forget, not as permission to mentally check out.

Common Questions Before You Buy

Can I use both Fireflies and Otter in the same meeting? Technically yes, but it's overkill and participants will find two bots joining weird. Pick one. If you're testing both, alternate which one you use per meeting rather than running them simultaneously.

Do these tools work with video platforms besides Zoom? Both support Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, and Webex. Fireflies has slightly better reliability on Teams in my testing. Otter works with in-person recordings via mobile app, which Fireflies doesn't handle as well. Neither works with Discord or Slack huddles as of June 2026.

What happens to my recordings if I cancel my subscription? Both Fireflies and Otter let you export transcripts before you cancel, but you'll lose access to the searchable archive and AI-powered features. Download anything important as plain text or PDF before your subscription ends. Fireflies keeps recordings for 30 days post-cancellation; Otter gives you 60 days to export your data.

Here's what to do next: Sign up for the free tier of whichever tool matches your primary use case—Fireflies if you need CRM integration, Otter if you want collaboration. Run it for 10 meetings. If you're not finding yourself referring back to the transcripts at least twice a week, AI transcription might not be a fit for your workflow yet. But if you catch yourself thinking "I need to check what they said about that budget," you'll know it's working.
