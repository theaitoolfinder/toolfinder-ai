Video Creators and Musicians Are Quietly Switching to Soundraw to Stop not being able to use the music you want in your videos without risking a copyright strike

August 03, 2026 7 min read By Papabenj · My AI Tools Finder

Most people believe you need a huge music licensing budget or hours of hunting through royalty-free libraries to score your videos safely. They're wrong — and it's costing them countless hours, creative freedom, and sometimes their entire channel when a copyright strike lands without warning.

You've felt this exact frustration: you spend three hours editing a video, find the perfect track that matches your vision, drop it in, upload... and within 48 hours, you're staring at a copyright claim. Maybe it's just monetization blocked. Maybe it's a full strike. Either way, your work is now hostage to a system that feels designed to punish creators who just want their content to sound professional.

The alternative isn't much better. You open one of those "free music" sites and scroll through 400 generic electronic loops that all sound like elevator music from 2012. Nothing fits your vibe. Nothing has the energy you need. You compromise, settle for something "good enough," and your final video feels flat because the audio doesn't match what you envisioned. Your audience can tell — watch time drops in the first 15 seconds because the whole thing feels amateur.

This isn't just annoying. It's expensive. Video creators report spending an average of 6-8 hours per month just searching for usable music across multiple projects. Musicians creating content about their work face an even crueler irony: they understand music better than anyone, but they still can't use most of what inspires them without legal risk. That's nearly two full workdays every month spent not editing, not filming, not growing your audience — just trying to find background music you're legally allowed to use.

The stakes get higher when you're building a brand or running a business channel. One copyright strike can tank your monetization. Three strikes and your channel disappears entirely, taking your audience and income with it. Even claims that don't result in strikes often redirect your ad revenue to rights holders, meaning you did all the work and someone else gets paid. For creators earning $2,000-$5,000 monthly from YouTube, losing even two weeks of monetization while fighting a claim represents real money they can't afford to lose.

Why the Obvious Fixes Don't Work

Most video creators try one of three workarounds, and all of them fall short in different ways.

Some pay for subscription libraries like Epidemic Sound or Artlist. These work fine until you realize you're paying $180-$300 per year for access to music that thousands of other creators are also using. Your travel vlog ends up with the same ukulele track as 47 other channels. Your product demo has the identical corporate tech background as your competitor's launch video. You're legally covered, sure — but you sound exactly like everyone else.

Others try to license individual tracks directly, which quickly becomes a budget nightmare. A single commercial-use license for a recognizable song can cost $500-$3,000 depending on your audience size and platform. If you're publishing 2-4 videos per week, that approach is financially impossible unless you're already making six figures from your content.

Then there's the "I'll just use YouTube's Audio Library" crowd. It's free, it's safe, and it's been picked over by 50 million other creators. The selection is limited, the quality is inconsistent, and good luck finding anything that matches a specific mood or tempo you need. You end up either forcing your edit to match the music instead of the other way around, or you publish with no music at all and hope your voice and visuals carry the whole piece.

The financial shift matters too. Instead of spending $15-25 per month per project on individual licenses, or $200+ yearly on a library subscription that limits your uniqueness, you're paying a flat rate for unlimited original music. Exactly How to Use It to Solve This

Here's the actual workflow, start to finish, for getting original music for your next video in under 10 minutes.

Step 1: Define what you actually need (2 minutes)

Before you touch Soundraw, watch your rough video cut and identify the emotional arc. Don't think "I need background music" — think "the first 20 seconds need urgency, the middle section needs steady focus, and the outro needs resolution." Write down three words maximum: maybe "Urgent, Focused, Resolved" or "Playful, Energetic, Warm." Also note your video length so you know how long the track needs to run.

Step 2: Set your core parameters (1 minute)

Log into Soundraw and click "Create Music." You'll see dropdown menus for mood, genre, and theme. For a tech product review video, you might select Mood: "Busy," Genre: "Corporate," Theme: "Technology." Set your length to match your video (or slightly longer — you can always fade out early). Set tempo: 120-130 BPM works for most upbeat content; 80-100 BPM for thoughtful or dramatic pieces. Click "Create" and Soundraw generates 15 complete tracks in about 10 seconds based on your parameters.

Step 3: Preview and customize (3 minutes)

Click play on the first few tracks. You're not looking for perfection yet — you're looking for the right basic vibe. Found one that's 80% there? Click it to open the editor. This is where Soundraw separates itself from stock libraries: you see your track broken into sections (Intro, Verse, Chorus, Ending), and you can adjust each section's energy, instruments, and length independently. Your video has a talking head intro that needs subtler music? Drag the "Intro" energy slider down. Main segment needs more drive? Increase the "Verse" intensity. Swap out instruments you don't like — remove the piano, add a synth pad. Regenerate just that section if it's still not working.

Step 4: Download and drop into your edit (1 minute)

Once you're happy with the arrangement, click "Download." You get a high-quality WAV or MP3 (your choice) in seconds. Import it into your editing software exactly like any other audio file. It's already structured with a clear intro and outro, so minimal additional editing is needed. Adjust levels, add a fade if you want, and you're done.

Step 5: Save the project for future use (1 minute)

Soundraw lets you save customized tracks in your library with tags. If you just created the perfect "tech review background" track, save it as such. Next time you're editing a similar video, you can pull the same track or duplicate and modify it for variation. Over time, you build a personal library of original music that defines your channel's sound.

Real example: A YouTuber creating a 5-minute camera gear review needs background music that sounds professional but doesn't overwhelm narration. In Soundraw, she selects Mood: "Smooth," Genre: "Ambient," sets 5:30 length and 95 BPM. Generates 15 options, previews three, picks one, then uses the editor to reduce the intro intensity (she's talking over it) and adds a gentle piano to the outro. Total time from starting Soundraw to having the finished track in Premiere Pro: 6 minutes. The resulting video sounds polished and distinct, with zero risk of copyright issues when she monetizes it.

"But What If…" — Answering the Real Objections

Objection 1: "Will AI-generated music sound generic or robotic?"

Fair question, and the honest answer is: some tracks will, especially if you're not customizing them. The raw generated output from Soundraw is professional-quality but can feel a bit "stock" if you just hit generate once and download. The difference comes in Step 3 — spending 3 minutes adjusting sections, swapping instruments, and tweaking energy levels. When you do that customization, the output becomes distinct enough that audiences don't register it as "AI music" — they just hear competent background scoring. It won't win Grammys, but it absolutely holds up against typical YouTube background music and surpasses most free stock libraries in both quality and uniqueness.

Objection 2: "I need really specific genres — what if Soundraw can't do my style?"

Soundraw handles about 12 primary genres (Corporate, Ambient, Cinematic, Hip Hop, Trap, Electronica, etc.) and dozens of mood variations within each. It's strong with modern electronic, ambient, corporate, and cinematic styles. It's weaker with complex acoustic genres like jazz, classical, or heavy metal. If your content absolutely requires authentic bluegrass or orchestral film scoring, Soundraw probably isn't your primary solution — you'll still need specialist libraries for those. But for the 80% of YouTube, TikTok, and Instagram content that needs modern background music (vlogs, tutorials, reviews, business content, fitness videos, tech demos), Soundraw covers the range well.

Objection 3: "Is $20/month worth it if I only post once a week?"

Break it down by time value. If you're currently spending 90 minutes per video searching for safe music, Soundraw saves you roughly 6 hours monthly (90 minutes × 4 videos). What's your time worth? Even at a conservative $25/hour, that's $150 in monthly time savings against a $20 cost — a 7.5x return. Plus, you eliminate copyright claim risk, which is impossible to price until it happens to you and tanks a week of revenue. If you're only posting twice monthly and currently using free libraries with zero copyright problems, you might skip this until your publishing volume increases. But most weekly creators find the math works clearly in Soundraw's favor within the first month.
