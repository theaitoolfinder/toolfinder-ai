The Exact AI Workflow That Turns One Blog Post Into 30 Pieces of Content

October 17, 2026 8 min read By Papabenj · My AI Tools Finder

One blog post can become 30 pieces of content in under four hours—and the entire process runs on five AI tools that cost less than $150 per month combined. I've watched solopreneurs and small marketing teams turn a single 1,500-word article into a full month of social posts, email newsletters, video scripts, and podcast snippets, publishing daily without writing a single new sentence from scratch. This isn't theory: it's a replicable workflow that turns one core idea into a distribution engine.

The math is simple. You spend three hours writing one strong blog post. Then you spend another two to three hours running it through this exact AI workflow. The result: 10 social media posts, 5 email newsletter segments, 3 video scripts, 6 quote graphics, 4 LinkedIn carousel drafts, and 2 podcast outlines—all derived from that original piece. The content stays on-message because it all stems from the same source. The distribution gets easier because you're not starting from blank pages every day.

This playbook walks through the exact stack, the step-by-step process, the real prompts, and the time investment for each phase. If you've ever published a blog post and then felt stuck trying to promote it, this is the system that changes that.

The Old Way vs. The New Way

Before this workflow existed, content repurposing meant manual labor. You'd write a blog post, then sit down separately to brainstorm social media captions. You'd open Canva and design quote graphics one by one. You'd rewrite sections in different tones for LinkedIn versus Twitter. If you wanted video content, you'd script it from memory or reread the entire post looking for pull quotes. The average creator spent 6–8 hours per week on this repurposing work, and most gave up after creating three or four derivative pieces because the effort didn't justify the return.

The new way treats your blog post as raw material. You feed it into a sequence of AI tools, each handling one transformation: text-to-social-posts, text-to-video-script, text-to-email-sequence, text-to-graphics. The tools don't write new ideas—they reformat, summarize, and reframe what you already said. You spend 2–3 hours total, most of it reviewing and tweaking outputs rather than generating from scratch. The result is 25–30 pieces of polished, on-brand content ready to schedule.

The time difference is stark: from 6–8 hours of manual work yielding 4–5 pieces, to 3 hours of AI-assisted work yielding 30 pieces. The cost difference matters too. Hiring a social media manager or content assistant to do this work runs $500–$1,500 per month. This workflow costs under $150 in tool subscriptions and runs on autopilot once you learn the sequence.

The Exact Stack

  • NeuronWriter – Your blog post optimization and content brief tool. Role: ensures the original blog post is SEO-optimized and structured for easy extraction. Cost: $23/month (Bronze plan). Key feature: exports clean HTML and structured outlines that feed cleanly into the next tools.
  • ChatGPT (Plus or Team) – Your content reformatter and prompt engine. Role: takes the blog post and generates social media posts, email drafts, and video scripts in batches. Cost: $20/month (Plus). Key feature: Custom GPTs let you save repurposing prompts and run them repeatedly without retyping instructions.
  • Make.com – Your automation backbone. Role: connects the blog post (via RSS or webhook) to the rest of the stack, triggering content generation automatically when you publish. Cost: $9/month (Core plan, 10,000 operations). Key feature: HTTP modules let you send your blog text directly to APIs or AI tools without manual copy-pasting.
  • Predis.ai – Your social media graphics and caption generator. Role: turns pull quotes and key points into branded visual posts for Instagram, LinkedIn, and Twitter. Cost: $32/month (Solo plan). Key feature: generates carousel posts and quote graphics in your brand colors with one click, no design skill required.
  • OpusClip – Your video script-to-short-clip tool. Role: if you record yourself reading sections of the blog post or talking through key points, OpusClip identifies the best 30–60 second segments and adds captions automatically. Cost: $29.50/month (Starter plan). Key feature: AI virality score tells you which clips are most likely to perform on social platforms.
  • Beehiiv – Your email newsletter platform with AI writing assist. Role: formats blog sections into email-friendly newsletters and suggests subject lines. Cost: Free (up to 2,500 subscribers), $49/month (Grow plan) for AI features. Key feature: AI Writing Assistant rewrites blog paragraphs into conversational email tone in seconds.

Total monthly cost: $113.50 (if using ChatGPT Plus and Beehiiv free tier) to $162.50 (if using all paid tiers). One-time learning curve: about 4 hours to set up automations and test prompts. After that, each blog post takes 2–3 hours to turn into 30 pieces.

The Step-by-Step Playbook

Step 1: Write and publish your blog post in NeuronWriter (30–90 minutes)

Start by drafting your blog post directly in NeuronWriter or pasting it in from Google Docs. Use NeuronWriter's content editor to ensure the post is well-structured with clear H2 and H3 headings, bullet points, and concise paragraphs. This structure matters because the AI tools in later steps will parse headings to extract key points. Aim for 1,200–1,800 words—long enough to contain multiple ideas, short enough to stay focused. Once the post scores 75+ on NeuronWriter's optimization scale, export the HTML or copy the full text. Time investment: 30–90 minutes depending on your writing speed.

Step 2: Feed the blog post into ChatGPT with your repurposing prompt library (15 minutes)

Open ChatGPT and paste your full blog post. Then run the first prompt from your saved Custom GPT (or paste it manually if you're not using Custom GPTs). Here's the exact prompt I use for social media posts:

"You are a content repurposing assistant. I'm giving you a blog post. Your job: extract 10 social media posts from this content. Each post should be 100–150 characters, include one key insight from the blog, and end with a question or call-to-action. Format as a numbered list. Do not add hashtags. Here's the blog post: [paste full text]"

ChatGPT will return 10 posts in under 30 seconds. Copy them into a Google Doc or Notion page labeled "Social Posts - [Blog Title]". Next, run the email sequence prompt:

"Take this blog post and break it into 5 email newsletter sections. Each section should be 80–120 words, written in a conversational tone, and focus on one key idea from the post. Include a subject line for each. Here's the post: [paste full text]"

Copy those outputs into a separate section of your Doc. Finally, run the video script prompt:

"Turn this blog post into 3 short video scripts (60–90 seconds each when spoken aloud). Each script should open with a hook, explain one core concept from the post, and end with a next step. Write in spoken, casual language. Here's the post: [paste full text]"

You now have 10 social posts, 5 email drafts, and 3 video scripts. Total time: 15 minutes including copy-pasting and organizing outputs.

Step 3: Generate quote graphics and carousels in Predis.ai (20 minutes)

Log into Predis.ai and navigate to the "Content Ideas" tab. Paste your blog post title or a 2–3 sentence summary of the post. Predis will suggest 8–12 content ideas, including quote graphics, carousel posts, and single-image posts. Select the 6 that best match your key points. For each, Predis auto-generates the visual in your brand colors (set these once in your brand kit). Review each graphic, tweak the text if needed (usually just tightening a word or two), and download. You'll end up with 6 branded graphics ready to post on Instagram, LinkedIn, or Twitter. Time: 20 minutes.

Step 4: Create LinkedIn carousels from blog sections (15 minutes)

Back in ChatGPT, paste one section of your blog post (usually 200–300 words) and run this prompt:

"Turn this section into a 6-slide LinkedIn carousel. Slide 1: a bold hook that makes someone want to swipe. Slides 2–5: one key point per slide, 15–25 words each. Slide 6: a call-to-action. Format as: Slide 1: [text], Slide 2: [text], etc. Here's the section: [paste text]"

ChatGPT will give you the text for each slide. Open Canva (or use Predis.ai's carousel template), drop the text onto each slide, and export as a PDF. Repeat this for 3–4 different sections of your blog post. You'll get 3–4 LinkedIn carousels from one post. Time: 15 minutes total.

Step 5: Record and clip short videos with OpusClip (45–60 minutes)

This step is optional but high-leverage. Open your video script from Step 2 and record yourself reading it aloud using your phone, Descript, or any screen recording tool. Don't worry about perfection—these are short, casual videos. Aim for 2–3 minutes per script (you'll have 3 scripts, so 6–9 minutes of total footage). Upload the raw video to OpusClip. The tool will analyze it, identify the best 30–90 second clips, add captions automatically, and score each clip for virality. Download the top 3–5 clips. You now have short-form video content for Instagram Reels, TikTok, YouTube Shorts, or LinkedIn video posts. Time: 45–60 minutes including recording and export.

Step 6: Format email newsletters in Beehiiv (20 minutes)

Log into Beehiiv and create a new post. Paste the first email section from Step 2 into the editor. Use Beehiiv's AI Writing Assistant to adjust tone if needed (I typically ask it to "make this feel warmer and more conversational"). Add a subject line—Beehiiv's AI can suggest 5 options based on your content. Repeat for the remaining 4 email sections. You can schedule these as a 5-part email series over the next two weeks, or combine two sections into longer emails. Either way, you've just created two weeks of newsletter content from one blog post. Time: 20 minutes.

Step 7: Automate the trigger with Make.com (one-time setup, 30 minutes; then automatic)

This step is optional for your first run-through, but it's what turns this from a manual workflow into a repeatable system. In Make.com, create a new scenario. Set the trigger to "RSS" or "Webhook" (depending on your blog platform). When a new blog post is published, Make.com will capture the post URL and full text. Add a second module: "HTTP Request" to ChatGPT's API (you'll need an API key, available with ChatGPT Plus or Team). Configure the request to send your repurposing prompts automatically and return the outputs. Add a third module: "Google Docs - Create a Document" to save all outputs in one organized Doc. Once this is live, publishing a blog post automatically generates your 10 social posts, 5 email drafts, and 3 video scripts without you opening ChatGPT. Time: 30 minutes to build the first time; zero minutes every subsequent run.

The Numbers

Here's the honest math. Writing the original blog post: 60–90 minutes. Running it through this workflow manually (Steps 2–6): 115–130 minutes. Total time investment per blog post: roughly 3 hours. Output: 10 social posts, 5 email drafts, 3 video scripts, 6 quote graphics, 4 LinkedIn carousels, and 3–5 short video clips. That's 31–33 pieces of content from one post.

If you automate Step 7 with Make.com, the time drops to about 2 hours because ChatGPT runs in the background. Monthly tool cost: $113.50–$162.50. Compare that to hiring a content repurposing freelancer at $25–$50 per hour (you'd pay $150–$300 per blog post for this volume of output) or a social media manager at $500+ per month. The break-even point is immediate: one blog post repurposed this way saves you 4–6 hours of manual work and $100–$200 in outsourcing costs.

The learning curve is real but short. Expect to spend 3–4 hours on your first full run-through as you test prompts, adjust outputs, and figure out which tools handle which steps best. By the third blog post, you'll move through the workflow in under 2 hours. After five posts, it becomes muscle memory.

Mistakes That Kill This Workflow

Mistake 1: Skipping structure in your original blog post. If your blog post is one long wall of text with no headings or bullet points, the AI tools will struggle to extract clean sections. ChatGPT will generate vague social posts because it can't identify distinct ideas. Predis.ai will produce generic graphics. Fix: Always write with clear H2/H3 headings and break ideas into short paragraphs. Use NeuronWriter's editor to enforce this structure before you start repurposing.

Mistake 2: Using generic prompts and accepting mediocre outputs. If you paste your blog post into ChatGPT and just say "make social media posts," you'll get bland, robotic captions that sound like everyone else's AI content. The quality of your outputs depends entirely on the specificity of your prompts. Fix: Save detailed prompts that specify length, tone, format, and what to avoid (like "do not add hashtags" or "write in spoken language, not formal"). Test and refine these prompts over 2–3 blog posts until the outputs need minimal editing.

Mistake 3: Trying to repurpose weak or off-brand blog posts. This workflow multiplies what you already wrote. If the original post is fluffy, generic, or doesn't reflect your voice, you'll just get 30 pieces of fluffy, generic content. Fix: Only repurpose blog posts you're genuinely proud of—posts with a clear point of view, specific examples, and useful takeaways. The workflow amplifies quality; it doesn't create it.

Mistake 4: Forgetting to batch and schedule. Generating 30 pieces of content is exciting, but if you don't immediately schedule them, they'll sit in a Google Doc and never get published. Fix: Block 30 minutes at the end of your workflow to load everything into your scheduling tool (Buffer, Hootsuite, or LinkedIn's native scheduler). Schedule social posts across the next 2–3 weeks, queue the emails in Beehiiv, and upload video clips to your short-form platforms. The workflow only works if the content actually goes live.
