Fireflies.ai vs Otter.ai: The Honest Breakdown for 2026

June 12, 2026 8 min read MyAI ToolsFinder

Here's the mistake I see almost every week: someone picks a meeting transcription tool based on a feature list screenshot, uses it for three months, then realizes it's completely wrong for how they actually work. They chose Otter.ai because it was "the popular one" when Fireflies.ai would've saved them hours. Or they went with Fireflies because it had more integrations, but they never use those integrations—they just needed Otter's better live transcription for client calls. The tool choice seemed trivial until they calculated how much time they'd wasted editing bad transcripts or searching for action items buried in 47-minute meeting dumps.

I'm writing this in June 2026 after watching both tools evolve significantly over the past year. Fireflies.ai added multi-language dialect support that actually works. Otter.ai rebuilt their action item extraction with a new model that understands context better than before. The gap between them has narrowed in some areas and widened dramatically in others. If you're picking one right now, you need current information—not recycled 2024 comparison charts.

This breakdown covers everything that matters: which tool handles your specific meeting types better, where each one falls short in ways the sales pages won't tell you, and exactly what you're getting at each pricing tier. I've used both extensively for different meeting contexts—sales calls, creative brainstorms, technical planning sessions, and podcast interviews. Let's get specific about what each one actually delivers.

Quick Verdict: Who Wins Where

  • Best for teams with complex workflows: Fireflies.ai — superior CRM integrations, custom topic tracking, and the AskFred AI search that finds specific moments across your entire meeting history in seconds
  • Best for solo professionals and real-time collaboration: Otter.ai — cleaner live transcription interface, better speaker identification in small groups, and the collaborative note-taking features actually get used
  • Best if you're budget-conscious: Otter.ai's free tier gives you 300 monthly minutes versus Fireflies' 40 minutes, making it better for light users who can't justify $18/month yet

Fireflies.ai: Built for Volume and Integration Depth

Fireflies.ai has positioned itself as the meeting intelligence platform for teams that live in their CRM and need meeting data flowing everywhere. The core strength isn't just transcription—it's what happens after the recording stops. Every call gets processed through their conversation intelligence engine that identifies topics discussed, tracks custom keywords you've defined, measures speaker talk time, and extracts action items with assigned owners.

Here's a specific use case where Fireflies.ai dominates: You're running a SaaS sales team that does 30+ demo calls weekly. You need those calls automatically logged to Salesforce with key objections tagged, pricing discussions flagged, and competitor mentions captured. Fireflies connects directly to your calendar, joins meetings as a participant, records everything, and pushes structured data back to your CRM without anyone touching a button. Their Slack integration posts call summaries to relevant channels with timestamps for important moments. If your CEO asks "how many prospects mentioned our enterprise plan pricing this month," you can answer in 90 seconds using their analytics dashboard.

The weaknesses are real though. Fireflies' live transcription lags about 3-5 seconds behind the speaker—not terrible, but noticeable if you're trying to read along during the call. The interface feels enterprise-focused in a way that can be overwhelming for solo users who just want clean transcripts. And their speaker identification struggles more than Otter's when you have 6+ people talking with similar voice profiles or heavy accents. I've had calls where it merged two speakers into one for entire sections, requiring manual correction.

Pricing in 2026 breaks down like this: Free tier gives you 40 minutes monthly across unlimited meetings (basically useless for regular users). Pro is $18/month per user with 8,000 minutes monthly, unlimited transcription credits, and API access. Business tier hits $29/month with advanced analytics, custom topic tracking, and conversation intelligence features. Enterprise pricing is custom but includes SSO, dedicated support, and data residency options. Most solopreneurs and small teams land on Pro—that 8,000 minute ceiling is essentially unlimited for normal meeting loads.

Ideal user profile: Revenue teams, agencies with client calls that need documentation, project managers coordinating across multiple tools who need meeting data everywhere, or anyone running more than 10 substantive meetings weekly who needs that data searchable and actionable.

Otter.ai: Optimized for Clarity and Real-Time Collaboration

Otter.ai took a different path—they focused on making transcription feel like a natural part of the conversation rather than surveillance software. Their interface is cleaner, their live transcription runs faster (1-2 second lag), and their collaborative features actually change how meetings work. During calls, participants can highlight text, add comments, insert images, and assign action items in real-time. I've watched this transform chaotic brainstorm sessions into organized decision records without requiring post-meeting cleanup.

The killer feature most people discover by accident: Otter's speaker identification is substantially better in small group settings (2-6 people). It correctly distinguishes voices even when people talk over each other or have similar vocal ranges. I ran a test with three female colleagues on a strategy call—Fireflies merged two of them for 40% of the transcript while Otter got it right throughout. This matters enormously when you're trying to follow who said what during decision-making conversations.

Where Otter falls short: Integration depth. You can connect it to Zoom, Google Meet, and Microsoft Teams, but the CRM integrations are limited and clunky compared to Fireflies. There's no native Salesforce sync, no Slack auto-posting with timestamps, and the API is more restricted. Their conversation analytics are basic—you get word clouds and talk time, but not the sophisticated topic tracking or custom keyword monitoring that Fireflies offers. If you need meeting data flowing into other systems automatically, Otter makes you do more manual work.

The mobile experience deserves mention—Otter's app is significantly better for recording in-person meetings. The interface is intuitive, battery usage is reasonable, and the audio quality processing handles noisy environments better. I've recorded interviews in coffee shops where Fireflies struggled with background noise but Otter produced usable transcripts.

2026 pricing structure: Free gives you 300 minutes monthly with 30 minutes per conversation—actually usable for light meeting loads. Pro is $16.99/month with 1,200 minutes monthly and 90-minute conversations. Business hits $30/month per user with 6,000 minutes and advanced collaboration features. Enterprise is custom with admin controls and priority support. The free tier is genuinely valuable here, unlike Fireflies' token gesture.

Ideal user profile: Freelancers, consultants, coaches who need clean transcripts fast, content creators recording interviews for podcasts or articles, small teams that collaborate during calls rather than just documenting them, or anyone who prioritizes transcription accuracy over automation depth.

Head-to-Head: Where Each Tool Actually Wins

Transcription Accuracy: Otter wins for English conversations in quiet environments with clear audio. Fireflies edges ahead for multi-language meetings, heavy accents, or technical jargon—their model handles domain-specific vocabulary better after you train it with custom terminology.

Speed and Real-Time Performance: Otter is noticeably faster for live transcription. If you're reading along during calls or need to reference what was just said, that 2-second lag versus Fireflies' 5-second lag matters. Post-meeting processing is comparable—both deliver full transcripts within 2-3 minutes of call completion.

Integration Ecosystem: Fireflies dominates completely. Native connections to Salesforce, HubSpot, Slack, Asana, Notion, and Zapier with rich data mapping. You can build workflows where meeting transcripts automatically populate project management tools with action items assigned to specific people. Otter's integrations feel like checkboxes—they exist but don't do much.

Search and Retrieval: Fireflies' AskFred feature is substantially better for finding specific information across your meeting history. Ask "what did Sarah say about the Q3 budget in our planning calls" and it pulls relevant clips with timestamps. Otter's search works but requires more specific keyword matching—it's a search bar, not an intelligent assistant.

Price-to-Value Ratio: Otter wins for solo users and small teams under 5 people. Their free tier is actually functional, and $16.99/month gets you everything most people need. Fireflies makes more sense at scale—if you're a 10-person team spending $180/month total, those integration and analytics features justify the cost. Under that threshold, you're paying for capabilities you won't use.

Learning Curve: Otter is simpler to start using effectively—sign up, connect your calendar, and you're getting value in 10 minutes. Fireflies requires configuration to shine—setting up custom topics, connecting your CRM properly, training teammates on the analytics dashboard. That investment pays off for teams, but it's overhead for individuals.

Real Scenarios: Which Tool Solves Your Specific Problem

Scenario 1: You're a product manager running 15-20 user research calls monthly. You need transcripts searchable by feature discussed, pain points mentioned, and user segments. You want to build a research repository where anyone on the product team can query "what did enterprise users say about pricing" and get clips from multiple calls.

Winner: Fireflies.ai. Set up custom topics for each major feature and pain point category. Configure it to auto-post summaries to your Slack #user-research channel. Six months later, you have a queryable database of user insights that took zero manual organization. Otter would give you great transcripts but require manual tagging and organization.

Scenario 2: You're a freelance marketing consultant who takes 8-10 client calls weekly. You need accurate transcripts to reference when writing strategies and proposals. You share notes with clients during calls to confirm understanding. Budget is tight—you're not paying for features you don't use.

Winner: Otter.ai. Start with their free tier (300 minutes covers your monthly load). The real-time collaboration means you can highlight key points during calls and share the transcript immediately after. Client sees you as organized and thorough. You're not using CRM integrations or analytics dashboards—you just need clean records of what was said. Save that $18/month.

Scenario 3: You're running a six-person sales team with 100+ prospect calls monthly. You need call data in Salesforce automatically, competitive intelligence tracked across all conversations, and coaching insights on which reps are talking too much versus asking questions. You want to train new reps by having them search for "best discovery question examples" across your team's call history.

Winner: Fireflies.ai without question. This is exactly what they built for. Your $174/month investment (six Pro licenses) gets you conversation intelligence that would take an operations person 20 hours weekly to compile manually. The analytics dashboard shows you talk-time ratios, question frequency, and objection patterns. Connect it to Make.com or Zapier for custom workflows—like auto-creating deal notes with key quotes when pricing gets discussed.

What Most Comparisons Won't Tell You

Both tools have quirks that only emerge after extended use. Fireflies' bot joining meetings can create awkward moments—I've had prospects ask "who's this Fireflies notetaker person" before calls start, requiring explanation that makes some people uncomfortable. You can use their dial-in option instead, but it's less convenient. Otter avoids this by integrating directly with meeting platforms rather than joining as a participant, but that means it only works for video calls—you can't easily capture phone conversations.

Storage and retention policies matter more than people realize. Fireflies stores your transcripts indefinitely on paid plans (deleted only when you manually remove them). Otter purges transcripts after a retention period based on your plan tier unless you explicitly save them. If you're building a long-term knowledge base of conversations, understand those policies before committing.

Both tools offer custom vocabulary training, but Fireflies' implementation is more powerful. You can upload glossaries of technical terms, product names, and industry jargon that it will recognize accurately. I tested this with medical device terminology—Fireflies got 90%+ accuracy after vocabulary training while Otter struggled with the same terms even after manual corrections.

The AI summary quality has improved substantially on both platforms through 2026, but they take different approaches. Fireflies generates structured summaries with distinct sections: overview, key points, action items, questions raised. Otter creates more narrative-style summaries that read naturally but require more effort to extract specific information. Choose based on whether you want structured data (Fireflies) or readable notes (Otter).

The Final Verdict: Stop Overthinking This

For most individual users—freelancers, consultants, coaches, content creators—Otter.ai is the right choice. It's cleaner, faster to value, has a functional free tier, and does the core job (accurate transcription with good search) better for solo use cases. You don't need enterprise features you won't configure properly.

For teams of 5+ people, especially those in sales, customer success, or product roles where meeting data needs to flow into other systems, Fireflies.ai justifies its complexity and cost. The integration depth and conversation intelligence features save hours weekly once properly configured. You're not buying transcription software—you're buying meeting data infrastructure.

The edge cases: If you're a solo user doing 20+ calls weekly with sophisticated workflow needs (you're already using Make.com or Zapier extensively, you track metrics obsessively), Fireflies might make sense even solo. If you're a small team that just needs shared transcripts without the automation complexity, Otter works fine at scale—their Business plan at $30/user isn't unreasonable.

My specific recommendation: Start with Otter's free tier for one month. If you find yourself frustrated by the 300-minute limit or wishing for better integrations, that's your signal to evaluate Fireflies Pro. If the free tier handles your needs or you upgrade to Otter Pro and it feels complete, you've saved yourself from overbuying features you'd never configure. Don't make the choice based on what you might need someday—choose based on your actual current workflow and upgrade when that workflow changes.

Questions People Actually Ask Before Buying

Can I switch tools without losing my transcript history? Both tools let you export transcripts as text files, PDFs, or subtitles. You'll lose the searchability and native features (timestamps, speaker labels, action items) but the content survives. If you're building long-term meeting documentation, factor in that switching costs. Neither offers a direct import tool for the other's exports—you'd be starting fresh with native features.

Do these tools work for non-English meetings? Fireflies supports 60+ languages with varying accuracy—Spanish, French, German, and Mandarin work well; less common languages are hit-or-miss. Otter primarily focuses on English with limited Spanish support that's notably worse than Fireflies. If you regularly conduct meetings in multiple languages or non-English languages, Fireflies is the only real option here despite its other limitations.

What happens if I record something sensitive or need to delete data? Both tools allow immediate transcript deletion and offer admin controls for data retention policies on business plans. Fireflies provides more granular controls—you can redact specific transcript sections or exclude certain meetings from processing entirely. Both are GDPR and SOC 2 compliant, but if data sovereignty matters (EU, healthcare, financial services), verify their enterprise data residency options match your requirements. Neither tool should be used for truly confidential discussions without explicit participant consent and legal review.
