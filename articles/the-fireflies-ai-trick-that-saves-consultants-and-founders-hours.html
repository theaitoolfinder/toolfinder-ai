The Fireflies.ai Trick That Saves Consultants and Founders hours of note-taking and re-explaining, Starting Today

October 10, 2026 7 min read By Papabenj · My AI Tools Finder

Six months ago, a 45-minute client discovery call meant another 90 minutes of reconstruction work afterward. Now it takes 3 minutes to review everything that mattered, with zero manual note-taking.

If you're a consultant or founder who spends your days in back-to-back client calls, strategy sessions, and discovery meetings, you know this pain intimately. Someone says something critical at minute 23. You're actively listening, building rapport, reading the room. You think "I'll remember that" or maybe you jot down a half-legible note that says "compliance issue - Q3?" and keep talking. The call ends. You have seventeen more things to do. And now you're staring at a blank document trying to reconstruct what actually happened.

Here's what this costs you every single week: At least 60-90 minutes per major client call spent writing up notes from memory. Another 30-45 minutes fielding follow-up emails because you missed a detail or got a number wrong. And the hidden cost nobody talks about: the calls you have to schedule to re-confirm what was actually decided, because neither you nor the client wrote it down accurately the first time. That's easily 6-8 hours per week just managing information you already heard once.

For founders running customer discovery interviews or investor meetings, the problem multiplies. You're having 15 conversations per week with potential customers. Each one contains gold—specific language they use to describe their problem, objections they raise, features they didn't know they needed. But you're talking, not transcribing. By Friday afternoon, interviews 1 through 6 have blurred together into a vague sense of "they seemed interested" with none of the verbatim quotes you need to write actual copy or build the right feature.

The financial cost is real too. If your billable rate is $200/hour and you're spending 7 hours per week on post-call administrative work that generates zero revenue, that's $1,400 in lost billing capacity. Per week. Scale that across a year and you're looking at $72,800 in opportunity cost from a problem that feels too small to fix but never actually goes away.

Why the Obvious Fixes Don't Work

Most consultants try one of three workarounds, and all of them have serious limitations.

The first is hiring a virtual assistant to join calls and take notes. This works for some people, but it changes the dynamic of every conversation. Clients get quieter when there's an extra person on the line. Sensitive topics don't come up as naturally. And you're now coordinating schedules with another human for every single meeting, plus paying $25-50 per call for the service. For founders doing rapid customer interviews, this approach doesn't scale at all—you'd need the VA on 15 calls per week, and the cost quickly exceeds a full salary.

The second workaround is recording calls manually and listening back later. Except nobody actually does this. Listening to a 45-minute call takes 45 minutes, and you've already lived through it once. The recordings pile up in a folder labeled "to review" and never get opened. It's technically a solution but practically useless because the friction is too high.

The third approach is just getting better at taking notes during the call. You buy a second monitor. You get faster at typing. You develop shorthand. But you're still splitting your attention between the human in front of you and the document in front of you, and everyone can tell. The quality of the conversation suffers because you're not fully present. You miss the subtle hesitation before they answer a question. You don't catch the moment to go deeper because you're typing.

What Changes Once This Is Solved

Imagine ending a client call and having a full transcript waiting for you in 90 seconds. Not just a wall of text, but organized notes with action items already pulled out, key decisions highlighted, and every important detail tagged by topic. You review it in three minutes, add it to the client's project folder, and move on.

The call you just had stays in your memory as a conversation, not a transcription task. You were fully present because you didn't have to divide your attention. The client felt heard because you were actually listening, not performing the theater of note-taking. And when you send the follow-up email 10 minutes later with a perfect summary of what was discussed and what happens next, the client thinks you're the most organized consultant they've ever worked with.

For founders, this means every customer interview actually becomes useful data. You can search across 30 conversations for every time someone used the word "compliance" or "frustrating" and pull exact quotes. You can identify patterns you would have missed because your memory isn't designed to hold verbatim text from 30 different people. The insights that used to require a dedicated researcher are now just sitting there, tagged and searchable, because the tool caught everything you didn't have capacity to write down.

You get 6-8 hours back per week. Your follow-up is faster and more accurate. And you stop having those panicked moments three days after a call when you realize you forgot to write down something critical and now you have to ask again.

The Fix

Fireflies.ai is a meeting assistant that joins your video calls, records everything, and automatically generates transcripts, summaries, and action items without any manual work from you. It's not a generic transcription service—it's built specifically for people who spend their days in meetings and need the important parts extracted and organized, not just a text dump of everything that was said.

Here's why it works where other solutions don't: Fireflies joins the call as a participant (you can even hide it from view if your video platform supports it), so there's nothing extra to remember to start or stop. The transcription accuracy is high enough that you can trust it for technical terms, numbers, and proper nouns—the stuff that matters most in client work. And instead of just giving you a wall of text, it uses AI to identify action items, key questions, and decisions made during the call, then formats them into sections you can actually use.

The tool integrates directly with Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, and most other major platforms, so it works with whatever your clients already use. You don't need to convince anyone to switch tools or download new software. From the client's perspective, they either see "Fireflies Notetaker" listed as a participant, or they don't notice anything at all if you've configured it to join silently.

Pricing starts with a free plan that gives you 800 minutes of transcription per month—enough for most consultants running 10-15 calls per week. The Pro plan runs $10/month per user and removes the cap while adding features like custom vocabulary for industry-specific terms. Business plans start at $19/month per user and add team features, analytics, and better search. For the hours it saves, even the paid tiers cost less than one billable hour per month.

What makes Fireflies different from just recording calls and using a generic transcription service is the post-processing. It doesn't just tell you what was said—it tells you what mattered. That's the difference between a tool you use once and abandon, and a tool that becomes part of how you work.

Exactly How to Use It to Solve This

Step 1: Connect your calendar and video platform. Sign up at fireflies.ai and link your Google Calendar, Outlook, or whichever calendar you use to schedule meetings. Then connect your video conferencing tool—Zoom, Google Meet, Teams, or others. Fireflies will automatically detect when you have a meeting scheduled and ask if you want it to join. You can set it to join every meeting by default, or manually select which ones get recorded. For client work, most consultants set it to auto-join everything, then manually remove it from internal team calls where notes don't matter.

Step 2: Let it join your next client call. When your meeting starts, Fireflies will join as a participant. Depending on your settings, it will either announce itself ("Fireflies Notetaker has joined") or join silently. If a client asks what it is, the honest answer works fine: "That's my note-taking assistant—it records and transcribes so I can focus completely on our conversation instead of splitting attention with my keyboard. Everything stays private to our project." In three years of consultants using this tool, client pushback is rare because the value proposition is obvious—you're more present, not less.

Step 3: Have the conversation like normal. This is the part that feels strange the first time and natural by the third call. You just talk. You don't take notes. You don't half-listen while typing. If the client mentions a specific deadline, you don't write it down—Fireflies caught it. If they describe a complex technical requirement, you stay in the conversation instead of asking them to slow down so you can type. The quality of your questions improves immediately because your brain isn't managing two tasks at once.

Step 4: Review the output in under five minutes. When the call ends, Fireflies processes the recording. Within 90 seconds to 3 minutes (depending on call length), you'll get an email with a link to the transcript and AI-generated summary. Open it, and you'll see the full transcript broken into speaker labels, a summary of key topics discussed, a list of action items extracted from the conversation, and timestamps for easy navigation. Here's what this looks like in practice: A 40-minute client discovery call generates a transcript, a 4-paragraph summary, and a list of 6 action items ("Client will send current contract by Friday," "Follow up on integration question with their IT lead," "Schedule technical demo for week of March 15"). You scan it in 3 minutes, verify the action items are accurate, and you're done.

Step 5: Use the search and share features for long-term value. The real power shows up two weeks later when the client references something from a previous call and you can't quite remember the details. Instead of digging through manual notes or re-listening to recordings, you open Fireflies, search for the client's name plus a keyword like "budget" or "timeline," and pull the exact quote in 10 seconds. You can also share specific clips or summaries directly with team members or clients—send just the 2-minute section where the client described their problem, not the entire 45-minute call.

Real example: A strategy consultant running customer discovery for a SaaS product had 22 customer interviews in three weeks. Instead of manually coding qualitative data, she searched Fireflies for the phrase "wish it could" across all 22 transcripts and found 18 feature requests she'd completely forgotten about from the earlier interviews. Those verbatim quotes went straight into the product roadmap presentation. Total time spent: 12 minutes. Traditional qualitative analysis of 22 hour-long interviews: 15-20 hours minimum.

"But What If…" — Answering the Real Objections

Objection 1: "Will clients be uncomfortable being recorded?" In practice, this is rare, especially if you mention it upfront ("I use a transcription assistant so I can focus completely on our conversation—is that alright with you?"). The framing matters: you're doing this to be more present and accurate, not to surveil them. For sensitive calls where recording truly isn't appropriate, you can turn Fireflies off for that specific meeting. But most consultants report that after the first mention, clients forget it's there—and many actually appreciate getting a copy of the summary afterward.

Objection 2: "I'm worried about the learning curve or setup time." Fireflies is deliberately low-friction. The initial setup—connecting your calendar and video platform—takes about 5 minutes. After that, it's completely automatic. There's no interface to learn for basic use, no daily habits to build. It just runs. The first time you review a transcript might take 10 minutes as you get familiar with the layout, but by your third call you'll be scanning summaries in under 3 minutes without thinking about it.

Objection 3: "What if it misses something important or transcribes incorrectly?" Transcription accuracy on Fireflies is typically 90-95% for clear audio with native English speakers, and higher if you add custom vocabulary for industry terms your clients use frequently. That's accurate enough that the risk of a critical error is low—and much lower than your risk of forgetting or misremembering something when relying on manual notes. You're not using this as a legal transcript; you're using it to capture details and action items that would otherwise be lost. For the occasional word that gets transcribed incorrectly, you catch it during your 3-minute review and correct it before sending the summary to anyone.
