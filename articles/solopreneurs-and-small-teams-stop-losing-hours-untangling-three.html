Solopreneurs and Small Teams: Stop Losing hours untangling three different apps — Taskade AI Solves This in Minutes

July 16, 2026 7 min read By Papabenj · My AI Tools Finder

A project manager costs $75/hour to untangle your scattered tasks, notes, and project timelines across Notion, Todoist, and Google Docs. With Taskade AI, it costs $8/month and fifteen minutes to have everything unified and talking to each other.

You know the drill. Your daily standup notes live in Google Docs. Your task list bounces between Todoist and a paper notebook. Your project roadmap sits in Notion. And when a client asks "where are we on the Q2 deliverables?" you spend the next twenty minutes opening tabs, cross-referencing dates, and mentally stitching together three different versions of the truth.

This isn't a minor inconvenience. For solopreneurs and small teams running lean, this fragmentation is bleeding 6-8 hours per week—time you could spend closing deals, shipping features, or actually finishing the work instead of managing the systems that are supposed to help you finish the work. When your to-do list doesn't know what your project plan says, and your meeting notes exist in a completely different universe from your deadlines, you're not just disorganized. You're running a business with one hand tied behind your back.

The cognitive overhead alone is crushing. Every context switch between apps costs you focus. Every manual sync between your task list and your project tracker introduces errors. Every time you have to explain to a teammate where to find the latest version of something, you're admitting your tools aren't working for you—you're working for them.

Why the Obvious Fixes Don't Work

Most people try one of three workarounds. The first is the "master spreadsheet" approach—building a single source of truth in Google Sheets or Airtable that links everything together. This works for about two weeks until you realize you've just added a fourth system that also needs constant updating, and now you're copying information into a spreadsheet instead of getting actual work done.

The second fix is hiring help. A virtual assistant or project coordinator who can keep everything synchronized across platforms. This solves the problem if you can afford $1,200-$2,500/month for part-time project management support. Most solopreneurs and two-person teams can't, and even if they could, they're still stuck with the underlying issue: tools that refuse to communicate with each other.

The third attempt is the "just use one tool for everything" strategy. Pick Notion or ClickUp or Asana and force all your workflows into it. The problem? These tools are built for specific use cases. Notion is brilliant for documentation but clunky for rapid task capture. Todoist is perfect for personal tasks but weak on collaborative project views. When you force everything into one platform, you end up with a mediocre experience across the board—and usually, within a month, you're back to using specialized tools for the things that matter most.

What Changes Once This Is Solved

Imagine opening one workspace in the morning and seeing everything: today's tasks automatically pulled from your project plans, yesterday's meeting notes linked to the relevant action items, and your weekly goals sitting right next to the tactical work that actually moves them forward. No tab-switching. No "where did I write that down?" moments. No Friday afternoons spent reconciling three different systems before you can report progress.

When your tasks, notes, and projects exist in the same environment and actually talk to each other, you get 5-7 hours back every week. That's a full workday. You spend less time managing information and more time using it. Your team (even if that team is just you and a contractor) sees the same reality at the same time. Questions like "what's the priority?" and "where did we land on that?" get answered in seconds instead of Slack threads.

The mental relief is immediate. You stop carrying a low-grade anxiety about what you might be forgetting, because the system is designed to surface what matters when it matters. Your planning and execution exist in the same place, so the gap between "what we said we'd do" and "what we're actually doing" shrinks to near-zero.

The Fix

Exactly How to Use It to Solve This

Step 1: Create a unified project workspace

Sign up for Taskade (free to start) and create your first workspace. Instead of separating tasks, notes, and planning into different tools, build one workspace per major project or client. Within that workspace, create sections for active tasks, project notes, timelines, and resources. Use Taskade's outline structure to nest related information—your Q2 marketing campaign workspace might have top-level sections for Strategy Notes, Content Calendar, Task Checklist, and Campaign Assets.

Step 2: Migrate your scattered information with AI assistance

Instead of manually retyping everything from your three existing apps, use Taskade's AI to speed this up. Paste your messy Google Doc meeting notes into a new Taskade section and prompt the AI: "Extract all action items from these notes and create a task list with owner assignments and deadlines." The AI scans the text, identifies commitments, and generates a structured task list in seconds. Do the same with project plans—paste rough outlines and ask the AI to "organize this into a project timeline with milestones and dependencies."

Step 3: Set up automated task generation from your workflows

For recurring projects, create a template once and use AI to generate variations. Let's say you run a weekly client report process. Build a template workspace with sections for data collection, analysis, and writeup. When a new week starts, duplicate the template and ask the AI: "Generate a task checklist for this week's client report, including data pulls from Monday and delivery by Thursday EOD." The AI populates your workspace with dated, actionable tasks based on your template structure.

Step 4: Let AI keep your task list and notes synchronized

When you're in a meeting taking notes in Taskade, the AI can actively suggest tasks as you type. Write "we need to finalize the budget by next Tuesday" and Taskade prompts you to convert that into a task with a due date. Your notes and tasks stay connected automatically. Later, if you update a task status, you can ask the AI to "summarize all completed tasks this week for my Friday update email"—and it pulls directly from your workspace.

Step 5: Use AI agents for project expansion

When starting something new, instead of opening a blank page and staring at it, ask a Taskade AI agent for structure. Prompt: "I'm launching a new email course for freelance designers. Create a project plan with phases for curriculum design, email sequence writing, landing page creation, and launch promotion." The AI generates a multi-phase project outline complete with task breakdowns. You're not starting from zero—you're editing and customizing a solid first draft that already lives in your task workspace.

Real example: A two-person content agency was managing editorial calendars in Airtable, draft notes in Google Docs, and task assignments in Trello. Consolidating into Taskade meant their content calendar, research notes, and production tasks existed in one workspace per client. When a writer needed context for an assignment, everything was there. When the founder needed to check status, one glance showed draft progress, pending edits, and publish dates. Time spent on project coordination dropped from 90 minutes daily to about 20.

"But What If…" — Answering the Real Objections

Objection 1: "I've already invested time customizing my current tools. Migrating sounds like a nightmare."

Fair concern. The AI-assisted migration makes this faster than you expect. You're not rebuilding systems from scratch—you're pasting existing content and letting AI restructure it. Most solopreneurs report getting their core projects migrated in 2-3 hours, not days. Start with one active project, leave the rest where they are, and expand once you feel the benefit. Taskade doesn't require all-or-nothing commitment on day one.

Objection 2: "Another subscription and another tool to learn feels like adding to the problem, not solving it."

Taskade replaces multiple subscriptions, not adds to them. If you're currently paying for Notion ($8-10/month), Todoist Premium ($4/month), and a transcription service for meeting notes ($10-15/month), you're already spending $22-29 monthly. Objection 3: "Will this actually work for my specific workflow? I have weird requirements."

Taskade's flexibility is specifically built for non-standard workflows. You can structure workspaces as strict kanban boards, loose outlines, mind maps, or hybrid combinations. A freelance developer uses it as a code project tracker with nested subtasks for features. A podcast producer uses it for episode planning with research notes and guest coordination in the same workspace. The free plan lets you test your specific workflow with zero financial risk.
