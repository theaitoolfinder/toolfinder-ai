Gamma vs Beautiful.ai: Features, Price, and the Winner for Your Use Case

June 20, 2026 8 min read By Papabenj · My AI Tools Finder

A designer charges $2,500 to build a 20-slide investor deck. With the right AI presentation tool, you can create something pitch-ready in under an hour for $20. But here's the catch: choosing the wrong tool means wasting three hours fighting templates that won't bend to your vision, or ending up with slides that scream "I used a template." When it comes to AI-powered presentation builders, Gamma and Beautiful.ai sit at the top of most shortlists—but they solve fundamentally different problems. Pick the wrong one, and you'll either spend your Saturday manually adjusting alignment issues or presenting slides that look slick but say nothing memorable. This isn't about features lists. It's about which tool actually gets you to "good enough to send" fastest, and which one makes your ideas look smarter than they actually are.

Both tools use AI to generate presentation layouts, but Gamma leans heavily into content generation and narrative flow, while Beautiful.ai obsesses over design consistency and visual polish. If you've ever opened PowerPoint, typed a brilliant idea, and then spent 40 minutes making the bullet points line up, you already understand why these tools exist. The question is whether you need an AI writing partner or an AI designer—and the answer depends entirely on what's harder for you: thinking of what to say, or making it look professional.

Quick Verdict: Who Wins Where

  • Best for founders and consultants who need to build narrative-driven decks fast: Gamma wins. Its AI writes coherent slide content from prompts, handles storytelling structure, and lets you ship decks without hiring a designer.
  • Best for marketers and salespeople who already have content but need flawless visual consistency: Beautiful.ai takes it. Every slide auto-adjusts when you add elements, and you literally cannot create an ugly layout—the tool won't let you.
  • Best if you're budget-conscious and need unlimited presentations: Gamma's free tier is shockingly generous. Beautiful.ai gates most useful features behind a $12/month paywall.

Gamma: The AI That Writes Your Deck While You Watch

Gamma treats presentations like documents with slides attached. You start by typing a prompt—something like "investor pitch for a B2B SaaS analytics platform targeting mid-market sales teams"—and the AI generates an outline, writes slide content, and suggests a visual structure. It's not just dropping your text into templates; it's making editorial decisions about what goes on which slide and how to sequence your argument.

The core strength here is speed from zero to draft. I tested this by building a product launch deck for a fictional project management tool. I gave Gamma a 200-word description of the product, target customer, and three key benefits. Eight minutes later, I had a 14-slide deck with headlines, body copy, a competitive positioning slide, and even placeholder CTAs. Was it perfect? No. Did it give me a structure I could edit instead of starting from a blank screen? Absolutely. That's worth real money if you bill hourly or just hate the cognitive load of structuring a narrative from scratch.

Where Gamma stumbles is design flexibility. The templates look modern but safe—lots of gradients, rounded corners, and that specific shade of purple every SaaS tool uses. You can customize colors and fonts, but the underlying grid system is rigid. If you want a slide with an asymmetric layout or overlapping elements, you'll fight the editor. Beautiful.ai handles this better by simply not letting you break the grid, which sounds limiting but actually creates more polished results.

Pricing is straightforward: Free tier gives you unlimited presentations (with Gamma branding), export to PDF, and basic templates. Plus ($8/month) removes branding, adds custom fonts, and unlocks advanced analytics embedding. Pro ($16/month) adds team collaboration, brand kits, and priority support. For solo founders or consultants who need to crank out pitch decks, one-pagers, and strategy docs regularly, the free tier handles 80% of use cases. The main reason to upgrade is removing the "Made with Gamma" footer before sending to clients.

Ideal user: You're a consultant, founder, or product manager who needs to create narrative-heavy presentations often. You're comfortable editing AI-generated content but dread staring at blank slides. You value speed over pixel-perfect design, and you'd rather spend 15 minutes refining AI output than 90 minutes building from scratch.

Beautiful.ai: The Design System That Won't Let You Fail

Beautiful.ai approaches presentations the way Figma approaches design: by enforcing rules that prevent visual chaos. Every template is built on a smart grid system that auto-adjusts when you add or remove content. Drop in a fourth bullet point, and the font size shrinks proportionally. Add an image to a two-column layout, and text reflows automatically. You're not designing slides—you're filling in a design system that someone already solved.

This is powerful if you already know what you want to say but have no design sense. I built a quarterly business review deck using Beautiful.ai's "Modern" template. I pasted in revenue charts, typed out key metrics, and added team photos. Every slide looked professionally balanced without me touching alignment tools or spacing. The AI here isn't generating content; it's making micro-adjustments to maintain visual hierarchy and consistency. When I added a longer headline, the tool automatically reduced font size and adjusted padding. When I dragged an image to a different zone, the layout reconfigured around it.

The weakness is creative constraint. Beautiful.ai gives you roughly 60 slide templates across a dozen themes, and while you can customize colors and fonts, you're fundamentally locked into their layout logic. If your brand demands a specific visual language—say, overlapping elements, text over images, or asymmetric grids—you'll hit walls quickly. The tool is opinionated by design. That's a feature for non-designers and a limitation for anyone who wants full creative control.

Pricing: Free tier is borderline unusable—you get three presentations total and can't export without watermarks. Pro ($12/month) unlocks unlimited presentations, PDF/PPT export, and all templates. Team ($40/user/month) adds collaboration features, brand controls, and shared asset libraries. The jump from free to Pro is steep, but if you're creating client-facing decks regularly, the time saved on alignment and spacing issues pays for itself in one project.

Ideal user: You're a marketer, salesperson, or consultant who presents often and needs every deck to look polished. You have clear ideas but weak design skills. You'd rather work within smart constraints than fight with alignment tools. You're fine paying $12/month to never think about slide layout again.

Head-to-Head: Where Each Tool Actually Wins

Content Generation: Gamma destroys Beautiful.ai here. Gamma writes slide content from prompts, suggests narrative structures, and can expand bullet points into full paragraphs. Beautiful.ai has zero AI writing features—it's purely a design tool. If you need help figuring out what to say, Gamma is the only choice.

Design Consistency: Beautiful.ai wins decisively. Its auto-layout engine ensures every slide maintains visual balance regardless of content length. Gamma templates look fine, but you can absolutely create ugly slides if you're not careful. Beautiful.ai makes that nearly impossible.

Speed to First Draft: Depends on your starting point. If you're beginning with just an idea, Gamma gets you to a reviewable deck faster because it writes the content. If you already have your talking points in a Google Doc, Beautiful.ai is faster—paste in your content, pick a template, done.

Customization Flexibility: Gamma offers more freedom to break layouts and experiment with structure, but that freedom creates opportunities to mess things up. Beautiful.ai is intentionally rigid. Neither tool gives you Figma-level control, but Gamma lets you get closer.

Collaboration Features: Both support real-time collaboration on paid plans, but Beautiful.ai's Team tier is genuinely built for agencies and marketing teams, with shared brand kits and asset libraries. Gamma's collaboration feels more like Google Docs—functional but not sophisticated.

Export and Sharing: Gamma exports to PDF and lets you share via link with analytics (who viewed, how long). Beautiful.ai exports to PDF and PowerPoint, which matters if your client needs to edit slides in their own tools. Gamma links are gorgeous for sending to investors or prospects; Beautiful.ai exports are better for handing off to teams.

Pricing Value: Gamma's free tier is usable for real work. Beautiful.ai's free tier is a trial in disguise. If budget is tight, Gamma wins. If you're paying anyway, the $8 vs $12 difference is negligible compared to time saved.

Real-World Scenarios: Which Tool to Grab

Scenario 1: You're a startup founder building a seed-round pitch deck. Use Gamma. You need narrative structure more than pixel-perfect design at this stage. Gamma will help you sequence your problem-solution-market-traction story, write initial slide content, and give you a deck you can iterate on with co-founders. Export to PDF, send to three investors, and refine based on feedback. The free tier handles this perfectly. Only upgrade if you're sending to top-tier VCs who'll judge the "Made with Gamma" footer (they will).

Scenario 2: You're a marketing manager creating monthly performance reports for executives. Use Beautiful.ai. You have the data and talking points already; you just need it to look consistent and professional every month. Pick a template, create slides for each key metric, and save it as a master. Each month, update the numbers and charts—Beautiful.ai auto-adjusts layouts so nothing breaks. Export to PowerPoint so your CMO can add their own slides before presenting to the board.

Scenario 3: You're a consultant delivering client strategy decks weekly. This one's closer, but I'd lean Gamma. Speed matters more than perfect design when you're juggling multiple clients, and Gamma's AI content generation cuts hours off deck creation time. Use a consistent template, build a library of reusable slides, and customize per client. The analytics feature is clutch here—you can see if your client actually reviewed the deck before your call. If your consulting brand depends on ultra-polished visuals, Beautiful.ai makes sense, but be prepared to write all content yourself.

What Neither Tool Does Well (And Why That Matters)

Both Gamma and Beautiful.ai struggle with complex data visualization. If you need custom charts, interactive graphs, or sophisticated infographics, you'll still need to build those in tools like Figma or Canva and import them. Beautiful.ai has better built-in chart templates, but they're still basic compared to dedicated data viz tools. Gamma's charts are frankly weak—stick to simple bar and line graphs or import from elsewhere.

Neither tool replaces PowerPoint for high-stakes presentations that need precise animation timing or custom transitions. If you're presenting on stage at a conference, you'll probably export from these tools and refine in Keynote or PowerPoint. They're excellent for decks that get shared digitally or presented in small meetings, less so for theatrical presentations.

Integration ecosystems are thin for both. You can't pull data directly from your CRM or analytics platform into slides. If you're building automated reporting workflows, you'd want something like Make.com to handle the data pipeline, then manually update your presentation. For most users this isn't a dealbreaker, but if you fantasized about auto-generated weekly reports, neither tool delivers that yet.

The Actual Winner for Most People

If you create more than two presentations per month and value time over perfect design, Gamma wins. The AI content generation alone saves hours of staring at blank slides, and the free tier is generous enough that you can use it seriously without paying. You'll get decks that look good enough for 90% of business contexts, and you'll ship them fast. Upgrade to Plus ($8/month) once the watermark starts feeling unprofessional.

Beautiful.ai is the better choice if you're design-sensitive, already have content workflows figured out, and present to audiences who notice visual inconsistencies. Marketers, sales enablement teams, and agencies get the most value here. The $12/month Pro plan is mandatory, which makes this a pricier tool, but the time saved on layout work genuinely adds up if you're creating multiple decks weekly.

For most solopreneurs and small teams, start with Gamma's free tier. If you find yourself manually adjusting slide layouts constantly and wishing things would just auto-align, switch to Beautiful.ai. If you're happy with the structure but wish the AI would write more, stay with Gamma and upgrade.

Don't overthink this. Both tools offer free trials or free tiers. Build the same deck in both and see which workflow feels faster. The best presentation tool is the one you'll actually use instead of procrastinating for three days before a deadline.

FAQ: The Questions You're Actually Googling

Can I import existing PowerPoint decks into Gamma or Beautiful.ai? Beautiful.ai lets you import PPT files, though formatting often breaks and you'll need to reapply templates. Gamma doesn't support direct PowerPoint import—you'll need to rebuild or copy-paste content. If you have a library of existing decks you want to modernize, Beautiful.ai has a slight edge here, but expect to spend time fixing imported slides regardless.

Which tool works better for team collaboration? Beautiful.ai's Team plan ($40/user/month) is built for this, with shared brand kits, asset libraries, and proper permissioning. Gamma's collaboration is more basic—multiple people can edit simultaneously, but there's no sophisticated version control or approval workflows. If you're a team of one or two, either works. If you're a marketing team of five creating dozens of decks monthly, Beautiful.ai's Team plan is worth the premium.

Do these presentations work offline or require internet? Both are cloud-based tools that require internet to create and edit. You can export to PDF and present offline, but there's no desktop app for either. If you present in places with unreliable wifi, export your final deck to PDF or PowerPoint before leaving the office. Gamma's web-based sharing links obviously won't work offline, so plan accordingly.
