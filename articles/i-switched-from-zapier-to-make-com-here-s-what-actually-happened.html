I Switched from Zapier to Make.com — Here's What Actually Happened

July 05, 2026 8 min read By Papabenj · My AI Tools Finder

I burned $247 in unnecessary Zapier subscription fees before I finally pulled the trigger and switched to Make.com. That's six months of paying for a tool that was doing maybe 40% of what I actually needed, while I manually handled the rest in spreadsheets and Gmail filters. The moment I migrated my first workflow to Make.com, I realized I'd been driving a sports car in first gear—and paying premium prices for the privilege.

If you're reading this, you're probably in one of two camps: either you're already frustrated with Zapier's pricing tiers and wondering if there's a better way, or you're trying to choose between these two automation platforms for the first time. Either way, you need the truth about what actually changes when you make this switch—not the sanitized marketing version, but the real operational differences that affect your daily work.

Here's what happened when I moved 23 active workflows from Zapier to Make.com over a three-week period in April 2026, including the unexpected problems I hit, the features I didn't know I was missing, and the exact scenarios where each platform still wins.

The Quick Verdict: Which Platform for Which User

  • Best for complex, multi-step automations: Make.com wins decisively. The visual flow builder handles conditional logic, data transformation, and parallel processing that would require Zapier's most expensive tiers.
  • Best for quick, simple two-app connections: Zapier remains faster to set up if you're just connecting Gmail to Google Sheets or similar straightforward tasks. Five-minute setup versus fifteen.
  • Best if you're budget-conscious: Make.com offers 10,000 operations per month on their free tier versus Zapier's 100 tasks. For small teams and solopreneurs, this isn't even close.

What Zapier Actually Does Well (And Where It Falls Short)

Let's be honest about Zapier's strengths first. I used it for three years before switching, and there were legitimate reasons I stuck with it that long.

Zapier's killer feature is its library of pre-built integrations. As of mid-2026, they're connected to over 6,000 apps—everything from mainstream tools like Grammarly and Ahrefs to obscure industry-specific SaaS products. When I needed to connect Beehiiv to our CRM for newsletter subscriber management, Zapier had a native integration ready to go. No API documentation reading required, no custom webhooks, just pick from dropdown menus and test.

The second genuine advantage is setup speed for simple workflows. If you're connecting two popular apps with a straightforward trigger-action relationship, Zapier's interface gets you there in minutes. I had a workflow running that sent new Fireflies.ai meeting transcripts to a specific Slack channel—took me four minutes to configure, worked perfectly every time.

But here's where the cracks showed: the moment I needed to add conditional logic, the simplicity became a constraint. I wanted to filter those Fireflies transcripts by meeting duration—only send summaries of calls longer than 20 minutes. In Zapier, this required adding a "Filter" step, which counted as an additional task in my usage quota. Then I wanted to extract specific action items from the transcript using text parsing. Another step, another task counted.

My workflow went from a clean two-step automation to a five-step chain, and suddenly I was burning through my monthly task allocation on a single use case. Zapier's pricing tiers tell the story: the Free plan offers 100 tasks per month (essentially useless for any real business workflow), Starter is $29.99/month for 750 tasks, and Professional jumps to $73.50/month for 2,000 tasks. If you're running multiple automations with any complexity, you'll hit Professional tier within your first month.

The ideal Zapier user is someone who needs 5-10 very simple automations, values speed over customization, and doesn't need heavy data transformation. That's a narrower use case than their marketing suggests.

Why Make.com Handles Complexity Differently (And What It Costs You)

Make.com—formerly Integromat before their 2022 rebrand—approaches automation from a fundamentally different philosophy. Instead of linear chains of triggers and actions, you build scenarios using a visual flow chart with branches, routers, and parallel processing paths.

When I rebuilt that Fireflies meeting summary workflow in Make.com, the difference was immediate. I could add filtering, text parsing, conditional routing, and custom formatting all within a single scenario, and Make.com counted it as operations based on how many times data moved between modules, not how many modules I used. My five-step Zapier workflow translated to a Make.com scenario that used about 3-4 operations per execution instead of 5 tasks.

The pricing model reflects this different approach. Make.com's Free tier includes 1,000 operations per month (later bumped to 10,000 in a February 2026 update—a massive advantage for new users). Core tier is $10.59/month for 10,000 operations, Pro is $18.82/month for 20,000 operations, and Teams is $34.12/month for 40,000 operations. You're getting 10-20x more execution capacity at comparable price points.

But Make.com's learning curve is real. My first scenario took me 47 minutes to build, and I screwed up the error handling twice before getting it right. The visual builder is powerful, but it demands you understand how data flows through modules, how to map variables between steps, and how to structure conditional logic using routers and filters.

I spent a full weekend watching Make.com's academy tutorials before I felt confident building scenarios independently. If you're the type who wants to click three buttons and have automation "just work," Make.com will frustrate you. If you're willing to invest 4-6 hours in learning the platform, you'll unlock automation capabilities that Zapier simply can't match at any price point.

One specific example: I built a content repurposing workflow that takes new blog posts, sends them to OpusClip for video snippet generation, routes the results to Predis.ai for social media caption creation, then posts to multiple platforms based on performance criteria. This workflow has 12 modules with conditional branching. In Zapier, it would count as 12+ tasks per execution and require their Professional tier. In Make.com, it runs about 8 operations per execution and fits comfortably in the Core tier.

Integration Library & App Coverage:

  • Zapier: ✓ 6,000+ apps, includes nearly every popular SaaS tool
  • Make.com: ✓ 1,500+ apps, covers all major platforms but gaps exist in niche software
  • Winner: Zapier, but the gap is shrinking quarterly

Pricing & Value for Complex Workflows:

  • Zapier: ✗ Expensive at scale; Professional tier ($73.50/month) required for most businesses
  • Make.com: ✓ Core tier ($10.59/month) handles sophisticated automations; operations model is more efficient
  • Winner: Make.com by a significant margin

Setup Speed for Simple Automations:

  • Zapier: ✓ 5-10 minutes for basic two-app workflows
  • Make.com: ✗ 15-25 minutes for equivalent scenarios as you map data fields manually
  • Winner: Zapier for single-use, straightforward connections

Advanced Features & Data Transformation:

  • Zapier: ✗ Limited conditional logic, text parsing costs extra tasks, no visual data flow
  • Make.com: ✓ Built-in routers, filters, data transformation functions, HTTP request modules, error handling paths
  • Winner: Make.com without question

Learning Curve & Onboarding:

  • Zapier: ✓ Intuitive for non-technical users, minimal training needed
  • Make.com: ✗ Requires understanding of data mapping, module configuration, and flow logic
  • Winner: Zapier for beginners, but Make.com's education resources are solid

Real-World Scenarios: Which Platform Wins Where

Scenario 1: Lead Enrichment & CRM Updates

You're running a content marketing operation. New leads come in through a Beehiiv newsletter signup form. You need to enrich those leads with company data from Apollo.io, check if they match your ideal customer profile criteria, route high-value leads to your sales CRM with priority tagging, and send lower-value leads to a nurture sequence.

In Zapier, this requires multiple Zaps or a complex multi-step Zap with Paths (only available in Professional tier or higher). Each enrichment lookup, each conditional check, each CRM update counts as a separate task. You're looking at 6-8 tasks per lead, minimum.

In Make.com, I built this as a single scenario with routers handling the conditional logic. Lead comes in, HTTP module calls Apollo.io API, router evaluates criteria against your ICP definition, branches send to appropriate destinations. Runs about 4-5 operations per lead. The cost difference at 100 leads per week: Zapier needs Professional tier ($73.50/month), Make.com runs comfortably on Core tier ($10.59/month).

Winner: Make.com

Scenario 2: Quick Social Media Cross-Posting

You publish a new YouTube video and want it automatically shared to Twitter, LinkedIn, and Facebook with the same description and thumbnail. No customization needed, just straightforward cross-posting.

Zapier handles this perfectly. Create one trigger (new YouTube video), add three actions (post to each platform). Five minutes of setup, works reliably. Make.com can do the same thing, but you'll spend extra time mapping the data fields for each platform manually rather than using Zapier's pre-configured templates.

Winner: Zapier

Scenario 3: Content Production Pipeline

You're managing a content workflow that involves Keyword Insights for topic research, NeuronWriter for SEO-optimized drafting, human editing, then publication. You need automation that routes drafts to different editors based on topic category, tracks revision cycles, sends reminders for missed deadlines, and updates your editorial calendar in Airtable.

This is where Make.com's visual flow builder becomes essential. You can see the entire workflow on one canvas—how drafts flow through different approval stages, where the conditional routing happens, which error handling catches edge cases. I built this exact scenario for our content team, and it includes 18 modules with multiple router branches. In Zapier, this would be either impossible or require 3-4 separate Zaps that don't communicate efficiently.

Winner: Make.com decisively

The Migration Experience: What Actually Broke and What Surprised Me

Moving 23 workflows from Zapier to Make.com took three weeks of evening work. Here's what I didn't expect:

The Good: Make.com's Zapier import feature worked for about 60% of my simple workflows. I could export my Zapier configuration and import it directly into Make.com with minimal manual adjustment. This saved probably 10 hours of rebuild time.

The Frustrating: Three of my workflows relied on Zapier-specific apps that didn't have Make.com equivalents. I had to find alternative tools or build custom webhook integrations. One workflow connecting to a legacy project management system required me to use Make.com's HTTP module and read API documentation—not difficult, but definitely slower than clicking through Zapier's pre-built integration.

The Surprising: Once I understood Make.com's data mapping system, I started rebuilding workflows better than they were in Zapier. I discovered I'd been working around Zapier's limitations with inefficient workarounds. For example, I had one Zapier workflow that used Google Sheets as an intermediary database because Zapier couldn't handle the array manipulation I needed. In Make.com, I just used the array aggregator and iterator modules to handle it directly. Cleaner, faster, more reliable.

The operational cost savings hit after month two. My Zapier Professional subscription was $73.50/month. My Make.com Pro subscription is $18.82/month. Even accounting for the one-time migration time investment, I broke even in under three months and I'm now saving about $655 annually while running more sophisticated automations.

The Honest Final Verdict: Make.com for Most Users, But Not Everyone

If you're running a business with more than 10 active automations, or if any of your workflows involve conditional logic, data transformation, or API integrations, Make.com is the better choice in 2026. The pricing advantage is too significant to ignore, and the capabilities gap only widens as your automation needs grow more sophisticated.

The learning curve is real—budget 4-6 hours for training yourself on the platform—but Make.com's academy tutorials and template library make this manageable. You're not learning to code; you're learning to think about automation workflows differently.

Stick with Zapier if you need one of their exclusive integrations that Make.com doesn't support, if you're only running 3-5 very simple automations, or if you're absolutely certain you won't need conditional logic or data transformation in the future. But honestly? That last scenario is rarer than you think. Most users discover new automation opportunities once they start using these platforms, and you don't want to be locked into Zapier's pricing model when that happens.

For solopreneurs, freelancers, and small teams building AI-assisted workflows that combine tools like ElevenLabs for voiceovers, Fireflies.ai for meeting intelligence, and content tools like Predis.ai or OpusClip, Make.com's operations-based pricing means you can run sophisticated multi-step automations without constantly worrying about your task quota.

My recommendation: Start with Make.com's free tier (10,000 operations is genuinely useful), spend a weekend with their academy tutorials, and build your first three scenarios. If you hit a genuine technical limitation or discover you absolutely need a Zapier-exclusive integration, you can always switch. But my experience suggests most users never look back.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I run Zapier and Make.com simultaneously during migration?

Yes, and I recommend it. Keep your critical Zapier workflows running while you test Make.com equivalents. I ran both platforms in parallel for two weeks to ensure nothing broke before canceling my Zapier subscription. Just make sure you're not creating duplicate actions—use filters to ensure only one platform is actively executing.

Does Make.com have mobile app access for monitoring workflows?

Make.com offers a mobile app for iOS and Android as of early 2026, but it's primarily for monitoring and basic edits. Building complex scenarios really requires desktop access. Zapier's mobile app is slightly more functional for quick edits, but neither platform is designed for mobile-first workflow building.
