Manually copying the same data between five different apps every single day? Make.com Is the Fastest Fix in 2026

August 23, 2026 7 min read By Papabenj · My AI Tools Finder

An operations manager at a 12-person marketing agency logs into their CRM at 9 AM, copies a new client's contact info, pastes it into their project management tool, then into their invoicing software, then into their team calendar, then into their reporting dashboard. By the time they're done with just three new clients, 45 minutes have evaporated. This happens every single day.

If you're an agency owner, operations manager, or part of a team that juggles multiple platforms—CRM, project management, invoicing, email marketing, spreadsheets—you know this pain intimately. You're manually ferrying the same data between five, six, sometimes seven different apps because none of them talk to each other. A new lead comes in through your contact form, and you're copying their email address, company name, and project details across every system that needs it. A client approves a proposal, and you're updating the status in three different places. A project milestone hits, and you're logging it everywhere manually.

The math is brutal. If you're spending 30 minutes a day on this data shuttling—and most agencies spend more—that's 2.5 hours per week. Over a year, that's 130 hours of pure repetitive busywork. For a team of three people all doing versions of this, you're burning nearly 400 hours annually just copying and pasting information that already exists in your systems. At a conservative hourly rate of $50, that's $20,000 in wasted labor. And that's before you count the errors: the client detail that didn't get copied over, the invoice that shows the wrong project name, the report with outdated numbers because someone forgot to update the spreadsheet.

This isn't a minor workflow hiccup. It's a systematic drain on your team's time, accuracy, and sanity. The work feels mindless because it is mindless—computers were invented to handle exactly this kind of repetitive data movement, yet here you are in 2026, still doing it by hand.

Why the Obvious Fixes Don't Work

Most teams try the same handful of workarounds, and all of them have serious limitations. The first instinct is usually to hire someone specifically to handle data entry—a junior ops person or virtual assistant whose job is basically to be a human copy-paste machine. This works in the sense that it gets the task off your plate, but you're still paying someone $15-25 per hour to do something a computer should handle automatically. You've outsourced the pain, not eliminated it.

Others try to consolidate tools—"if we just use one platform for everything, the problem goes away." But all-in-one platforms are rarely good at everything. Your CRM might have project management features, but they're clunky compared to your dedicated PM tool. Your project manager might have invoicing, but it doesn't integrate with your accounting software. You end up either sacrificing functionality or still using multiple tools anyway.

Then there's the "just be more careful" approach—create checklists, standard operating procedures, reminder systems to make sure everyone updates every platform. This might reduce errors slightly, but it doesn't reduce the time cost at all. In fact, adding more process overhead often makes the problem worse. Your team is still manually copying data; they're just doing it with more steps now.

What Changes Once This Is Solved

Imagine this instead: A new lead fills out your contact form. Within 60 seconds, their information automatically appears in your CRM with the correct tags, a project gets created in your project management tool with the right template and team members assigned, a row gets added to your master tracking spreadsheet, and a personalized welcome email goes out. You didn't touch any of it. You wake up to find yesterday's work already organized across all your systems, exactly where it needs to be.

When a client approves a proposal in your document system, the deal status updates in your CRM, the invoice generates and sends automatically, the project kickoff tasks get created, and your team gets a Slack notification—all in the span of a minute. Zero manual steps. Zero chance of forgetting to update a platform. Zero time spent switching between browser tabs to copy the same client name five times.

Your team gets back 10-15 hours per week, collectively. That's time they can spend on actual client work, strategy, relationship-building—the things you actually get paid for. Your error rate drops to near-zero because humans aren't transcribing data anymore. Your reporting becomes trustworthy because every system is working from the same real-time information. And you stop losing opportunities because someone forgot to log a lead in the CRM or didn't see the project deadline buried in another tool.

Exactly How to Use It to Solve This

Let's walk through building an automation that eliminates one of the most common daily tasks: syncing new clients from your CRM into your project management tool, invoicing system, and team spreadsheet.

Step 1: Set Up Your Trigger

Log into Make.com and create a new scenario. Select your CRM as the trigger app—let's say you're using HubSpot. Choose "Watch Deals" as the trigger module, and set it to watch for deals with status "Closed Won." Make.com will now check your HubSpot account every few minutes (or instantly, on higher-tier plans) for any deals that just moved to that status. When it finds one, it pulls all the deal data: client name, contact email, deal value, project type, close date—everything attached to that record.

Step 2: Create the Project Management Record

Add a second module and select your project management tool—Asana, ClickUp, Monday, whatever you use. Choose "Create a Task" or "Create a Project" depending on your setup. Map the fields: HubSpot's "Company Name" goes into Asana's project name field, HubSpot's "Deal Value" goes into a custom field for budget, HubSpot's "Close Date" gets used to calculate your project start date. You can set a specific project template, assign team members based on the project type, and add tags automatically. When this module runs, a fully configured project appears in Asana without anyone touching it.

Step 3: Generate the Invoice

Add a third module for your invoicing software—QuickBooks, FreshBooks, or similar. Choose "Create an Invoice" and map the client details from HubSpot: customer name, email, invoice amount (pulled from the deal value), due date (calculated from your payment terms), line items (you can set these statically or pull them from custom fields in HubSpot). The invoice gets created and can be set to send automatically or save as a draft for review.

Step 4: Update Your Master Spreadsheet

Add a fourth module for Google Sheets or Airtable. Choose "Add a Row" and map the fields again: client name, project type, deal value, start date, assigned team members, current status. This gives you a centralized view of all active clients without manually updating a spreadsheet every time a deal closes.

Step 5: Notify Your Team

Add a fifth module for Slack. Choose "Create a Message" and send it to your projects channel. The message can say something like: "New project started: [Company Name] - [Project Type] - $[Deal Value]. Assigned to [Team Member]. Asana project: [link]." Your team knows immediately that there's new work, who's responsible, and where to find the details.

Real Example: A digital marketing agency sets this up with HubSpot, ClickUp, QuickBooks, Google Sheets, and Slack. When a $15,000 website redesign deal closes in HubSpot, Make.com creates a ClickUp project called "ABC Corp - Website Redesign" with 23 pre-loaded tasks based on their website template, generates a QuickBooks invoice for $15,000 due in 30 days, adds a row to their "Active Projects Q3 2026" spreadsheet showing ABC Corp with a start date of August 25 and project manager Jessica Lee, and posts a Slack message in #new-projects. Total time elapsed: 90 seconds. Manual effort required: zero.

Once you've built this scenario, click "Turn On" and it runs automatically from that point forward. Every single new closed deal triggers the entire chain. You can duplicate this scenario and modify it for other workflows—new leads from forms, project milestone completions, payment receipts, anything that involves moving the same data across multiple systems.
