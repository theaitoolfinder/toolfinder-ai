Make.com Review (2026): Is It Actually Worth Paying For?

June 10, 2026 8 min read By Alex Rivera · MyAI ToolsFinder

You used to spend 3 hours every week copying leads from LinkedIn to your CRM, manually sending Slack notifications when deals closed, and updating spreadsheets across five different apps. Now you click one button, and Make.com runs 47 automated steps in 8 seconds while you're making coffee.

That's the promise. But after testing Make.com for six months on client projects, running over 200,000 operations, and paying for three different pricing tiers, I can tell you exactly when it's worth the money and when you're better off sticking with something simpler. This isn't a theoretical review—I'm going to show you the actual automations I built, what broke, what worked brilliantly, and whether the pricing makes sense for your specific situation.

What Make.com Actually Does (And Why It Exists)

Make.com is a visual automation platform that connects your apps and moves data between them without code. It was built by a Czech company called Celonis in 2012 (originally called Integromat before rebranding in 2021), specifically targeting people who found Zapier too limiting but didn't want to hire developers.

The core difference: while Zapier uses a linear "if this, then that" model, Make.com gives you a visual canvas where you can build complex workflows with branches, routers, filters, and parallel paths. You can process data in loops, transform JSON objects, handle errors differently based on conditions, and run multiple scenarios simultaneously.

Current pricing as of June 2026: Free tier gives you 1,000 operations per month. The Core plan is $10.59/month for 10,000 operations. Pro starts at $18.82/month for 10,000 operations plus premium apps and features. Enterprise is custom pricing. Operations are counted every time a module (action) runs—so one automation with 5 steps uses 5 operations.

The visual editor looks like a flowchart. You drag modules (which are individual actions like "Get Email" or "Update Row") onto a canvas, connect them with lines, and configure each step. It supports over 1,500 apps including every major platform: Gmail, Slack, Airtable, Google Sheets, Notion AI, HubSpot, and hundreds more.

The One Thing Make.com Does Better Than Everything Else

The killer feature is the Iterator and Aggregator combo—and it's not even close. This is what lets you process bulk data in ways that would cost you hundreds of dollars per month on Zapier or require actual programming.

Here's how it works: An Iterator takes a bundle of data (like 50 rows from a spreadsheet) and processes each item individually through whatever steps you want. Then an Aggregator collects all those processed items back into one bundle. Between them, you can add routers that send different items down different paths based on conditions.

Real example: I built an automation for a content agency that pulls new articles from Beehiiv each morning, extracts the main topics using GPT-4, checks if those topics match their client list in Airtable, routes matching articles to specific Slack channels for each client, and compiles a summary email. Without Iterator/Aggregator, you'd need to process articles one at a time (slow and expensive) or hire a developer to write custom code.

Before Make.com: The agency owner manually reviewed 30-40 industry articles every morning, spent 45 minutes tagging which clients might care, and sent individual Slack messages. After: 4 minutes of automated processing, zero manual work, and clients get relevant content within 15 minutes of publication. The automation uses about 180 operations per day (6 operations per article × 30 articles) which costs roughly $0.20 on the Core plan.

Every Feature That Actually Matters

Visual Scenario Builder: The drag-and-drop canvas is genuinely intuitive once you understand the logic. You can see your entire automation at a glance, which makes debugging infinitely easier than text-based tools. The downside: complex scenarios get visually messy fast. I have one automation with 34 modules that's basically impossible to navigate without zooming in and out constantly.

Data Mapping: Every module shows you exactly what data came from previous steps, and you can click to insert variables instead of typing them. It supports advanced mapping with functions—you can format dates, manipulate text, do math, parse JSON, and more. This is more powerful than Zapier's formatter, but the learning curve is steeper. Expect to spend 2-3 hours learning the function syntax.

Error Handling: You can add error handlers to individual modules that trigger different actions when something fails. I use this to send myself a Slack message when API calls fail, then retry with different parameters. Most automation tools just stop and email you a generic error. Make.com lets you build resilience directly into the workflow.

Scheduling Options: Run scenarios on intervals (every 15 minutes, hourly, daily), on specific schedules (weekdays at 9am), or triggered by webhooks (instant). The free plan limits you to 15-minute intervals. Paid plans go down to 1 minute. One gotcha: scheduled scenarios count as one operation per run even if they find no new data, which can burn through your operation limit if you're not careful.

Data Stores: Built-in databases that let you save information between automation runs. I use these to track which leads I've already processed so I don't send duplicate emails. They're simple key-value stores, not full databases—fine for flags and small datasets, but don't try to build a CRM in them. Included in Pro and higher plans.

HTTP/Webhook Modules: Make API calls to any service, even if Make.com doesn't have a pre-built integration. This is how I connected Make.com to Cursor (our development tool) and to custom internal tools. You need to understand REST APIs and authentication, but it opens up literally unlimited possibilities. This feature alone justifies the price for technical users.

Team Collaboration: Share scenarios with team members, set permissions, and track who changed what. Available on Pro and Enterprise. The version history is basic—it shows who edited and when, but doesn't let you easily roll back to previous versions. You'll want to manually duplicate scenarios before major changes.

I Ran These Exact Tests So You Don't Have To

Test 1: Client Onboarding Automation — I built a scenario that triggers when someone fills out a Typeform, creates a client folder in Google Drive, adds them to our CRM (Airtable), sends a welcome email through Gmail, schedules a kickoff meeting via Reclaim AI, and posts a notification in Slack. Input: one form submission. Output: 6 actions completed in 12 seconds using 7 operations. Manual process took 15-20 minutes per client. Over 30 clients per month, that's 8+ hours saved. Cost: about 210 operations monthly ($0.22 on Core plan).

What worked: The Google Drive folder creation was flawless, including nested subfolders with specific naming conventions. The Airtable integration pulled related records automatically. What didn't: Reclaim AI's Make.com module is limited compared to their native API—I could schedule meetings but couldn't set buffer times or mark them as flexible. Had to use the HTTP module instead, which took 3 hours to configure.

Test 2: Content Repurposing Pipeline — Every time I publish an article in Notion AI, Make.com extracts key quotes, generates 5 social media posts using GPT-4, creates short-form video scripts for OpusClip, saves everything to a content calendar in Airtable, and sends the scripts to our video editor via Slack. Input: one published article. Output: 15+ content assets in about 90 seconds. This scenario uses 23 operations per article.

The GPT-4 integration is native and works beautifully—you can set temperature, max tokens, and system prompts directly in the module. The output quality is identical to using ChatGPT directly. The limitation: you need your own OpenAI API key (not included in Make.com pricing), and you're charged per token on top of your Make.com subscription. For my usage, that's about $12/month in OpenAI costs.

Test 3: Lead Enrichment From Multiple Sources — When a new lead comes into our system, Make.com looks them up in Clearbit, checks if their domain uses any of 12 specific tools by hitting their website, searches for their LinkedIn profile, scores them based on 8 criteria, and routes high-value leads to a different Slack channel with an @mention for immediate follow-up. This one pushed Make.com's limits. The scenario uses 19 modules and needs Iterator/Aggregator to check multiple tools. Processing one lead takes 8-12 seconds and uses 19 operations.

What impressed me: Make.com handled the parallel API calls gracefully, and the error handling caught when websites timed out. What frustrated me: The scenario failed 3 times before I got the logic right, and debugging required manually running it while watching each module's output. The built-in debugger is good but not great—you can't set breakpoints or step through slowly.

Who Should Actually Pay For This

The data-heavy freelancer: If you're managing multiple clients and moving information between more than 5 apps regularly, Make.com pays for itself immediately. I'm thinking specifically of social media managers who pull content from Beehiiv, schedule to Buffer, track analytics in Google Sheets, and report to clients in Slack. The Core plan ($10.59) replaces 4-6 hours of manual work per week.

The agency owner who's drowning in process work: Client onboarding, status updates, report generation, lead qualification—these are all perfect for Make.com. One agency I consulted for automated 23 different workflows and calculated they saved 31 hours per week across a 4-person team. They're on the Pro plan ($18.82/month) and use about 65,000 operations monthly. That's $0.0003 per operation—absurdly cheap compared to hiring another person.

The creator building a one-person business: If you're publishing content, managing a newsletter, selling digital products, and trying to maintain a social presence, you need automation or you need to sleep less. Make.com connects your entire stack. The specific use case: you write in Notion AI, publish to your blog, send to your Beehiiv newsletter, extract quotes for social, repurpose with OpusClip, and track everything in one dashboard. Core plan handles this easily.

The technical product person who needs custom integrations: If you understand APIs and need to connect internal tools or services without Make.com integrations, the HTTP module is ridiculously powerful. I've used it to connect to Fireflies.ai's API for meeting transcripts, Keyword Insights for SEO data, and half a dozen internal tools. This is cheaper than building custom integrations or paying for enterprise Zapier.

The Stuff That Genuinely Doesn't Work Well

The documentation is inconsistent. Some modules have detailed examples and video tutorials. Others have two sentences and expect you to figure it out. I spent 4 hours learning how the Google Sheets "Update a Row" module works because the docs didn't explain that you need to map the Row ID separately from the data.

The app integration quality varies wildly. Gmail, Slack, Airtable, and Google Sheets work perfectly. Newer or smaller apps often have modules with limited functionality compared to their native APIs. I've had to use HTTP modules for about 30% of the apps I wanted to connect because the official integration was missing key features.

Billing transparency is poor. You can see your total operations used, but tracking which scenarios are burning through your limit requires manually checking each one. I went over my operation limit twice before I realized one poorly-configured scenario was running every minute checking for new data that only came once per day. A usage dashboard by scenario would solve this immediately.

The mobile experience is non-existent. You can't build or edit scenarios on mobile. You can't even easily check if automations are running. If something breaks over the weekend and you're away from your computer, you're stuck. At minimum, they need a monitoring app that shows scenario status and lets you pause/resume.

Customer support on lower tiers is email-only with 24-48 hour response times. When an automation breaks and it's blocking client work, that's unacceptable. Pro plan gets priority support but still no phone or live chat. Enterprise gets a dedicated contact, but you're paying thousands per month at that point.

Breaking Down What Each Pricing Tier Actually Gets You

The Free tier (1,000 operations/month) is genuinely useful, not a teaser. You can run 2-3 simple automations on 15-minute intervals. Perfect for testing if Make.com fits your brain before paying. But you'll hit the limit fast if you actually use it. One automation checking email every 15 minutes uses 2,880 operations per month (96 checks/day × 30 days) before it does anything with those emails.

Core ($10.59/month, 10,000 operations) is the sweet spot for solopreneurs and freelancers. That's enough for 5-8 moderately complex automations running throughout the day. You get 15-minute minimum intervals and access to all standard apps. You don't get data stores, team features, or priority support. If you're just connecting apps and moving data, this is all you need.

Pro ($18.82/month, 10,000 operations) adds premium apps (Salesforce, SAP, some analytics tools), data stores, team collaboration, 1-minute intervals, and priority support. Worth upgrading if you need any of those specific features. The operation limit is the same as Core, which seems odd—you're paying $8 more for features, not capacity. You'd upgrade to Pro for capabilities, then buy operation add-ons if you need volume.

Enterprise (custom pricing) is for companies running business-critical automations at scale. You get dedicated infrastructure, custom operation limits, SLA guarantees, and actual support. If you're processing 500,000+ operations monthly or need guaranteed uptime, this is your only option. Pricing starts around $299/month from what I've heard from other users.

Operation add-ons are available: 10,000 additional operations for $9. This is where costs can creep up. If you're consistently using 30,000 operations monthly, you're paying $10.59 + $18 in add-ons = $28.59. At that point, you might want Enterprise pricing or to optimize your scenarios to use fewer operations.

How It Stacks Up Against What Else Is Out There

Zapier is simpler and has more app integrations (7,000+ vs Make.com's 1,500+). If you need linear "trigger → action" automations and don't want to learn visual logic, Zapier is still the right choice. But Zapier's pricing is worse for complex automations—processing arrays or loops requires Formatter steps that count as tasks, and you'll hit pricing tiers faster. Zapier starts at $29.99/month for 750 tasks. Make.com gives you 10,000 operations for $10.59. If you're doing data transformation or multi-step logic, Make.com is 5-10x cheaper. I wrote a detailed comparison earlier this week, but the short version: Zapier for simplicity, Make.com for complexity and cost.

n8n is the open-source alternative. You can self-host it for free if you have technical skills. It's more powerful than Make.com in some ways (you can write JavaScript directly in nodes), but you're managing servers, updates, security, and uptime yourself. I tested n8n for 2 months before switching to Make.com. The $10/month was worth not dealing with server maintenance and debugging deployment issues. If you're a developer building automations for your own SaaS product, n8n makes sense. If you're a freelancer automating client work, pay for Make.com.
