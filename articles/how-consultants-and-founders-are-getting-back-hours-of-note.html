How Consultants and Founders Are Getting Back hours of note-taking and re-explaining With Fireflies.ai

August 20, 2026 7 min read By Papabenj · My AI Tools Finder

You just wrapped a 45-minute strategy call with a high-value client. They outlined three new priorities, referenced a conversation from two months ago you barely remember, and asked you to "circle back on that framework we discussed." You smiled, nodded, and said you'd send over next steps by end of day. The moment the call ends, panic sets in. What exactly did they say about budget? Which deliverable did they want first? And what the hell was that framework they mentioned?

You're a consultant or founder managing multiple client relationships simultaneously, and every single call contains critical details that directly impact project success, billing accuracy, and client satisfaction. But there's no way to absorb everything in real-time while also actively listening, asking smart questions, and building rapport. So you're left frantically typing incomplete notes during calls—which makes you look distracted—or trying to reconstruct conversations from memory afterward, which means you're spending another 20-30 minutes per call just documenting what was said.

Then comes the re-explaining. Because your team wasn't on that call, you now have to brief your designer on what the client actually wants, remind your developer about the technical constraints mentioned, and forward context to your VA so they can schedule the right follow-up. Each client call spawns three more internal conversations, all because the original information lives only in your fragmented notes or unreliable memory.

Add it up across a week: if you're doing eight client calls, that's 2-4 hours of post-call note cleanup and another 2-3 hours re-explaining context to your team. That's nearly a full workday every week spent on administrative cleanup of conversations that already happened. For consultants billing $150-300 per hour, that's $750-$1,800 in lost billable time weekly. For founders, it's six hours that could have gone to product development, sales, or literally anything that moves the business forward.

The cost isn't just time. It's the client who feels unheard when you ask them to repeat something they already told you. It's the project delay because your team started building the wrong thing based on your incomplete notes. It's the nagging Sunday evening anxiety knowing you have four sets of call notes to clean up before Monday morning.

Why the Obvious Fixes Don't Work

Most consultants and founders try the same workarounds. You tell yourself you'll just get better at note-taking during calls. So you open a Google Doc and attempt to type while listening, which means you're now splitting attention between what the client is saying and what your fingers are doing. You miss the nuance in their tone when they express hesitation about timeline. You don't catch the subtle shift when they mention a stakeholder who hasn't been looped in yet. Your notes capture words, but miss meaning.

Some people try recording calls manually and listening back later. This technically works, but now you're spending 45 minutes re-listening to a 45-minute call just to extract the five critical decisions that were made. You've doubled your time investment instead of reducing it.

Hiring a VA to join calls and take notes sounds promising until you realize you're now paying someone $25-50 per hour to sit silently on Zoom, and they still don't know your business well enough to identify which details actually matter. They write down everything, which means you're still sifting through pages of transcription looking for the actionable items.

The "just remember better" approach fails because human memory is unreliable by design, especially when you're juggling six active client projects. The manual recording approach fails because it trades one time-sink for another. The VA solution fails because it adds cost without actually solving the core problem: transforming conversation into usable, searchable, shareable information without manual effort.

What Changes Once This Is Solved

Imagine finishing a client call and having complete, accurate notes waiting for you within three minutes—including action items automatically extracted, key decisions highlighted, and the entire conversation searchable by keyword. You didn't take notes during the call. You didn't ask anyone else to. You were fully present, reading the client's energy, asking better questions, building the relationship that actually retains business.

Your designer opens a shared link and sees exactly what the client said about brand guidelines—verbatim quote, timestamp included. Your developer searches "API" in the transcript and jumps straight to the two-minute technical discussion that matters to them. Nobody asks you to re-explain. Nobody builds the wrong thing because of secondhand information loss.

You get back those six hours per week. For consultants, that's 24 billable hours per month—potentially $3,600-$7,200 in recovered revenue monthly. For founders, it's time redirected to revenue-generating activities: sales calls, product decisions, strategic planning. The cognitive load drops immediately. No more Sunday evening note-cleanup sessions. No more mid-call anxiety about whether you're capturing everything. No more "can you remind me what we discussed" emails that make you look unprepared.

Client relationships actually improve because you remember everything they told you, reference previous conversations accurately, and follow through on commitments they mentioned once, three weeks ago. That's the business impact of solving this problem completely.

Exactly How to Use It to Solve This

Step 1: Connect Your Calendar and Meeting Platforms
Sign up at Fireflies.ai and connect your Google Calendar, Outlook, or whichever calendar system holds your client meetings. Then authorize Fireflies to access your Zoom, Google Meet, or Microsoft Teams account. This takes about three minutes. Once connected, Fireflies automatically detects upcoming meetings on your calendar and joins them as a participant (it shows up as "Fireflies Notetaker" in the participant list—you can customize the name in settings).

Step 2: Let It Join Your Next Client Call
You don't need to do anything. Fireflies joins automatically when the meeting starts, begins recording and transcribing in real time. During the call, you're free to be fully present—no laptop typing, no frantic scribbling. If you're on video, you're making eye contact. If you're walking and talking on your phone, you're walking and talking. Fireflies is capturing everything in the background.

Step 3: Review the AI Summary and Transcript
Within 2-5 minutes after your call ends, Fireflies emails you a summary and makes the full meeting record available in your dashboard. The summary includes an overview paragraph, bulleted action items (with timestamps linking to the exact moment each was mentioned), key questions asked during the call, and topic threads. The full transcript is searchable, speaker-labeled, and includes timestamps every few seconds so you can jump to any moment.

Step 4: Share Relevant Sections With Your Team
Instead of writing a briefing email to your team, you create a Soundbite—a highlighted clip from the transcript—of the two-minute section where the client explained their design preferences. You share that link in Slack with your designer. You create another Soundbite of the technical requirements discussion and drop it in your developer's project channel. Each person gets exactly the context they need, in the client's own words, with zero summarization loss from you playing telephone.

The client mentions budget ($40K-$50K range), timeline (needs assets by Q4 launch), three competitor brands they admire, and one they actively want to differentiate from. They also reference "that minimalist direction we talked about in the intro call"—a call that happened three weeks ago. Fireflies captures all of it. "But What If…" — Answering the Real Objections

Objection 1: "Will clients be uncomfortable with a bot joining calls?"
In practice, this rarely becomes an issue if you handle it professionally. Mention it upfront: "I use an AI notetaker so I can be fully present with you instead of typing—it'll join as Fireflies Notetaker." Most clients appreciate that you're prioritizing attention over multitasking. If a client requests no recording for a sensitive discussion, you can easily exclude specific meetings. Fireflies only joins meetings on your connected calendar, so you maintain full control. By mid-2026, AI notetakers have become standard in professional services—clients expect them.

Objection 2: "I'm not great with new software—will this be complicated to learn?"
Fireflies requires almost zero learning curve. You authorize it once, and it works automatically from that point forward. The interface is simpler than Google Docs—transcripts appear, you click to read them or search them, done. If you can use email, you can use Fireflies. There's no training required, no manual workflow to remember. It's genuinely set-and-forget.

Objection 3: "What if it misses important details or gets the transcription wrong?"
Fireflies transcription accuracy sits above 90% for clear audio with decent microphones, comparable to human transcription services and notably better than your real-time note-taking during a call while also trying to engage. Speaker labels occasionally mix up if two people sound very similar, but you can correct labels in the dashboard. The AI summary feature highlights multiple redundant action items, so even if one extraction misses something, you'll catch it in the full transcript, which is searchable. The point isn't perfection—it's that Fireflies captures 95%+ of what was said, versus the 60-70% you'd capture manually while distracted by typing.
