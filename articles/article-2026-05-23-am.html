Zapier vs Make.com: The Honest 2026 Verdict

May 23, 2026 8 min read By Papabenj · My AI Tools Finder

Both Zapier and Make.com promise to automate your workflow without writing code. Both deliver on that promise — but for very different kinds of users. After building real automations on both platforms, here's the unvarnished comparison that actually helps you pick.

The Quick Answer

Choose Zapier if: you need to connect apps fast, you're not technical, and your automations are simple (trigger → one or two actions). Choose Make.com if: you need complex multi-step logic, you're cost-conscious, or you process high volumes. The pricing alone often makes the decision — Make gives you 10,000 operations per month for $9, while Zapier's $19.99 starter plan caps at 750 tasks.

Where Zapier Genuinely Wins

App coverage is not close. With 7,000+ integrations, Zapier connects to tools that Make.com hasn't built yet — especially niche SaaS tools, legacy CRMs, and new apps that launch Zapier integrations on day one. If your workflow relies on a specific tool, check Zapier first.

Setup speed. The linear step editor is genuinely faster to learn. A non-technical founder can build a working Zap in under 10 minutes. Make's visual canvas is more powerful, but also has a steeper learning curve. If you just need email → spreadsheet → Slack notification, Zapier ships it faster.

AI-powered Zap builder. Type in plain English what you want ("When I get a new lead in HubSpot, add them to my Mailchimp list and send a Slack alert") and Zapier drafts the Zap automatically. This alone can save hours if you're building automations frequently.

Where Make.com Clearly Beats Zapier

Price-to-volume ratio. Make's $9/month Starter gives you 10,000 operations — roughly 13× more than Zapier's $19.99 plan per dollar spent. For any workflow that runs frequently (hourly syncs, form submissions, e-commerce orders), the math favors Make by a wide margin.

Complex logic. Make's visual canvas was built for branching workflows. Conditional routes, error handlers, iterators over arrays, aggregators — these are awkward in Zapier's linear model but feel natural in Make. If your automation has more than 5 steps or needs IF/ELSE branching, Make is the better tool.

Real-time execution. Even on free plans, Make.com scenarios can run near-instantly when triggered. Zapier's free tier has a polling delay of 2–15 minutes. For time-sensitive workflows (customer support alerts, payment confirmations), this matters.

Real Workflow Examples: Which to Use

  • "New Typeform submission → add row to Google Sheets → send Slack message" → Either works equally well. Zapier is faster to set up; Make is cheaper to run.
  • "Parse PDF invoice → extract line items → create QuickBooks entry → email receipt" → Make.com. The multi-step data transformation with conditional logic is exactly what its canvas handles well.
  • "Sync new Shopify customers to 4 different tools" → Zapier if those tools include obscure integrations. Make if cost efficiency matters most.
  • "Monitor RSS feeds → summarize with AI → post to LinkedIn" → Make.com, using its native OpenAI module. Clean, affordable, runs on a schedule without chewing through tasks.

The Hidden Costs to Watch

Zapier's task counting can be aggressive. Multi-step Zaps consume one task per action step, not per trigger. A 5-step Zap processing 200 records uses 1,000 tasks — quickly burning through a Starter plan. Make counts "operations" differently: each module execution is one operation, but it's more predictable.

Make.com's learning curve has a real time cost. Budget 2–3 hours to get comfortable with the canvas and module system before you're productive. Zapier's onboarding is genuinely faster for simple use cases.

Verdict for Solopreneurs

If you're running a lean solo or small-team operation and you're doing it on a budget, Make.com is the better default. You get more volume for less money, the AI modules are built-in, and once you learn the canvas it's more powerful. Start on the free tier — 1,000 ops covers most light workflows.

Switch to Zapier when you hit a specific app that Make doesn't integrate with, or when you need to move fast and your workflows are genuinely simple. The two aren't mutually exclusive — many teams run Zapier for simple connective tissue and Make.com for their complex, high-volume pipelines.
