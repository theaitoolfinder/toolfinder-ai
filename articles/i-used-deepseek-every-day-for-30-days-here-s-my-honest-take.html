I Used DeepSeek Every Day for 30 Days — Here's My Honest Take

June 20, 2026 8 min read By Papabenj · My AI Tools Finder

Six months ago, researching a technical topic for a client report took me four hours of tabbed chaos—bouncing between Google Scholar, Reddit threads, ChatGPT, and three different paywalled journals. Last Tuesday, I fed the same type of query into DeepSeek and had a fully cited, nuanced answer in 11 minutes. That's not hyperbole. I timed it.

I decided to test whether that experience was a fluke or the norm, so I committed to using DeepSeek as my primary AI assistant for every single workday in May 2026. No cherry-picking tasks. No falling back to ChatGPT when things got hard. Just me, DeepSeek, and 30 days of real-world work—from debugging Python scripts to drafting investor emails to building content outlines for clients.

Here's what I learned, what broke, and whether this tool actually deserves a spot in your daily workflow.

What DeepSeek Actually Does (And Why It Exists)

DeepSeek is a large language model developed by DeepSeek AI, a Chinese research lab that's been quietly outperforming Western models on specific benchmarks since late 2024. Unlike ChatGPT or Claude, which are built for broad consumer appeal, DeepSeek was designed with a technical bias—it excels at math, code, structured reasoning, and research-heavy tasks where precision matters more than personality.

The interface is bare-bones: a chat window, model selector, and temperature slider. No flashy integrations. No "magic writing modes." It's built for people who know what they want from an LLM and don't need hand-holding.

Pricing is refreshingly straightforward. The free tier gives you 50 queries per day on DeepSeek-V2, their standard model. The Pro plan ($20/month) unlocks unlimited queries, access to DeepSeek-V3 (their reasoning-optimized model), API access, and priority speed during peak hours. There's also an Enterprise tier ($99/month) with team collaboration features and usage analytics, but that's overkill unless you're running a dev team.

What surprised me most: DeepSeek doesn't gatekeep features. The free tier is genuinely usable for real work, not just a teaser to push you toward paid plans.

The One Thing DeepSeek Does Better Than Anything Else

Chain-of-thought reasoning on technical problems. Full stop.

Most LLMs give you an answer. DeepSeek shows you how it arrived at that answer, step by step, with visible intermediate reasoning. This matters enormously when you're debugging code, validating research claims, or checking math.

Here's a concrete example: I asked DeepSeek to help me optimize a Python function that was processing 10,000 CSV rows in 14 seconds. I pasted the code. Instead of just spitting out a refactored version, DeepSeek walked me through why the bottleneck existed (nested loops inside a pandas apply function), explained three different optimization strategies with tradeoffs, then generated refactored code with inline comments explaining each change.

The refactored version ran in 1.8 seconds. When I asked ChatGPT the same question for comparison, it gave me cleaner code that ran in 3.2 seconds—faster, but not nearly as optimized, and with zero explanation of why the changes worked.

This chain-of-thought approach isn't just useful for code. When I asked DeepSeek to fact-check a claim about AI adoption rates in legal firms, it broke down its verification process: identified the original source, cross-referenced with two other datasets, flagged a discrepancy in how "adoption" was measured, and then gave me a revised answer with confidence intervals. That's not something I've seen from Claude, Gemini, or ChatGPT with the same consistency.

Every Feature That Actually Matters

Model Switching: You can toggle between DeepSeek-V2 (faster, cheaper) and DeepSeek-V3 (slower, deeper reasoning) mid-conversation. V3 is noticeably better for logic puzzles, math proofs, and multi-step technical tasks. V2 handles everyday queries just fine. I used V2 about 70% of the time and only switched to V3 when I hit a wall.

Code Execution: DeepSeek can run Python code directly in the chat and show you the output. This is huge for testing snippets without switching to a local IDE. I used this daily for validating regex patterns, testing API calls, and prototyping data transformations. It's not as polished as GitHub Copilot's inline suggestions, but it's faster than copy-pasting into a Jupyter notebook.

Citation Mode: When you enable citation mode, DeepSeek includes inline references for factual claims. These aren't always perfect—I caught two instances where it cited a source that didn't quite support the claim—but it's far better than the hallucination-prone "trust me bro" answers you get from most LLMs. For research tasks, this feature alone justified my subscription.

Temperature Control: You can manually adjust how "creative" versus "deterministic" the model is. I kept it at 0.3 (low creativity, high consistency) for technical work and cranked it to 0.8 when brainstorming blog headlines or email subject lines. This level of control is standard in APIs but rare in consumer chat interfaces.

Export Options: You can export entire conversations as Markdown or JSON. This sounds trivial until you're trying to save a 40-turn debugging session for documentation. I exported three conversations and dropped them directly into NeuronWriter to build SEO content briefs—worked flawlessly.

API Access: The Pro plan includes API credits. I used Make.com to build a workflow that ingests customer support tickets, routes them to DeepSeek for initial triage, then drafts response templates. It's not as fast as OpenAI's API, but it's 40% cheaper and the output quality for structured tasks is comparable.

Multi-Language Support: DeepSeek handles Chinese, English, and code-switching between the two better than any Western model I've tested. If you work with bilingual content or international teams, this is a quiet superpower.

Three Real Tasks I Ran Through DeepSeek (With Receipts)

Task 1: Building a content calendar for a B2B SaaS client. I gave DeepSeek the client's target keywords, audience persona, and competitor blog URLs. It generated 12 article titles, mapped them to buyer journey stages, suggested internal linking opportunities, and estimated search volume for each topic. Time: 9 minutes. When I ran the same prompt through ChatGPT, I got decent titles but zero strategic structure. I cross-referenced DeepSeek's keyword estimates with Keyword Insights—they were within 15% accuracy, which is impressive for an LLM.

Task 2: Debugging a webhook integration that was dropping 30% of incoming events. I pasted my Node.js webhook handler and error logs. DeepSeek identified the issue (async race condition in event acknowledgment), explained why it caused dropped events under load, and rewrote the handler with proper async/await patterns. I deployed the fix and monitoring dropped to under 2% failure rate. Total debug time: 22 minutes, including deployment. For context, this same issue took me three hours to solve two months ago using Stack Overflow and trial-and-error.

Task 3: Summarizing five academic papers on transformer model efficiency for a research memo. I fed DeepSeek the abstracts and asked for a comparison table highlighting methodology, results, and limitations. It delivered a clean Markdown table, flagged two conflicting findings between studies, and suggested a follow-up question about dataset bias. I checked the summaries against the full PDFs—accurate on all five. Time saved versus reading all five papers myself: roughly 90 minutes.

Who Should Be Using This Right Now

If you're a developer writing or debugging code daily: DeepSeek's code execution, reasoning transparency, and Python-first bias make it better than ChatGPT for technical problem-solving. Pair it with Cursor for inline suggestions and you've got a complete AI coding stack.

If you're a researcher, analyst, or consultant who needs cited, verifiable answers: The citation mode and chain-of-thought reasoning are worth the $20/month alone. I replaced 60% of my Google Scholar sessions with DeepSeek queries during the 30 days.

If you're building AI workflows with no-code tools: The API access at $20/month is a steal compared to OpenAI's pricing. I built three Make.com automations using DeepSeek as the intelligence layer—one for meeting transcription summaries (pulling data from Fireflies.ai), one for social media content repurposing (pairing with Predis.ai), and one for SEO meta descriptions.

If you're a content creator who needs fast, structured ideation: DeepSeek is excellent at generating outlines, taxonomies, and frameworks. I used it to build episode structures for a client's podcast, plan a 12-week email course, and brainstorm 30 LinkedIn post hooks. It's less "creative" than Claude in terms of voice, but far more organized.

What DeepSeek Genuinely Struggles With

Voice and tone. If you need conversational copy, empathetic customer service responses, or punchy marketing headlines, DeepSeek feels robotic. I tried using it to draft a casual welcome email for a newsletter and the output read like a technical manual. Claude or ChatGPT are better for anything where personality matters.

Image generation and multimodal tasks. DeepSeek is text-only. If your workflow involves analyzing screenshots, generating images, or working with video transcripts, you'll need to bolt on other tools like ElevenLabs for voice or OpusClip for video.

Real-time web search. DeepSeek's knowledge cutoff is March 2026, and it doesn't browse the web. For breaking news, live data, or current events, you'll still need to manually feed it context or use a tool like Perplexity.

Speed on complex queries. When I ran DeepSeek-V3 on a multi-step logic puzzle, it took 28 seconds to respond. That's slow compared to ChatGPT's near-instant replies. If you're used to rapid-fire Q&A sessions, the latency on reasoning-heavy tasks will frustrate you.

Is the Pricing Actually Worth It?

The free tier (50 queries/day on V2) is good enough for casual users or people testing the waters. If you're asking 5–10 questions a day, you'll never hit the limit.

The Pro tier ($20/month) makes sense if you're using this for work and need unlimited access, V3 reasoning, or API calls. I burned through 400+ queries in 30 days, so the free tier would've capped me by day 8. Compared to ChatGPT Plus (also $20/month), you're getting better technical reasoning but weaker creative writing and no image generation. It's a trade.

The Enterprise tier ($99/month) is only worth it if you're managing a team and need shared conversation history, usage tracking, or SSO. Solo users should skip it.

One caveat: API costs are metered separately. I spent an extra $12 in API credits running my Make.com workflows, so factor that in if you're planning heavy automation.

How It Stacks Up Against the Obvious Alternatives

DeepSeek vs. ChatGPT: ChatGPT wins on speed, voice, and integrations (plugins, DALL-E, web browsing). DeepSeek wins on reasoning transparency, code quality, and citation accuracy. If you're a developer or researcher, choose DeepSeek. If you're a marketer or generalist, stick with ChatGPT.

DeepSeek vs. Claude: Claude has better conversational flow and is significantly better at long-form writing with personality. DeepSeek beats Claude on math, structured reasoning, and anything requiring step-by-step proof. I'd use Claude for client-facing content and DeepSeek for backend logic.

DeepSeek vs. GitHub Copilot: Copilot is faster for inline code suggestions and integrates directly into your editor. DeepSeek is better for explaining why code works, debugging logic errors, and generating architecture diagrams. Use both—they're complementary, not competitive.

My Honest Verdict After 30 Days

For developers and technical users: 9/10. This is the best reasoning-focused LLM I've used for code and research. The chain-of-thought transparency alone is worth the subscription.

For content creators and marketers: 6/10. It's useful for structure and strategy but weak on voice. You'll still need Claude or ChatGPT for client-facing copy.

For researchers and analysts: 8.5/10. Citation mode and reasoning depth make this indispensable for knowledge work. The lack of real-time web search is the only major gap.

Who should subscribe today: Anyone doing technical problem-solving, research synthesis, or building AI automations. The $20/month pays for itself if you save even two hours a month.

Who should skip it: If your primary use case is creative writing, social media content, or anything requiring real-time information, you're better off with ChatGPT or Claude. DeepSeek isn't built for that, and trying to force it into that role will just frustrate you.

After 30 days, I'm keeping my Pro subscription. DeepSeek earned a permanent spot in my stack—not as a replacement for everything, but as the tool I reach for when I need an answer I can actually trust.
