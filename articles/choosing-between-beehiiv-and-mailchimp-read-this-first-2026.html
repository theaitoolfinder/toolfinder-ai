Choosing Between Beehiiv and Mailchimp? Read This First (2026)

June 10, 2026 8 min read MyAI ToolsFinder

You spent three hours setting up your newsletter, writing your first issue, and building a signup form—only to realize your email tool charges you double what you expected once you hit 1,000 subscribers. That stings. The choice between Beehiiv and Mailchimp isn't just about features; it's about whether you're paying for legacy infrastructure you don't need or betting on a platform purpose-built for the creator economy. Here's what actually matters when you're about to commit your audience—and your revenue—to one of these platforms.

Both tools will send emails. Both have automation. But they were designed for fundamentally different users, and picking the wrong one means either overpaying for complexity you'll never touch or outgrowing your platform six months in. The real question isn't which is "better"—it's which one matches how you actually plan to build and monetize your newsletter.

Quick Verdict: Who Should Pick What

  • Best for newsletter creators building a media business: Beehiiv wins with better monetization tools, referral programs, and a pricing model that doesn't punish growth the way Mailchimp does.
  • Best for ecommerce brands and traditional email marketing: Mailchimp still dominates if you need deep ecommerce integrations, product recommendations, and customer journey mapping tied to purchase behavior.
  • Best if you're bootstrapping and need to start free: Beehiiv's free tier gives you more runway with up to 2,500 subscribers and no daily send limits—Mailchimp's free plan caps at 500 contacts and 1,000 sends per month.

Beehiiv: Built for the Substack Generation (But Without the Tax)

Beehiiv launched in 2021 with a specific thesis: newsletter creators were tired of giving Substack 10% of their revenue, and they wanted growth tools baked in from day one. The platform was designed by the team behind Morning Brew, so it's optimized for the exact workflow of someone building a media brand through email: write, grow, monetize, repeat.

The standout feature is the built-in referral program. You can set up a milestone-based reward system in about five minutes—"Refer 3 friends, get a bonus guide; refer 10, get a free month of premium access." This isn't something you hack together with Zapier and a spreadsheet. It's native, it tracks automatically, and it works. Creators consistently report 15-25% of their new subscribers come through referrals once they activate this feature. That's not a vanity metric—that's compounding organic growth while you sleep.

The ad network is the other differentiator. Once you hit 10,000 subscribers on the Scale plan, you can join Beehiiv's ad marketplace and start selling sponsorships without cold-pitching brands yourself. The platform handles matching, billing, and insertion. Creators are seeing $8-15 CPMs depending on niche. If you're in finance, tech, or marketing, those rates climb higher. This is infrastructure Mailchimp simply doesn't offer because it wasn't built for the creator revenue model.

Weaknesses? Beehiiv's automation is functional but basic. If you need complex behavioral triggers—"send email X if they clicked link Y but didn't open email Z within 72 hours"—you'll find the logic builder limiting. The platform also doesn't integrate with ecommerce platforms the way Mailchimp does. There's no native Shopify sync, no product recommendation engine, no abandoned cart flows. If you're selling physical products or running a traditional online store, Beehiiv isn't built for you.

Pricing is transparent and growth-friendly. The free Launch tier covers up to 2,500 subscribers with unlimited sends and basic analytics. The Grow plan starts at $49/month for up to 10,000 subscribers and adds the referral program, polls, and better segmentation. The Scale plan at $99/month unlocks the ad network, priority support, and advanced analytics. Notably, there's discussion in creator communities about people leaving Substack specifically because Beehiiv's $99/month flat fee beats paying 10% of revenue once you're making over $1,000/month.

Ideal user: You're a solo creator, educator, or niche media brand building an audience-first business. You plan to monetize through subscriptions, sponsorships, or premium content. You want tools that help you grow without hiring a growth marketer.

Mailchimp: The Email Marketing Incumbent That Grew Up

Mailchimp has been around since 2001, and it shows—both in the depth of its feature set and the complexity of its interface. This is a platform built for marketers running multi-channel campaigns, A/B testing subject lines against send times, and syncing customer data from a dozen different sources. If you're running an ecommerce brand, a SaaS company, or a local business with a loyalty program, Mailchimp's ecosystem is hard to beat.

The ecommerce integrations are where Mailchimp dominates. Native connections to Shopify, WooCommerce, BigCommerce, and Magento mean your product catalog, purchase history, and customer lifetime value automatically flow into your email segments. You can trigger abandoned cart emails, send product recommendations based on browsing behavior, and build customer journey maps that span email, SMS, and retargeting ads. Beehiiv can't touch this level of integration—it wasn't designed to.

The automation builder is genuinely powerful. You can create workflows with multiple branches, time delays, conditional logic, and triggers based on almost any data point Mailchimp tracks. Need to send a different email series to customers who spent over $200 versus under $50? Easy. Want to re-engage subscribers who haven't clicked in 90 days with a special offer, then remove them if they still don't engage after 30 more days? That's a 10-minute build. This is where Mailchimp's 20+ years of development show up as real utility.

The big downside is cost. Mailchimp's pricing scales aggressively with subscriber count, and it's not linear. The free tier caps at 500 contacts and 1,000 sends per month—laughably low if you're trying to grow. The Essentials plan starts at $13/month for 500 contacts but jumps to $50/month at 1,500 contacts, $80/month at 3,000, and $145/month at 10,000. That's significantly more than Beehiiv's flat $49 or $99. And if you need advanced features like multivariate testing or comparative reporting, you're pushed to the Standard plan, which starts at $20/month but hits $150/month at 5,000 contacts.

The interface is also dated. Mailchimp's dashboard feels like it was designed by committee over two decades—because it was. Finding the specific setting you need often requires navigating through three submenus and a help doc. The email builder is fine, but newer platforms like Beehiiv have cleaner, faster editors that feel less like wrestling with software.

Another real concern: Mailchimp's deliverability has taken hits in recent years. Multiple users report higher spam folder rates compared to dedicated newsletter platforms, likely because Mailchimp's IP reputation is diluted across millions of users sending everything from transactional receipts to spammy promotional blasts. Beehiiv, being newer and more focused on editorial content, tends to have cleaner sender reputation.

Ideal user: You're running an ecommerce business, a SaaS with complex onboarding flows, or a marketing agency managing multiple clients. You need deep integration with your tech stack, and you value mature automation capabilities over a clean interface. You're willing to pay more for flexibility.

Head-to-Head: Where Each Platform Actually Wins

Ease of Use: Beehiiv is dramatically simpler. The dashboard is intuitive, the email composer is fast, and you can go from signup to first send in under 30 minutes. Mailchimp requires genuine onboarding time—expect to spend a few hours learning where everything lives. ✓ Beehiiv

Growth Tools: Beehiiv's referral program and recommendation network are purpose-built for organic growth. Mailchimp has landing pages and signup forms, but no native viral growth mechanics. ✓ Beehiiv

Automation Depth: Mailchimp's workflow builder handles far more complex logic, conditional branches, and cross-channel orchestration. Beehiiv's automation is sufficient for basic drip campaigns but can't match the sophistication. ✓ Mailchimp

Ecommerce Integration: Not even close—Mailchimp syncs with every major ecommerce platform, tracks purchase behavior, and enables product-driven campaigns. Beehiiv doesn't play in this space. ✓ Mailchimp

Pricing for Creators: Beehiiv's flat-rate pricing is far more predictable and affordable as you scale. Mailchimp's per-contact model gets expensive fast. ✓ Beehiiv

Monetization Features: Beehiiv's native ad network and subscription tools are built for creator revenue. Mailchimp has no equivalent—you're on your own for sponsorships. ✓ Beehiiv

Analytics: Mailchimp offers deeper reporting, including revenue attribution, customer lifetime value tracking, and comparative campaign analysis. Beehiiv covers the basics well but doesn't go as deep. ✓ Mailchimp

Real-World Scenarios: Which Tool Fits Your Actual Workflow

Scenario 1: You're launching a weekly tech newsletter and want to grow to 10,000 subscribers in your first year. Go with Beehiiv. Set up the referral program from day one, offer a solid lead magnet at the 5-friend milestone, and let compound growth do the work. Use the free tier until you hit 2,500 subscribers, then upgrade to Grow at $49/month. Once you cross 10,000 subscribers, activate the ad network and start generating $800-1,200/month in sponsorship revenue without cold outreach. Mailchimp would cost you $145/month at 10,000 subscribers and wouldn't offer any of the growth or monetization mechanics that make this business model work.

Scenario 2: You run a Shopify store selling outdoor gear, and you want to recover abandoned carts and send personalized product recommendations. Mailchimp is the right choice. Connect your Shopify account, enable the abandoned cart automation (which triggers 1 hour, 24 hours, and 3 days after cart abandonment), and set up post-purchase flows that recommend complementary products based on what customers bought. Use segmentation to separate one-time buyers from repeat customers and send different re-engagement campaigns. Beehiiv can't do any of this—it's not designed to sync with product data or trigger based on purchase behavior.

Scenario 3: You're a freelance consultant building a personal brand, and you want to nurture leads with an educational email course. Beehiiv is the cleaner, simpler choice. Set up a 5-email drip course that triggers when someone downloads your lead magnet. The automation builder handles this easily, and the interface won't overwhelm you. As your list grows, you can add a paid newsletter tier or use the referral program to expand reach. Mailchimp can absolutely do this, but you're paying for a ton of features you'll never touch, and the setup friction is higher.

What the Migration Data Actually Shows

There's a quiet trend worth noting: creators are leaving platforms like Substack—and some are even leaving Mailchimp—for Beehiiv specifically. The discussion around "Substack tax" has intensified because once your newsletter is making $2,000/month, you're paying Substack $200/month in fees. Beehiiv's $99/month flat rate becomes dramatically cheaper as revenue scales. One creator community thread noted several writers with 15,000+ subscribers who saved $150-300/month by switching and picked up better growth tools in the process.

On the flip side, ecommerce brands aren't moving to Beehiiv—they have no reason to. If your revenue comes from product sales and email is a retention and conversion channel, Mailchimp's integration depth (or alternatives like Klaviyo) remains essential. The choice isn't about which platform is objectively better; it's about which revenue model you're operating.

The Honest Final Recommendation

If you're building a newsletter, blog, or audience-first business, choose Beehiiv. The pricing is better, the growth tools are native, and the platform is optimized for exactly what you're trying to do. You'll save money, grow faster, and have a clearer path to monetization. The only reason to hesitate is if you know you need complex automation that Beehiiv's builder can't handle—but most creators overestimate how much automation they'll actually use.

If you're running an ecommerce store, a SaaS product, or any business where email is part of a larger customer journey tied to purchase behavior, stick with Mailchimp (or explore Klaviyo as a more modern alternative). The integration ecosystem and automation sophistication are worth the higher cost. Just be realistic about the pricing as you scale—budget for $100-200/month once you're past 5,000 contacts.

For the 80% of creators asking this question, the answer is Beehiiv. The platform was built for you, it costs less, and it helps you grow without needing a marketing team. Mailchimp is a phenomenal tool, but it's solving a different problem for a different user.

Three Questions People Actually Ask Before Switching

Can I migrate my existing list from Mailchimp to Beehiiv without losing subscribers? Yes. Export your Mailchimp list as a CSV, then import it into Beehiiv. Both platforms support standard CSV formats with email, name, and custom field data. The only friction is re-confirming subscribers if your original Mailchimp signup didn't use double opt-in—some subscribers may need to confirm again to stay compliant with email regulations. Plan for about 5-10% drop-off during migration if you're moving a cold or older list.

Does Beehiiv work with tools like Make.com or Zapier for automation? Yes. Beehiiv has a Zapier integration that lets you connect it to thousands of other tools, including Make.com, which offers more complex workflow logic if you need it. You can trigger actions based on new subscribers, send subscriber data to a CRM, or add people to Beehiiv from form submissions on your website. It's not as deep as Mailchimp's API ecosystem, but it covers most use cases for creators who want to connect their newsletter to other parts of their business.

Which platform has better email deliverability in 2026? Beehiiv generally reports higher inbox placement rates, particularly for editorial content. Mailchimp's deliverability varies more widely because its user base includes everything from legitimate brands to spammy promotional senders, which dilutes sender reputation across shared IPs. That said, your own sender reputation matters more than the platform—if you send valuable content to an engaged list, both platforms will deliver fine. If you're sending cold outreach or purchased lists (don't), neither platform will save you from the spam folder.

Now go export that subscriber list, pick the tool that matches your actual business model, and stop paying for features you'll never use. Your first 1,000 subscribers are waiting.
