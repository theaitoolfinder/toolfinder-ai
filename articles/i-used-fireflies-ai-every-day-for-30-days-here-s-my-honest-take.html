I Used Fireflies.ai Every Day for 30 Days — Here's My Honest Take

June 12, 2026 8 min read By Alex Rivera · My AI Tools Finder

Ever sprint to your keyboard after a 45-minute client call, only to realize your notes consist of "discuss pricing," "they mentioned Q3," and a doodle of what might be a cat? I spent the first week of May in exactly that situation—six back-to-back strategy calls, zero usable notes, and a growing sense that I was either going to hire an assistant or start recording everything like a paranoid spy. That's when I committed to using Fireflies.ai for every single meeting, call, and interview for thirty straight days. No cherry-picking the easy wins. No skipping the messy, chaotic calls. Every conversation, every day, for a month.

What I found wasn't what I expected. Some features I thought would be gimmicks became daily essentials. Others that looked incredible in the demo turned out to be more trouble than they're worth. And after hundreds of hours of recorded meetings, I learned exactly where Fireflies.ai earns its keep—and where it still stumbles in 2026.

What Fireflies.ai Actually Does (Beyond the Marketing Speak)

Fireflies.ai is an AI meeting assistant that joins your video calls, records the audio, generates transcripts, and pulls out action items, questions, and key topics automatically. It integrates directly with Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, and a handful of other platforms, appearing as a participant named "Fireflies Notetaker" that silently captures everything said.

The company was founded in 2016 by Krish Ramineni and Sam Udotong, originally focused on voice-activated productivity tools before pivoting hard into meeting intelligence. By 2026, they're processing millions of conversations monthly and competing directly with Otter.ai, Grain, and a dozen newer entrants.

Pricing breaks down into four tiers: Free (800 minutes of storage, basic transcription), Pro ($10/month for unlimited transcription and advanced search), Business ($19/month per seat with team collaboration features), and Enterprise (custom pricing with API access and security controls). The free tier is genuinely usable—not just a teaser—which matters if you're testing the waters or only have a few critical meetings per week.

The One Feature That Justifies Everything: Smart Search

Forget the transcription itself—every tool does that now. The killer feature is Smart Search, and it's the reason I kept using Fireflies.ai even when other features frustrated me. This isn't basic keyword search. You can search across every meeting you've ever had for concepts, not just exact phrases.

Here's the before/after that sold me: I needed to find every time a specific client mentioned budget concerns across four months of weekly check-ins. Manual approach? Open sixteen transcripts, Command+F for "budget," "cost," "price," "expensive," and hope I didn't miss synonyms. With Smart Search, I typed "budget concerns" and got every relevant moment across all meetings, ranked by relevance, with the actual context around each mention.

It found moments where the client said "we're being careful with spend right now" and "leadership is watching every dollar"—phrases that didn't include my search terms but clearly matched the intent. That single search saved me two hours of transcript-skimming and caught three conversations I'd completely forgotten about. When you're juggling a dozen clients or managing a team, this kind of instant recall is the difference between looking prepared and looking like you haven't been paying attention.

Complete Feature Breakdown: What Works and What Doesn't

Automatic Recording and Transcription: Works flawlessly 95% of the time. The bot joins your call within seconds of the start time, and transcription accuracy hovers around 90-95% for clear speakers with decent microphones. Heavy accents, background noise, and cross-talk still trip it up—I had one call with a construction crew working outside where the transcript was borderline gibberish. But for standard office environments, it's reliable enough that I stopped taking manual notes entirely.

Speaker Identification: This is where things get messy. Fireflies attempts to label who said what, but it frequently confuses speakers in groups larger than four people, especially when voices are similar. I had a three-person team meeting where it confidently labeled everything the wrong person said. You can manually correct labels afterward, but that defeats the automation promise. For one-on-one calls? Perfect. For panel discussions or large team meetings? Plan to do cleanup work.

Action Items and Task Extraction: Hit or miss, leaning toward miss. The AI attempts to identify commitments and to-dos from conversation, but it over-indexes on any sentence containing "will," "should," or "need to." I got flagged action items like "we should probably get lunch sometime" and "I'll think about that"—neither of which were actual commitments. The useful action items were buried among the false positives. I ended up using this as a suggestion tool rather than a reliable task list, always manually reviewing what it surfaced.

Topic Tracking and Conversation Analytics: Genuinely useful for understanding patterns over time. Fireflies automatically tags topics discussed in each meeting and shows you how much time you spent on each. After thirty days, I could see that I was spending 40% of client calls discussing timelines and only 12% on creative direction—a wake-up call that my conversations were too operational and not strategic enough. This feature shines for managers and client-facing roles where conversation patterns matter.

Integrations with Slack, Notion, and CRM Tools: The Slack integration is solid—you can set it to automatically post meeting summaries to specific channels, which my team found helpful for transparency. The Notion integration worked but felt clunky, requiring too many manual steps to get transcripts into the right database. CRM integrations (Salesforce, HubSpot) are Business tier and above, so I couldn't test them fully, but based on user reports, they're reliable for automatically logging call notes.

Soundbites and Highlight Reels: You can create shareable clips from longer meetings—helpful for pulling testimonials, key decisions, or training moments. I used this twice in thirty days, both times to capture a client's exact approval language for a project scope. It's a nice-to-have, not a daily driver.

Conversation Intelligence (Business Tier and Up): This includes things like talk-to-listen ratios, sentiment analysis, and keyword tracking across your team's calls. I didn't have access to this tier during my test, but colleagues who use it for sales teams swear by the ability to identify which reps are dominating conversations versus actively listening. If you're managing a sales or support team, this is where Fireflies.ai separates itself from basic transcription tools.

Real-World Test Results: Three Scenarios, Zero Sugarcoating

Test 1: Client Strategy Call (Six Participants, 90 Minutes)

This was a complex project kickoff with stakeholders from three different departments. Fireflies joined on time, recorded the full call, and delivered a transcript within three minutes of the meeting ending. Transcription accuracy was around 92%—good enough that I could follow the conversation flow, but I had to correct about a dozen critical terms (product names, technical specifications) where it guessed wrong. Speaker labels were chaos—it thought there were four people instead of six and mixed up voices constantly. Smart Search afterward worked beautifully, though. I could instantly find every mention of "timeline," "deliverables," and "stakeholder approval" without scrolling through 18,000 words of transcript. Time saved versus manual notes: approximately 45 minutes.

Test 2: One-on-One Coaching Session (60 Minutes)

This was the ideal use case—two clear speakers, minimal cross-talk, focused agenda. Transcription was near-perfect, speaker identification worked flawlessly, and the automatic topic tagging accurately identified the four main themes we discussed. The action items feature flagged six potential tasks, three of which were legitimate and three of which were conversational filler. I used the Notion integration to push the transcript into my client database, which worked but required four clicks and a page refresh—not exactly frictionless. Time saved versus manual notes: approximately 30 minutes, plus the ability to quote the client verbatim when writing the follow-up plan.

Test 3: Rapid-Fire Interview Series (Five 20-Minute Calls in One Day)

I was conducting user research interviews and scheduled five back-to-back conversations. Fireflies handled the logistics perfectly—automatically joining each call based on my calendar, no manual intervention needed. Transcripts were ready before I started the next interview, which meant I could review key points between calls. However, the action items feature was useless here (interviews don't generate tasks), and the topic tracking was too generic to be helpful. What saved me was Smart Search the next day when I needed to find every mention of "onboarding friction" across all five conversations. Time saved versus manual transcription: at least four hours, possibly more.

Who Should Be Using This Right Now

If you're a consultant or freelancer juggling multiple clients: Fireflies.ai is close to essential. The ability to search across months of conversations and instantly recall what a client said in March is worth the Pro subscription alone. You'll look more attentive, respond faster, and never lose track of a verbal commitment again.

If you're managing a remote team with distributed meetings: The automatic recording and summary features create institutional memory. New team members can search past meetings to understand context, and you can hold people accountable to commitments without playing "he said, she said." The Business tier's analytics also help you spot communication patterns—who's monopolizing conversations, what topics are eating up time, whether meetings are actually productive.

If you're in sales or customer success: Conversation Intelligence features (Business tier and up) give you data on what's working. Which reps are asking the right questions? Which objections come up most often? What language correlates with closed deals? If you're serious about revenue, this is where Fireflies.ai earns its premium pricing.

If you're a content creator doing interviews: The transcription speed and Smart Search are perfect for podcast prep, article research, or video content based on conversations. I used it for three podcast interviews during the thirty days, and being able to pull exact quotes without rewinding audio saved hours of production time. Pair it with OpusClip for turning long-form interviews into social clips, and you've got a solid content workflow.

The Honest Limitations Nobody Mentions in Demos

Speaker identification is still not reliable enough for hands-off use in group settings. If you have more than three people on a call, plan to manually verify who said what before trusting the transcript for attribution. This is especially problematic in legal, journalistic, or compliance contexts where misattribution could cause real problems.

The action items feature generates too many false positives to be a primary task management system. You'll waste time sorting through suggestions that aren't actually commitments. I ended up disabling the automatic action item emails because the signal-to-noise ratio was too low.

Integration friction is real. While Fireflies.ai connects to dozens of tools, most integrations require manual configuration, regular re-authentication, or feel like afterthoughts compared to the core product. If you're expecting it to automatically populate your CRM or project management tool without any babysitting, you'll be disappointed.

Privacy concerns are legitimate. When Fireflies joins a meeting, everyone sees "Fireflies Notetaker" in the participant list, but not everyone pays attention. I had two situations where participants didn't realize they were being recorded until halfway through the call, which created awkward moments and required re-consent. Always verbally announce that you're recording, even if the bot is visible.

Pricing Breakdown: What Each Tier Actually Unlocks

The Free tier gives you 800 minutes of storage (about 13 hours of meetings), unlimited transcription for the first month, then reverts to limited credits. It's genuinely useful for light users or people testing the platform, but you'll hit the storage cap quickly if you have more than a few meetings per week. Basic search works, but you don't get Smart Search or conversation analytics.

Pro at $10/month is the sweet spot for individual users. You get unlimited transcription and storage, Smart Search across all conversations, advanced filters, and priority customer support. This is the tier that made me a believer. If you have more than five substantive meetings per week, the time savings justify the cost within days.

Business at $19/month per seat adds team collaboration features, conversation analytics, custom vocabulary (helpful for industry jargon), CRM integrations, and video storage. This makes sense once you have three or more people on your team who need shared access to meeting intelligence. The analytics alone—talk-time ratios, topic trends, sentiment tracking—are worth it for managers.

Enterprise pricing is custom but starts around $39/seat based on reports from companies using it. You get API access, advanced security controls, dedicated support, and custom integrations. Unless you're a company with compliance requirements or need to process thousands of calls monthly, you don't need this tier.

My honest take: Start with Free to test it on a handful of critical meetings. If you find yourself wishing you'd recorded more conversations, upgrade to Pro. Don't jump to Business unless you're managing a team and need the analytics—individual users won't use most of those features.

How It Stacks Up Against the Alternatives

Compared to Otter.ai, Fireflies has better integrations and more powerful search, but Otter's real-time transcription during the meeting is noticeably faster and more accurate. If you need to reference notes while still on the call, Otter wins. If you care more about post-meeting analysis and cross-conversation search, Fireflies is stronger.

Grain is purpose-built for customer-facing teams (sales, support, user research) and has better video highlight features and deal intelligence. If you're primarily recording client calls and need to share clips with your team, Grain's interface is cleaner. But Fireflies is more versatile across different meeting types—internal standups, strategy sessions, interviews—where Grain feels over-engineered.

Fathom is the lightweight alternative—faster, simpler, less expensive—but it doesn't have the depth of analytics or search power. If you just need transcripts and don't care about conversation intelligence, Fathom is probably enough. But if you're managing multiple projects or clients, you'll miss the advanced features.

Where should you choose a competitor? Pick Otter if you need real-time transcription during the call. Pick Grain if you're primarily focused on revenue conversations and video clips. Pick Fathom if you want something simpler and cheaper. Pick Fireflies if you need the most powerful search and analytics across all your conversations, regardless of context.

The Final Verdict: Who Should Buy Today

For individual consultants and freelancers: 8.5/10. The Pro tier at $10/month pays for itself if you have more than three client calls per week. Smart Search alone is worth the subscription.

For remote team managers: 9/10. The Business tier gives you visibility into how your team communicates, what's eating up meeting time, and creates institutional memory that prevents knowledge loss when people leave.

For content creators doing interviews: 7.5/10. The transcription is fast and accurate enough to replace manual work, but you'll need to supplement it with editing tools. Pair it with Descript or OpusClip for a complete workflow.

For sales and customer success teams: 9.5/10 at the Business tier and above. Conversation Intelligence features give you data you can't get any other way, and the competitive advantage is measurable in closed deals and customer retention.

Who should buy it today: Anyone who's ever thought "I wish I could remember exactly what was said in that meeting three weeks ago" should be using Fireflies.ai on the Pro tier minimum. Who should wait or look elsewhere: If you're only recording one or two calls per month, the free tier is enough.
