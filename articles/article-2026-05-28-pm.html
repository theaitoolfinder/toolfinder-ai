AI Tools Digest — May 28, 2026 (Evening Edition)

May 28, 2026 6 min read MyAI ToolsFinder

End-of-week check-in: what's worth adding to your workflow before Monday, what's worth canceling, and the three tools that generated the most discussion in the communities we follow this week.

The Short-Form Video Stack: What's Actually Working

Short-form video (TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts) is the highest-ROI content format for most creators right now. The challenge is volume — you need to post consistently to grow, which means the tools that reduce production time pay for themselves immediately. Here's the current stack worth knowing:

  • OpusClip ($15–$29/month) — Takes a long-form video and automatically clips it into 10–15 short viral moments, adds captions, and reformats for each platform. The AI scores each clip's "virality potential." For anyone repurposing long videos, this replaces 2–3 hours of manual editing per video.
  • CapCut (free) — Best-in-class auto-captions with 98%+ accuracy, AI background removal, and a template library with 50M+ trending formats. The free tier covers everything most creators need.
  • Descript ($12–$24/month) — Transcript-based video editing. Edit the video by editing the words, not the timeline. Removes filler words automatically. Underrated for interview-based content.

The AI Research Stack: What's Changed

Perplexity AI has quietly become the most-used research tool among the solopreneurs we track — not because it's the most powerful, but because it's the most trustworthy. Every answer is cited. You can verify every claim in one click. Compare that to ChatGPT, which will confidently fabricate statistics if it doesn't know the answer.

For deep research that needs citation integrity: Perplexity free (5 pro searches/day) → Perplexity Pro ($20/month) once you hit the limit. NotebookLM (free from Google) is the best companion for long documents — upload a 200-page PDF and ask questions directly against it, with citations back to specific pages.

What to Cancel Before Next Month

The tools worth canceling are ones where you're paying for features you use less than once a week. Common offenders: AI writing assistants you added during a trial but now use ChatGPT for, SEO tools you subscribed to for one project, image generators you haven't opened in a month. Run this audit: if you can't name a specific workflow a tool supports, it probably shouldn't be in your stack.

One Tool Worth Testing This Weekend

Granola (free beta) is an AI meeting notes app that runs locally on your Mac — no bot joining your calls, no cloud upload. It listens in the background, transcribes, and generates a structured summary after your meeting. For anyone concerned about data privacy in meeting transcription, it's the alternative to Otter.ai and Fireflies.ai that doesn't send your conversations to a server.
