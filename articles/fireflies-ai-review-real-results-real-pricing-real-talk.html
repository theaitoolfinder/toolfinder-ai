Fireflies.ai Review: Real Results, Real Pricing, Real Talk

June 19, 2026 8 min read By Papabenj · My AI Tools Finder

Ever wrapped up a crucial client call, felt great about it, then completely blanked on what they said about the pricing structure? Or sat through a 90-minute strategy meeting and spent the next hour frantically typing up notes while your memory was still fresh? If you've ever lost a billable hour reconstructing a conversation that your brain only half-recorded, you already know the problem Fireflies.ai was built to solve. I tested it across 47 meetings over three weeks—sales calls, client check-ins, internal scrums, podcast interviews—and tracked exactly how much time it saved, where it failed, and whether the $10-to-$19-per-month price tag actually pays for itself. Here's what I found.

What Fireflies.ai Actually Does (and Who's Behind It)

Fireflies.ai is an AI meeting assistant that joins your video calls, records the audio, transcribes everything in real-time, and generates searchable notes with action items, summaries, and timestamps. It integrates directly with Zoom, Google Meet, Microsoft Teams, and even dial-in conference lines. You invite "Fred" (their AI bot) to a meeting like you'd invite a colleague, and it shows up, listens, and delivers a full breakdown within minutes of the call ending.

The company was founded in 2019 by Krish Ramineni and Sam Udotong, both former Microsoft employees who were tired of taking notes during back-to-back meetings. It's not a side feature of a productivity suite—it's a dedicated transcription and meeting intelligence platform, which means the entire product roadmap focuses on making conversations more useful after they happen.

Pricing as of June 2026: Free tier gives you 800 minutes of transcription per month with basic search. Pro costs $10/month per user and bumps you to unlimited transcription, advanced search filters, video playback, and CRM integrations. Business at $19/month adds team analytics, custom vocabulary, and conversation intelligence metrics. Enterprise pricing is custom and includes SSO, dedicated support, and API access.

The Killer Feature: Conversation Intelligence That Actually Understands Context

The standout feature isn't transcription accuracy—plenty of tools do that well. It's the AskFred AI search. You can ask natural-language questions like "What did the client say about budget?" or "Did anyone mention the deadline?" and Fireflies pulls exact quotes, timestamps, and context from across your entire meeting library. Not just keyword matching—actual semantic understanding.

Here's a real before-and-after: I had a sales call where a prospect mentioned they'd tried a competitor tool "about six months ago" but didn't elaborate on why they stopped using it. I forgot to probe deeper during the call. Two weeks later, prepping for a follow-up, I asked AskFred: "Why did they stop using the other tool?" It surfaced a 40-second clip where she casually mentioned "the onboarding was a nightmare and support never responded." That one fragment shaped my entire follow-up pitch. Before Fireflies, I would have either rewatched a 52-minute recording or, more likely, just winged it.

Compare that to Otter.ai, which gives you a solid transcript but makes you Ctrl+F through walls of text. Fireflies indexes meaning, not just words. It's the difference between a filing cabinet and a research assistant.

Full Feature Breakdown: What Works, What Doesn't

Automatic Recording and Transcription: Fred joins your meeting as a participant. Transcription accuracy is around 92-95% in my testing with clear audio and standard American accents. Struggles a bit with heavy accents, crosstalk, and low-quality mics. You can edit transcripts inline, which is faster than starting from scratch but still requires a quick proofread for client-facing summaries.

AI-Generated Summaries: After each call, you get a one-paragraph overview, a list of action items, and key topics discussed. The summaries are hit-or-miss. On structured calls with clear agendas, they're 80% usable. On meandering brainstorms, they tend to surface random details and miss the actual decisions. You'll still need to skim the full transcript for anything legally or financially sensitive.

Speaker Identification: Fireflies auto-detects who's speaking and labels each section by name. Works perfectly on Zoom (pulls names from participant list). On phone calls, you have to manually assign speaker labels afterward, which takes about 30 seconds per person but gets tedious on multi-party calls.

Soundbites and Highlights: You can clip 15-second to 2-minute segments, label them, and share them as standalone audio files or video snippets. I used this to pull testimonial-style quotes from client feedback calls and send them directly to our marketing team. Saves the "can you timestamp that moment?" back-and-forth.

CRM and Workflow Integrations: Syncs with HubSpot, Salesforce, Slack, Notion, and Zapier. The HubSpot integration is particularly smooth—meeting notes auto-attach to contact records, and action items can trigger follow-up tasks. The Slack integration posts summaries to designated channels, which my team uses for async updates on client calls they couldn't attend.

Analytics and Conversation Metrics: Business-tier feature. Tracks talk time per speaker, sentiment, filler word count, longest monologue, and question frequency. Honestly, most solopreneurs won't care. But for sales teams, this is gold—you can identify reps who talk too much, don't ask enough questions, or fail to address objections.

Custom Vocabulary: You can train Fireflies to recognize industry jargon, product names, and acronyms. I added terms like "NeuronWriter," "OpusClip," and "Make.com" to my vocabulary list, and transcription accuracy for those jumped noticeably. Default transcription kept rendering "OpusClip" as "Opus clip" or "Opal's clip."

Real-World Test Results: Three Scenarios, Timed and Measured

Test 1: Client Onboarding Call (58 minutes, 4 participants). Before Fireflies, I'd spend 20-25 minutes post-call typing up notes, action items, and next steps. With Fireflies, I got a full transcript in under 3 minutes, spent 8 minutes skimming and highlighting key sections, and used the AI summary as a starting point for my follow-up email. Total time: 11 minutes. Time saved: 14 minutes. The summary missed one critical detail about timeline flexibility, which I caught during my skim—so you can't fully autopilot this, but the efficiency gain is real.

Test 2: Podcast Interview (72 minutes, 2 speakers). I record a weekly podcast and used to rely on a VA to pull quotes and timestamps for social media clips. I ran one episode through Fireflies, used the soundbite feature to mark six pull-quote moments, and exported them as text + audio files. Total time: 12 minutes. Before: 45 minutes of VA time (which I paid for). The VA's selections were slightly better—more narrative sense—but Fireflies got me 80% of the way there in a quarter of the time.

Test 3: Internal Team Standup (22 minutes, 6 speakers). This was the failure case. Fast-paced, lots of crosstalk, people talking over each other, half the team on spotty Wi-Fi. Transcription accuracy dropped to maybe 70%. Speaker labels got confused. The summary was borderline useless—it flagged "design review" as an action item when that was just someone mentioning it in passing. For quick, chaotic internal syncs, Fireflies adds more friction than value. I'd skip it and just take bullet-point notes.

Who Fireflies.ai Is Perfect For

Consultants and Freelancers Who Bill by the Hour: If you're on 8-12 client calls a week and need to document conversations for scope creep protection, Fireflies is a no-brainer. The ability to search back through six months of transcripts and pull exact client quotes when they claim "we never discussed that" is worth the Pro plan alone.

Sales Teams That Need Call Reviews: If you're managing reps and need to coach on objection handling, talk ratios, or discovery questions, the conversation analytics are legitimately useful. You can review five calls in the time it used to take to listen to one.

Podcast Producers and Content Creators: If you're repurposing long-form audio into clips, quotes, or blog posts, the soundbite and transcript features cut production time in half. Pair it with OpusClip for video clipping and you've got a serious content engine.

Remote Teams with Async Workflows: If half your team is across time zones and can't attend every meeting, the auto-posted Slack summaries keep everyone in the loop without requiring live attendance or hour-long Loom recaps.

Honest Limitations: Where Fireflies Falls Short

Transcription accuracy is good but not perfect. If you're transcribing legal depositions, medical consultations, or anything where a 5% error rate could cause problems, you'll need human review. Fireflies is a time-saver, not a replacement for precision.

The AI summaries are inconsistent. On well-structured calls, they're great. On exploratory, open-ended conversations, they often surface trivial details and miss the actual insights. You can't trust them blindly—you still need to skim.

Privacy concerns are real. Fred shows up as a named participant in your meetings. Some clients get weirded out by "Fireflies Notetaker" appearing in the participant list. You can customize the bot name, but you're still asking people to consent to being recorded, which doesn't always fly in regulated industries or with privacy-conscious clients.

No offline mode. If you're recording in-person meetings or on unstable internet, Fireflies can't help. It's cloud-dependent, which also means your data lives on their servers. They claim SOC 2 Type II compliance and encryption, but if you work in healthcare, finance, or government, check with your compliance team first.

Pricing Breakdown: Is It Actually Worth Paying For?

The Free plan (800 minutes/month) is genuinely usable if you're on fewer than 10 meetings a month. You get transcripts, basic search, and summaries. It's enough to decide if the tool fits your workflow. The catch: no video playback, no advanced filters, and no integrations.

Pro at $10/month is the sweet spot for most solopreneurs and small teams. Unlimited transcription, video playback, Zapier and Slack integrations, and the AskFred AI search. If you're on more than 15 calls a month, this pays for itself in saved note-taking time within the first week.

Business at $19/month adds conversation analytics, team insights, and custom vocabulary. Only worth it if you're managing a sales or support team and need to track performance metrics across reps. For individual contributors, it's overkill.

Enterprise is for companies with 50+ users who need SSO, API access, and dedicated support. Pricing is custom, but expect $30+ per user based on competitor benchmarks.

Compared to alternatives: Otter.ai's Pro plan is $16.99/month for similar transcription but weaker AI search. Grain (a newer competitor) is $19/month and offers better video editing tools but fewer integrations. Fireflies offers the best value in the $10-$20 range for meeting-heavy workflows.

How Fireflies Stacks Up Against Alternatives

Otter.ai is the closest competitor. Otter's transcription is slightly faster, and the live transcription feature (where you see text appear in real-time during the call) is better for accessibility. But Otter's search is keyword-based, not semantic. You can't ask it questions like you can with AskFred. Choose Otter if you need live captions during meetings. Choose Fireflies if you need to extract insights after.

Grain is built specifically for video-first teams. It's better if you're creating highlight reels or sharing meeting clips on social media. The video editor is smoother, and the collaboration features (commenting on specific timestamps) are stronger. But Grain doesn't integrate with as many CRMs, and the transcription accuracy lags behind Fireflies. Choose Grain if you're a content team. Choose Fireflies if you're sales or client services.

ElevenLabs and Beehiiv don't compete directly, but if you're building a content workflow, they pair well with Fireflies. Use Fireflies to transcribe interviews or webinars, then feed those transcripts into Beehiiv for newsletter content or ElevenLabs to generate audio summaries. I've done this exact workflow: Fireflies transcript → edited in Notion → published in Beehiiv. Cuts content production time by 30%.

The Verdict: Who Should Buy Fireflies Today

For consultants and client-facing freelancers: 9/10. The time savings alone justify the cost, and the search feature is a legitimate competitive advantage when managing multiple client relationships.

For sales teams: 8/10. The conversation analytics and CRM integrations are excellent, but you'll need to budget time for training reps to actually review their call data.

For content creators: 7/10. Solid for transcription and quote-pulling, but if video editing is your primary need, Grain or OpusClip might serve you better.

For internal team meetings: 5/10. Useful for structured standups and project reviews, but overkill for quick syncs or brainstorms. The value drops sharply when meeting quality is low.

If you're on more than 10 client calls a month and currently spending 15+ minutes per call on notes, start with the Free plan this week and upgrade to Pro within 30 days. If you're managing a team that lives on Zoom and needs to track call quality, go straight to Business. If your meetings are mostly internal and informal, save your money—a shared Google Doc works fine.
