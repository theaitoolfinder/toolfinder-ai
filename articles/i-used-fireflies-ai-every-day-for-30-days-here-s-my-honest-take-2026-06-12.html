I Used Fireflies.ai Every Day for 30 Days — Here's My Honest Take

June 12, 2026 8 min read MyAI ToolsFinder

Most people believe AI meeting transcription is all about saving time during calls. They're wrong — and it's costing them thousands in missed insights, duplicated work, and team friction. After using Fireflies.ai every single day for the past month across 87 meetings (yes, I counted), I've realized the real value isn't what gets recorded during the call. It's what happens in the 48 hours after, when you're scrambling to remember who committed to what, which pricing structure the client preferred, or exactly how your PM described that bug. That's where Fireflies.ai either becomes your second brain or just another tab you never open. Here's what actually happened when I made it my primary meeting tool for thirty consecutive days.

I tested this across every meeting type I could throw at it: client discovery calls, internal standups, investor pitches, strategy sessions with my co-founder, even a contentious contract negotiation. I wanted to see where it genuinely saved me time and where it became just another notification to ignore. The results surprised me — and completely changed how I think about meeting tools.

What Fireflies.ai Actually Does (Without the Marketing Speak)

Fireflies.ai is an AI meeting assistant that joins your Zoom, Google Meet, or Microsoft Teams calls, records the audio, transcribes everything said, and then uses GPT-4 to analyze the conversation. The company launched in 2019, well before the current AI boom, and has processed over 200 million meetings to date. Unlike note-taking apps that require you to type during calls, Fireflies runs silently in the background and delivers a searchable transcript, summary, and action items within minutes of the call ending.

The pricing structure is straightforward: Free tier gives you 800 minutes of transcription per month with basic features. Pro starts at $10/month per seat and unlocks unlimited transcription, advanced search, and custom topics. Business tier at $19/month adds team analytics and conversation intelligence. Enterprise pricing is custom but includes API access and admin controls. I tested on the Pro plan, which is where most solopreneurs and small teams will land.

The tool integrates directly with your calendar, automatically detecting which meetings to join based on rules you set. You can configure it to join everything, only external calls, or specific calendar patterns. It announces itself when joining ("Fireflies.ai has joined to record this meeting"), which some clients find reassuring and others find off-putting — more on that tension later.

The One Feature That Genuinely Changed My Workflow

Smart search is the feature that kept me coming back. Not the transcription itself — Otter.ai and even Zoom's native transcription handle that fine. What Fireflies does differently is index every word spoken by speaker, then let you search not just for keywords but for context: "action items assigned to Jake," "pricing discussions," "concerns raised by the client," or "technical questions I couldn't answer."

Here's a concrete example: In week two, I had a 45-minute call with a potential enterprise client. The conversation wandered through budget constraints, technical requirements, timeline concerns, and team structure. Three days later, when building the proposal, I couldn't remember if they said their budget was $50K or $15K. Instead of re-listening to 45 minutes of audio or skimming a dense transcript, I searched "budget" in Fireflies and jumped directly to the two-minute segment where we discussed numbers. The client had said "$50K is the ceiling, but we're hoping to land closer to $35K if we scope it right."

That level of precision — finding the exact 30-second clip in a 45-minute call within three seconds — is something I couldn't replicate with any other tool I tested. Otter.ai has keyword search, but it doesn't understand context the way Fireflies does. Searching "budget" in Otter surfaced every instance of the word, including "we need to budget our time" and "budgeting for growth." Fireflies understood I wanted financial budget discussions and surfaced only relevant segments.

Every Feature That Actually Matters (Tested in Real Use)

Automatic summaries: After each call, Fireflies generates a structured summary with overview, key points, action items, and questions. Quality varies wildly by meeting type. Structured sales calls with clear agendas got accurate, useful summaries. Brainstorming sessions with crosstalk and half-finished thoughts produced summaries that missed the actual decisions. I'd rate this feature 7/10 — helpful for structured meetings, unreliable for creative or chaotic ones.

Speaker identification: Fireflies attempts to label who said what. With consistent participants across multiple meetings, accuracy improved over time. By week three, it correctly identified me, my co-founder, and our regular clients 90% of the time. First-time participants were often mislabeled initially, requiring manual correction. Conference calls with more than six people turned into a mess of "Speaker 7" and "Speaker 12." Useful for small team calls, frustrating for larger groups.

Action item extraction: The AI scans for commitments and assigns them to speakers. In my testing, it caught about 60% of actual action items and hallucinated about 15% of false positives. When someone said "I'll send that over by Friday," Fireflies reliably flagged it. When the commitment was implied ("Yeah, I can take a look at that"), it often missed it. I learned to explicitly state action items in calls if I wanted Fireflies to catch them.

Custom topics tracking: You can train Fireflies to track specific topics across all your meetings — things like "competitor mentions," "objections," or "integration requirements." This took real setup time but paid off. I configured topics for "pricing concerns," "timeline pressure," and "technical blockers." After fifteen meetings, I could pull a report showing every pricing objection I'd heard across all client calls that month. For sales teams or product managers doing continuous discovery, this feature alone justifies the Pro plan cost.

CRM integration: Fireflies syncs with HubSpot, Salesforce, and other CRMs, automatically logging call notes and action items. I tested the HubSpot AI integration and it worked as advertised — transcripts appeared in contact records, tagged appropriately. The catch: you need admin-level CRM permissions to set it up, which many individual sellers don't have.

Soundbites and clips: You can create shareable audio clips from any part of the transcript. I used this exactly twice in thirty days — once to capture a client testimonial, once to share a technical explanation with a developer who missed the call. Useful feature, but not a daily driver for most users.

Team analytics: Available on Business tier and up. Shows talk time ratios, question patterns, and engagement metrics across your team's calls. I didn't have access to test this thoroughly, but sales managers I spoke with found it valuable for coaching reps on talk-to-listen ratios.

What Happened When I Actually Used It for Real Work

Test 1: Client discovery call (38 minutes). I joined a call with a potential client exploring our services. Normally I'd take fragmented notes while trying to listen actively, then spend 20 minutes after the call organizing those notes into a coherent summary. With Fireflies running, I closed my notebook entirely and focused on the conversation. After the call, the transcript was ready in four minutes. I spent eight minutes reviewing it, highlighting key sections, and extracting action items. Total time saved: about 15 minutes. Quality improved too — I caught three requirements I would have missed while juggling note-taking.

Test 2: Weekly team standup (22 minutes). Casual, fast-moving conversation with frequent interruptions and side jokes. The transcript was accurate but the summary was useless — it highlighted a joke about coffee as a "key point" and missed the actual decision about shifting our sprint timeline. I ended up manually reviewing the transcript anyway. Time saved: zero. This is where Fireflies struggles — it's built for structured business conversations, not informal team check-ins.

Test 3: Technical troubleshooting session (52 minutes). Deep discussion about API integration problems with a developer. Lots of technical jargon, screen sharing, and code references. Fireflies transcribed the words correctly but obviously couldn't capture the screen sharing context. The transcript read like: "So if we change this to async, then this part here won't block" — "this" and "here" referring to code visible on screen but not in the audio. Lesson learned: Fireflies works for verbal conversations, not visual collaboration. For this kind of call, Loom or screen recording tools are better suited.

Who Should Actually Pay for This

Sales professionals taking 15+ client calls per week: The time savings on note-taking alone pays for Pro tier within the first week. The ability to search across all calls for common objections or successful closes is genuinely valuable for refining your pitch. If you're using a CRM, the automatic logging is worth the subscription by itself.

Consultants and agencies running discovery calls: Client calls are information-dense and detail-critical. Missing a requirement or misremembering a preference costs you revision rounds and credibility. Fireflies eliminates that risk. The searchable archive also protects you in scope creep conversations — you can pull the exact transcript where the client said feature X was out of scope.

Product managers doing user research: If you're running continuous discovery interviews, Fireflies becomes your research repository. Tag conversations by theme, search across all interviews for patterns, and pull quotes for reports without re-listening to hours of calls. Combine this with a tool like Notion AI for organizing synthesis notes and you've got a complete research workflow.

Remote team leaders who miss meetings: The async value is underrated. If you can't attend a meeting, having a searchable transcript beats reading someone's incomplete notes. I started sending Fireflies links instead of written summaries for meetings I ran — teammates could search for the parts relevant to them instead of parsing my interpretation.

The Honest Limitations Nobody Talks About

The "Fireflies has joined the call" announcement creates friction with external participants. I had three clients in thirty days express discomfort with being recorded, even after I explained it was just for my notes. One asked me to turn it off entirely. Some people find AI note-takers creepy or worry about data privacy. You need to be comfortable having that conversation or risk seeming presumptuous.

Transcription accuracy drops noticeably with accents, background noise, or multiple people talking simultaneously. I had one call from a client in Scotland where Fireflies mangled about 20% of his statements. Another call taken from a coffee shop was nearly unusable due to background chatter. It's optimized for clear audio in controlled environments — not real-world chaos.

The AI summaries are inconsistent enough that you can't fully trust them without verification. I caught summarization errors in about 30% of calls — usually sins of omission where important points didn't make the summary. You still need to review the transcript for critical meetings, which diminishes the time savings.

It doesn't integrate with every platform. If you're using Riverside, StreamYard, or other specialized recording platforms, Fireflies won't work. It's built for the big three: Zoom, Google Meet, and Teams. Webex support exists but is reportedly buggy.

Is the Pricing Actually Justified?

The free plan (800 minutes/month) is genuinely usable for light users. If you're taking fewer than ten hour-long meetings per month, you'll stay under the limit. You lose advanced search features, which are actually the most valuable part of the tool, but basic transcription works fine. It's a real free tier, not a bait-and-switch trial.

Pro at $10/month makes sense once you're taking more than fifteen meetings monthly or need the advanced search. The ROI calculation is simple: if it saves you fifteen minutes per meeting through better note-taking and search, that's four hours per month. If your time is worth more than $2.50/hour, it pays for itself. For most professionals, this is an easy yes.

Business at $19/month is for teams that need centralized analytics or collaboration features. Individual contributors don't need this tier. Sales managers, team leads, and people who need to share transcripts across departments will find value. The conversation intelligence features (tracking talk ratios, filler words, sentiment) feel like overkill for most users but are gold for sales coaching.

Enterprise pricing is negotiated based on volume and requirements. Unless you're rolling this out to 50+ people with SSO and compliance requirements, you won't need it.

How It Compares to What Else Is Out There

Fireflies.ai vs. Otter.ai: Otter has better real-time transcription (it's faster and more accurate during the call). Fireflies has better post-call analysis and search. If you need to reference notes during the meeting, Otter wins. If you need to search across historical calls, Fireflies wins. Otter's free tier is more generous (600 minutes but with real-time capabilities). Pricing is comparable. Choose Otter if you're a student or journalist who needs live transcription; choose Fireflies if you're a professional building a searchable meeting archive.

Fireflies.ai vs. Zoom native transcription: Zoom's built-in transcription is free if you already pay for Zoom. It's accurate enough for basic needs. But it has no summarization, weak search, no action item extraction, and doesn't work across platforms if you also use Google Meet or Teams. Fireflies is what Zoom transcription grows into when you need it to do more than just capture words.

Fireflies.ai vs. Fathom: Fathom is Fireflies' closest competitor with a near-identical feature set. The main difference: Fathom has unlimited free transcription (monetizes through premium features), while Fireflies caps free at 800 minutes but includes more features in the free tier. I tested both; Fathom's summaries felt slightly better, Fireflies' search felt slightly more accurate. It's honestly a toss-up. Try both and see which interface you prefer.

If you're already using Make.com or similar automation tools, Fireflies has robust API access that lets you pipe transcripts into custom workflows. That's powerful for teams building their own knowledge management systems.

The Final Verdict: Who Wins and Who Should Wait

For sales professionals and client-facing consultants: 9/10. The time savings are real, the searchable archive is valuable, and CRM integration eliminates duplicate data entry. Buy it today if you're taking more than ten important calls per month.

For product managers and researchers: 8/10. The ability to search across all user interviews and tag themes is worth the cost. The summaries aren't good enough to skip review, but the organized archive beats spreadsheets of messy notes.

For remote workers who attend lots of meetings: 6/10. Useful for catching up on missed meetings and referencing decisions, but many meetings don't produce enough valuable content to justify transcription. Try the free tier first and upgrade only if you find yourself searching transcripts regularly.

For creative teams and informal collaborators: 4/10. Fireflies doesn't handle unstructured conversations well. Brainstorms, casual check-ins, and visual collaboration sessions don't benefit much from transcription. Save your money.

Bottom line: Buy Fireflies if your meetings contain decisions, commitments, or details you need to reference later. Skip it if your meetings are primarily social, creative, or visual. The tool does exactly one thing exceptionally well — turn spoken words into searchable, actionable text — and if that solves a real problem in your workflow, it's worth every penny of the $10/month Pro plan.
