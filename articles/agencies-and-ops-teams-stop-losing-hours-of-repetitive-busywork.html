Agencies and Ops Teams: Stop Losing hours of repetitive busywork every week — Make.com Solves This in Minutes

July 24, 2026 7 min read By Papabenj · My AI Tools Finder

Agency ops teams are losing an average of $2,400 per month in billable hours — not on client work, but on copying the same data between apps. Every single day, someone on your team is manually exporting a list from your CRM, reformatting it in a spreadsheet, uploading it to your project management tool, copying key fields into your email platform, and updating your analytics dashboard. It takes 45 minutes. Sometimes an hour. And tomorrow, you'll do it all over again.

This isn't a small inefficiency — it's a structural drain on your agency's capacity. If you're running a team of five, and each person loses just 5 hours a week to this kind of busywork, that's 25 hours gone. That's more than half a full-time employee's output, vanished into the gap between your tools. You can't bill it. You can't delegate it away. And every hour spent moving data around is an hour not spent on strategy, creative work, or actually serving clients.

The worst part? Your team knows it's wasteful. They feel it. The data entry fatigue is real, and it compounds. When your project manager spends the first 90 minutes of every Monday morning syncing client intake forms with task boards and time tracking systems, they're not energized for the week — they're already exhausted. When your account lead has to manually cross-reference three different platforms just to pull a simple client report, they start dreading what should be a routine task.

This problem doesn't exist because your team is inefficient. It exists because modern agencies run on 8 to 12 different specialized tools — and those tools don't talk to each other. Your CRM doesn't automatically update your project tracker. Your form submissions don't flow into your client database. Your time logs don't sync with your invoicing system. So humans become the glue, manually shuttling information from one silo to another, every single day.

Why the Obvious Fixes Don't Work

Most agencies try three things first. They hire a VA or junior ops person to "just handle the data entry." That works for about six weeks, until that person burns out or the workload scales faster than one person can keep up. You've also just created a single point of failure — when they're out sick or on vacation, the whole system grinds to a halt. And you're still paying $2,500 to $4,000 a month for someone to do work that shouldn't exist in the first place.

Others try Zapier or basic native integrations. These help with one or two simple connections — like sending new form entries to a Google Sheet — but they break down fast when you need conditional logic, multi-step workflows, or data transformation. If your workflow is more complex than "when this happens, copy that," you hit a wall. And agency workflows are always more complex than that. You need to filter, reformat, route to different places based on client type, and handle errors when something doesn't match.

The third attempt is usually just working harder — coming in earlier, staying later, or accepting that "this is just how agencies work." That's not a solution. That's resignation. And it costs you more than time. It costs you the ability to take on new clients, the mental bandwidth to think strategically, and the team morale that comes from doing work that actually matters.

Exactly How to Use It to Solve This

Let's walk through a real scenario most agencies deal with daily: a new client fills out an intake form, and you need that information to flow into your CRM, create a project in your task manager, assign the right team members, schedule a kickoff meeting, and send a welcome email. Right now, that takes 30 to 45 minutes of manual work. Here's how to automate it completely in Make.com.

Step 1: Connect your intake form as the trigger. Log into Make.com and create a new scenario. Add your form tool — let's say Typeform or Google Forms — as the first module. Authenticate it, then set the trigger to "Watch New Responses." This tells Make.com to check for new form submissions and start the workflow every time one comes in.

Step 2: Add your CRM and map the fields. Add a new module for your CRM — HubSpot, Pipedrive, or whatever you use. Choose "Create a Contact" or "Create a Deal." Now map the form fields to your CRM fields — first name goes here, company name goes there, email maps to this field. Make.com shows you a dropdown of every field from the previous step, so you're just clicking to connect them. If you need to reformat anything — like splitting a full name into first and last — you can add a text transformation step in between.

Step 3: Create the project in your task manager. Add another module for your project management tool — Asana, ClickUp, Monday, or Notion. Choose "Create a Project" or "Create a Database Item." Pull in the client name from the form, set a project template, assign a default due date (you can use formulas like "14 days from now"), and assign the project owner based on the service type the client selected in the form. This is where conditional logic comes in — if they selected "SEO," assign it to Sarah; if they selected "Paid Ads," assign it to Mike.

Step 4: Add a router for conditional steps. Click the "Add a Router" module. This lets you split the workflow into different paths based on conditions. For example, if the client is marked as "high priority" in the form, send a Slack notification to your leadership channel. If they're standard priority, just log it in your general ops channel. Each path can have its own series of steps.

Step 5: Schedule a kickoff meeting. Add a Calendly or Google Calendar module. Use the "Create an Event" action, set the date to 3 business days from today, invite the client's email from the form, and include a pre-written meeting description that pulls in their company name and project details. This happens automatically — no back-and-forth, no calendar Tetris.

Step 6: Send the welcome email. Add a Gmail or email platform module at the end. Compose a template that says "Hi [First Name], welcome to [Your Agency]! Your project with [Project Name] is set up and your kickoff meeting is scheduled for [Meeting Date]. Here's what to expect next…" Pull those variables from earlier steps in the workflow. Hit save.

Step 7: Test it and turn it on. Click "Run Once" at the bottom of the scenario. Make.com will simulate a form submission and show you exactly what happens at each step. If something breaks — like a field isn't mapping correctly — it'll show you the error and where it happened. Fix it, test again, then flip the scenario to "On." From that moment forward, every new form submission triggers the entire workflow automatically.

That's it. You built it once. Now it runs forever, or until you decide to change it. The next time a client fills out your intake form, all of this happens in under 60 seconds, with zero human involvement. Your team gets a Slack notification that the project is live and ready to start. The client gets their welcome email and meeting invite. And you didn't touch a spreadsheet.
