Synthesia: 7 Genuine Pros and 5 Real Cons You Need to Know

July 03, 2026 7 min read By Papabenj · My AI Tools Finder

Synthesia is the best AI video tool for corporate training and internal communications—but it's a poor choice if you need content that feels human. After testing it extensively across client projects in 2026, I'm giving it a 7.5/10: powerful for specific use cases, limiting for others. Here's exactly where it excels and where it falls short.

Last month, a marketing director I know spent $800 on a Synthesia annual plan, excited to create YouTube content for her wellness brand. Three weeks later, she canceled. The avatars looked polished in the demo, but her audience immediately flagged the videos as "artificial" and "cold." Her engagement dropped 40%. She switched to recording herself on her phone—less polished, but authentic. That's the Synthesia paradox: it's technically impressive but contextually limited. Getting this decision right means understanding exactly what you're buying and what you're sacrificing.

What Synthesia Actually Is

Synthesia generates AI video using digital avatars and text-to-speech voices. You type a script, select an avatar, and the platform renders a video of that avatar speaking your words. No filming, no editing software, no video crew.

Pricing (July 2026): Starter plan at $29/month (10 video minutes), Creator at $89/month (30 minutes), Enterprise custom pricing starting around $1,000/month for unlimited minutes and custom avatars.

Best for: Training managers, L&D teams, HR departments, and internal communications professionals who need to produce consistent video content at scale without on-camera talent.

The Pros: Where Synthesia Actually Delivers

1. Eliminates the Video Production Bottleneck for Training Content

If you're producing compliance training, product onboarding, or software tutorials, Synthesia cuts production time from days to hours. A global SaaS company I consulted with in Q1 2026 used Synthesia to create localized training videos in 17 languages—something that would have cost $50,000+ with traditional production. They spent $15,000 annually on Synthesia Enterprise instead. The avatars don't need breaks, don't have scheduling conflicts, and never ask for retakes. For training content where information delivery matters more than emotional connection, this efficiency is genuine.

2. Multi-Language Video Creation Without Hiring Translators or Voice Actors

Synthesia supports 130+ languages with native-sounding text-to-speech. You write your script once in English, duplicate it, translate the text (using ChatGPT or DeepL), and generate videos in Spanish, Mandarin, German, and Portuguese in the same afternoon. The voice synchronization is accurate—no lip-sync issues like you'd get with dubbed video. For multinational companies, this feature alone justifies the cost. Compare this to ElevenLabs, which offers superior voice cloning but requires separate video editing tools to sync with visuals.

3. Template Library That Actually Saves Time

Synthesia's template collection includes pre-designed layouts for training modules, product announcements, and educational content. You're not starting from a blank canvas. Select a template, swap in your logo, adjust colors to match brand guidelines, and input your script. A compliance training video that would take a designer four hours to layout in Premiere Pro takes 20 minutes in Synthesia. The templates aren't revolutionary, but they're professional enough for internal use and client presentations where video quality is secondary to information delivery.

4. Version Control and Update Speed That Traditional Video Can't Match

When product features change or compliance regulations update, you edit the script and regenerate the video in minutes. Traditional video requires re-shooting, re-editing, and re-rendering. A financial services firm I worked with uses Synthesia for regulatory training that changes quarterly. Before Synthesia, they spent $8,000 per quarter updating videos. Now they spend two hours and zero additional dollars. This iterative capability makes Synthesia valuable for documentation that evolves frequently.

5. Custom Avatar Creation for Brand Consistency

Enterprise plans let you create custom avatars—digital twins of real employees or branded characters. A tech startup used this to create an avatar of their CEO for weekly company updates, saving him three hours per week he was spending on recording and retakes. The custom avatar costs around $1,000 to create but becomes a reusable asset. It's not perfect—there's still an uncanny valley effect—but for recurring internal communications where the same person needs to deliver messages consistently, it works.

6. Screen Recording Integration for Software Training

You can combine avatar presentations with screen recordings, making Synthesia genuinely useful for software tutorials. Record your screen demonstrating a process, then have an avatar explain the steps in a split-screen layout. This beats recording yourself on camera because you can update the narration without re-recording the screen capture. For SaaS companies creating help documentation, this feature reduces tutorial production time by roughly 60%.

7. Collaboration Features That Support Team Workflows

Multiple team members can work on scripts, review videos, and leave timestamped feedback within the platform. This isn't revolutionary—tools like Fireflies.ai offer better collaboration for meeting notes—but for video production, it eliminates the endless email chains and Slack threads about revision requests. The approval workflow is straightforward: stakeholders review, comment, and approve without needing access to video editing software.

The Cons: What Synthesia Gets Wrong

1. The Avatars Still Look Synthetic in 2026

Despite improvements, Synthesia avatars trigger immediate "this is AI" recognition. The micro-expressions are limited, hand gestures are repetitive, and eye movement feels programmed. For external marketing content, YouTube videos, or anything requiring audience trust and emotional connection, this is disqualifying. A B2B consultant tested Synthesia videos against self-recorded iPhone videos in LinkedIn ads. The iPhone videos had 3x higher engagement and 2.5x better conversion rates. The polish doesn't compensate for the lack of authenticity. If your audience values human connection—and most consumer-facing audiences do—Synthesia will hurt your results.

2. Voice Customization Is Limited Compared to Dedicated Voice AI Tools

While Synthesia's text-to-speech is competent, it lacks the emotional range and naturalness of ElevenLabs or the voice cloning precision of Murf. You can't adjust pacing mid-sentence, add emotional emphasis to specific words, or create truly custom voice profiles without upgrading to Enterprise. For podcast-style content or narration requiring tonal variation, dedicated voice tools paired with video editors give better results. Synthesia's voices work for straightforward instructional content but fall flat for storytelling.

3. Expensive for Solopreneurs and Small Teams

At $89/month for 30 video minutes on the Creator plan, Synthesia costs more than Canva Pro ($15/month), OpusClip ($19/month for short-form video repurposing), and Predis.ai ($32/month for social media video creation) combined. If you're a freelance course creator or small agency, you'll hit the minute limit quickly. A 10-minute training video requires testing, revisions, and multiple renders—easily consuming 15-20 minutes of quota. The pricing makes sense for enterprises producing dozens of videos monthly, but it's prohibitive for independent creators who need video occasionally.

4. Limited Creative Control Compared to Traditional Video Editing

You can't customize camera angles, lighting, avatar positioning, or create dynamic b-roll integration like you can in Premiere Pro or Final Cut. The templates are rigid. If you want an avatar to gesture toward specific on-screen elements at precise moments, you're limited by preset animations. For creative video producers who want artistic control, Synthesia feels restrictive. It's a document generator that outputs video, not a creative video production tool.

5. Export and Integration Limitations on Lower Tiers

The Starter plan watermarks videos and limits resolution to 720p. In 2026, when 4K is standard and 1080p is the minimum for professional content, 720p looks outdated. You need the Creator plan ($89/month) for 1080p exports. Additionally, while Synthesia integrates with learning management systems (LMS), it doesn't connect natively with automation tools like Make.com or Zapier without custom API work. If your workflow involves automated content pipelines, you'll spend development time building connections.

Who Should Buy Synthesia

Corporate training teams producing multi-language instructional content: If you're an L&D manager at a company with 500+ employees across multiple countries, and you need to create compliance training, onboarding modules, or policy updates in 10+ languages quarterly, Synthesia will save you tens of thousands annually compared to traditional production.

SaaS companies creating product documentation and tutorial libraries: If you release feature updates monthly and need to update help videos, demo recordings, and customer onboarding content quickly without scheduling video shoots, Synthesia's screen recording integration and fast iteration make it valuable. The ROI shows up in reduced support ticket volume when customers watch updated tutorials.

Internal communications managers at distributed organizations: If you're sending weekly or monthly video updates from executives to remote teams, and you value message consistency over personality, Synthesia lets you script messages carefully, translate them accurately, and deliver them without scheduling C-level executives for video recording sessions.

Who Should Skip Synthesia

Content creators building personal brands on YouTube, TikTok, or Instagram: If your audience follows you for your personality, authenticity, or unique perspective, Synthesia will damage your brand. The avatars can't replicate the warmth, humor, or spontaneity that builds audience connection. Record yourself on your phone instead—even with worse lighting and audio, you'll get better engagement.

Marketing teams creating external ads or promotional content: If you're running paid ads, creating landing page videos, or producing content where conversion rates matter, Synthesia avatars will lower your performance. A/B tests consistently show real humans outperform AI avatars in trust metrics and click-through rates for consumer-facing content.

Budget-conscious freelancers or startups needing occasional video: If you create 2-3 videos per month and don't need multi-language support, you'll get better value from OpusClip for repurposing existing content, Canva for simple animated videos, or just recording yourself with decent lighting. The Synthesia pricing only makes sense at scale.

How It Compares

HeyGen: Similar avatar-based video generation with slightly more natural-looking avatars and better pricing for small teams ($29/month for more minutes). Choose HeyGen if you want avatar video but aren't ready for Synthesia's price point.

Descript: Better for creators who want to edit real video with AI assistance—transcription-based editing, filler word removal, and voice cloning of your actual voice. Choose Descript if you're recording yourself and want AI to speed up editing, not replace you on camera.

Loom: Superior for quick screen recordings with webcam overlay for internal team communication. Choose Loom if you need async video messages and screen shares without the formality or cost of Synthesia.

The Bottom Line: 7.5/10 for the Right Use Case

Synthesia earns a 7.5 because it excels in a specific lane—corporate training and internal communications at scale—but fails outside that lane. The efficiency gains are real if you're producing dozens of multi-language training videos quarterly. The ROI is clear for enterprise L&D teams. But for external content, personal branding, or creative video production, it's the wrong tool entirely.

It's worth paying for if: You're producing 20+ internal training or communication videos per year, need multi-language support, and your audience prioritizes information accuracy over emotional connection. At that volume, the time savings and production cost reduction are measurable and significant.

To become a 10/10, Synthesia would need: Avatars indistinguishable from real humans (we're not there yet technologically), more flexible pricing for small teams ($29/month for 60+ minutes), and deeper creative control over avatar movements and camera work to compete with traditional video editing tools.

If you're still unsure whether Synthesia fits your needs, start with a one-month Creator plan trial. Produce three typical videos you'd actually use. Show them to your target audience—not colleagues, but real end users. If they don't notice or care that it's AI, buy the annual plan. If they comment on the artificial look, cancel immediately and invest in a decent microphone and ring light instead. The tool isn't bad; it's just specific. Make sure you're in the specific use case where it thrives.
